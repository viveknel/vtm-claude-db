# Clanbook: Tzimisce (Revised) — Index

## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Tzimisce,
the "Fiends" — the founding clan of the Sabbat, defined by mastery of
flesh-shaping (Vicissitude), territorial obsession with their Carpathian
homeland, and a philosophy of perpetual self-transformation. The book is
narrated largely through named in-character voices (Titus Villicent, a
"Prince of Paupers" priest, various Sabbat Tzimisce) rather than a single
frame. Covers a distinctive origin myth built on metamorphosis rather
than static curse, the clan's revenant servant families, the Sabbat's
founding, and character material.

## Introduction: Winter Fiend (pp. 5-8)
**Summary:** Fiction piece set on the WWII Eastern Front (November 1942):
a German tank crewman, Dietrich, encounters something monstrous and
transformative among frozen, mutilated tank crews, ending with an
unsettling implication of Tzimisce involvement in the carnage of the
Eastern Front.
**Key themes:** War and atrocity as fertile ground for Tzimisce
predation and transformation.

## Chapter One: A Rabble of Fiends (pp. 10-38)
**Summary:**
- **Origin myth ("The Progenitor's Legacy"):** Distinctively framed
  around change rather than static curse. **Ynosh** (Enoch), Caine's
  first childe, tries to purge his own "impure" Beast-tainted essence by
  spitting it into a mortal seer/magician — this vessel becomes **the
  Eldest** (the Tzimisce Antediluvian), who emerges not feral but
  possessed of a uniquely *fluid* identity and capacity for growth,
  unlike the "stagnant" other Antediluvians. Dragons (Azhi Dahaka,
  Dracon, Illuyankas, Dracula, Leviathan) recur as the clan's mythic
  symbol of transformation.
- **Kupala:** The Eldest frees an ancient, blighting earth-spirit,
  **Kupala**, from a Lupine-built prison in the Carpathians in exchange
  for magical knowledge — the origin of **koldunic sorcery** and the
  reason Tzimisce must rest on their native soil (a Sabbat-era voice
  reframes this "gift" as actually a curse/leash).
- **Flood, Curse, and Exodus:** The Deluge, the Tzimisce's practice of
  taking child-tribute from refugee mortal tribes (origin of the
  **revenant families**), Caine's curse forcing earth-bound rest, and
  the clan's descent into isolationist, tyrannical rule from Carpathian
  keeps.
- **Historical arc:** Roman-era contact, Vlad Dracula's forced
  integration into the clan via Lambach Ruthven, war with the Tremere
  over the Carpathians, the Mongol invasion, the Anarch Revolt/Sabbat
  founding (Tzimisce as a primary founding clan), 19th-20th century
  material including the World Wars, Soviet gulag infiltration, and
  modern refugee-era Balkan conflicts. Also briefly covers African and
  Indian Tzimisce (limited, acknowledged as sparse clan knowledge).
**Key themes:** Transformation/metamorphosis as a genuine philosophical
core rather than mere body-horror flavor; territoriality and the
soil-bound curse as central to both clan identity and vulnerability;
the clan's role as the Sabbat's philosophical and institutional
backbone.
**Key terms/entities:** the Eldest, Ynosh/Enoch, Kupala, koldunic
sorcery, the revenant families, Vlad Dracula/Lambach Ruthven.

## Chapter Two: Ego and Evolution (pp. 40-75)
**Summary:** Covers clan philosophy, internal loyalty structures, and
the revenant families in detail.
- **Loyalties and blood bonds:** The clan's historic innovation of the
  blood bond as a political tool; Camarilla vs. Sabbat Tzimisce
  perspectives (including reflections attributed to the Sabbat priscus
  Sascha Vykos); relations with other clans (Ravnos dismissed briefly,
  wary/skeptical treatment of other supernatural beings).
- **Revenant families:** Detailed coverage of the Tzimisce's mortal
  servant bloodlines — the **Romanian Legacy Foundation** (a modern
  public front), **Bratovitch** (loyal to voivodes during Tzimisce
  anarch revolts), **Grimaldi**, **Zantosa** (handle worldly/kine
  matters), **Obertus** ("the hidden," monastic clergy-scholars since
  Constantinople), and the **Oprichniki** (a vassal lineage absorbed
  after historical conflict).
- **Paths of Enlightenment:** Extensive treatment of the **Path of
  Metamorphosis** (a Tzimisce-specific philosophy of continual self-
  transformation, with Storyteller guidance on running it distinctly
  from other Paths) and the **Path of Lilith**.
- **Game material:** Deep Vicissitude/Metamorphosis material, revenant
  family stat blocks and the "Revenant Weakness" Flaw, Animalism (Eye of
  the Szlachta), Auspex, combination Disciplines, and clan-specific
  derangements (e.g. Misfortune of Chernogolov).
**Key themes:** Revenant families as a structurally central (not
peripheral) part of Tzimisce society, each with distinct clan loyalties
and roles; Metamorphosis as the clan's answer to Golconda/Path
philosophy, treating change itself as the spiritual goal.
**Key terms/entities:** blood bonds (Tzimisce origin), the revenant
families (Bratovitch/Grimaldi/Zantosa/Obertus/Oprichniki), Path of
Metamorphosis, Path of Lilith, Sascha Vykos.

## Chapter Three: Among Fiends (pp. 76-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Lord of the Ghetto**, **The Siamese
Twin**, **Most Eugenic Baby, 1929**, **Child of the Dracon**, **Koldun
Nuevo**, **Kennel Master**, **Star, Fucker**, **Modern Primitive**,
**The New Prometheus**, **Oprichniki Man Friday**. Closes with a
detailed **Sample Pack** including **Zaljko** (a fire-dancing, body-
modified Fiend with thick bones granting natural Fortitude) and profiles
of named Sabbat elders, including **Myca** (a Constantinople-era figure
who moved to the New World), **Radu**, and **Sascha Vykos** (referenced
again from Ch.2, detailing her role in Sabbat sect politics and the pack
she leads, "the Jagiellonians").
**Key themes:** Range of body-modification and transformation themes
carried through into character concepts, reinforcing Metamorphosis as a
lived, not just philosophical, clan practice.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Change as the clan's central value**: from the Eldest's uniquely
  fluid nature in the origin myth, through the Path of Metamorphosis, to
  the character templates' body-modification themes, transformation is
  treated as the Tzimisce's core philosophical and narrative throughline
  rather than incidental body horror.
- **Territoriality as both strength and vulnerability**: the Kupala
  bargain and Caine's soil-bound curse tie Tzimisce power directly to
  the Carpathian homeland, explaining both the clan's historic dominance
  there and its vulnerability when displaced (refugee-era material in
  Ch.1).
- **Revenants as extended family, not mere tools**: Chapter Two treats
  the revenant families as socially and narratively central to Tzimisce
  identity, with named lineages, distinct loyalties, and internal
  politics of their own.
- **Named, contested in-character narration**: similar to several other
  clanbooks in this library, historical claims are voiced by named,
  potentially biased in-character sources rather than an omniscient
  narrator.
