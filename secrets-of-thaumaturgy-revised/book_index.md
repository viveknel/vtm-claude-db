# Blood Magic: Secrets of Thaumaturgy — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A 2000 White Wolf sourcebook (classic Revised Edition line, pre-V20) devoted
entirely to Thaumaturgy and its rival blood-sorceries. Unusually for a
Revised-line book, its narrative frame is not a single elder's memoir but a
deliberately unreliable "exposé": an anonymous anarch chronicler (Chapters
One–Two) who turns out, from marginalia left by a Tremere editor annotating
the text, to be a Brujah/Malkavian antitribu impersonating a Tremere monk —
"Father Terrell Harding, Magus Dominus... aka Ronnie Royce, Clan Brujah, aka
Brother Demetrius Vance, Malkavian antitribu." The book pairs this narrative
history and clan-culture material (Chapters One–Two) with new crunch
(Chapter Three: original paths/rituals for Tremere Thaumaturgy; Chapter
Four: Necromancy, Setite Sorcery, Assamite Sorcery and Koldunic Sorcery) and
a bestiary appendix of Thaumaturgy-spawned creatures and revenant families.
Published at the tail end of the Revised era, it is soaked in end-of-line
Gehenna-omen foreshadowing: the Assamites' blood curse breaking "overnight,
without warning," the Sabbat Tremere antitribu vanishing without a trace,
and blood magic itself visibly "faltering."

## Chapter One: The History of Blood Magic (pp. 18-29)
**Summary:** An unreliable first-person chronicle of blood magic's history,
narrated by a self-identified Tremere magus whose footnoted marginalia (from
a second, in-universe Tremere "editor") repeatedly undercuts his claims —
and whose true identity is revealed only in the byline at the chapter's end.
Traces blood magic from a legendary "Caine as first magician" theory,
through pre-Thaumaturgy magic practiced piecemeal by early Assamites,
proto-Cappadocians ("Ashurians"), and Tzimisce; a catastrophic war between
rival blood-magician factions in "the Inner Wastes" (Altai Mountains/Tarim
Basin, explaining their real-world aridity as magical fallout); the
Antediluvians' passing into torpor scattering magical knowledge across
clans; blood magic's role in Carthage (siding with the existing library's
"Carthage disagreement" theme — this narrator claims Brujah didn't practice
blood magic themselves but tolerated magician-tenants within the city); the
Tremere's transformation from mortal Hermetic mages (led by Tremere and
"the cunning Goratrix") who extracted vitae from Tzimisce vampires to
achieve undeath, at the cost of losing their original mortal magic; the
subsequent Tremere/Tzimisce war; and Thaumaturgy's codification as the
first attempt to unify the many scattered blood-sorcery traditions (though
explicitly *not* absorbing Koldunic Sorcery, Setite Sorcery, or the
Assamite viziers' magic — these remain separate).
**Key themes:** unreliable/plural narration as a structural device (the
in-text "editor" marginalia directly contradicts and mocks the narrator);
blood magic as older and more fractured than Thaumaturgy; the price of
power (mortal mages lose true magic upon Embrace).
**Key terms/entities:** Caine-as-magician theory (p.21); the Second City's
proto-blood-magic factions — Assamites, "Ashurians"/proto-Cappadocians,
Tzimisce (p.22); the Inner Wastes catastrophe/Altai-Tarim dead zone (p.23);
Tremere and Goratrix's mortal Hermetic order and the extraction of Tzimisce
vitae (pp.27-28); "the Ashrad"/"the Gallû" (unexplained mass deaths of blood
magicians during the era of folk-wandering, p.26).
**Notable claims/data points:** the Assamite blood-bond curse collapsing
"overnight, without warning" with no successful Tremere fix (p.31 — this is
a Revised-era foreshadowing thread, distinct from and earlier than any V20
account); the entire Sabbat Tremere antitribu vanishing without explanation,
with rumors of Salubri antitribu and "walking Methuselahs" (p.31); the
chronicle's true authorship revealed only in its closing byline as a
Brujah/Malkavian antitribu hoax rather than an actual Tremere account —
meaning everything "Tremere" in this chapter is itself of doubtful
authenticity, the book's own built-in unreliable-narrator twist.

## Chapter Two: The Rites of Blood (pp. 30-65)
**Summary:** Narrated by a different unreliable voice (a self-described
escaped Tremere-turned-anarch, with a separate loyalist-Tremere "editor"
adding hostile marginal notes throughout), this chapter covers Thaumaturgy's
Hermetic theory and Tremere clan culture in detail: the four Major
Principles (Blood, Will, Knowledge, Identity) and three minor principles
(Sympathy, Contagion, Inherency) that structure all thaumaturgical
formulae; True Names and their decline in modern use; the difference
between paths (innate, vitae-costly, generation-independent) and Hermetic
rituals (formulaic, time-costly, usually vitae-free); minority magical
traditions practiced by non-Hermetic Tremere — Kabbalah, Voodoo/Santeria,
Wicca, and Dark Thaumaturgy/infernalism (each with its own principle
emphasis and clan-political stigma); the chantry system, clan ranks
(apprentice through councilor, seven Circles of Mystery per rank),
certamen dueling, apprenticeship and the master/apprentice power dynamic;
and a catalog of famous blood-magic texts (the Veneficorum Artum Sanguis,
Nominis Inferni, Das Tiefe Geheimnis, the Journals of Thomas Bremond, The
Eight Disks/Path of Technomancy). Closes with "The Magic Falters" — the
same end-of-line omens as Chapter One (Assamite curse broken, Sabbat
Tremere antitribu vanished) reprised with the explicit whispered word
"Gehenna."
**Key themes:** knowledge as the currency of Tremere power and paranoia;
blood magic's cross-clan reach beyond the Tremere monopoly; unreliable
narration continued from Chapter One, now with a second author's voice and
a second hostile editorial layer.
**Key terms/entities:** the four Major Principles and three minor
principles (pp.38-44); Kabbalah, Voodoo/Santeria, Wicca, Dark Thaumaturgy
as minority traditions (pp.47-53); chantry structure, Regent/Lord/
Pontifex/Councilor ranks, Circles of Mystery, certamen (pp.53-63); named
texts including Veneficorum Artum Sanguis (Etrius), Nominis Inferni, Das
Tiefe Geheimnis (Johann Kloepfer), Journals of Thomas Bremond, The Eight
Disks (pp.63-66).
**Notable claims/data points:** a mortal cult successfully working crude
blood magic at the cost of total self-exsanguination (p.40); the Assamite
curse-breaking and vanished Sabbat antitribu reprised as the chapter's
closing omen, explicitly naming "Gehenna" (p.67) — the clearest textual
link between this book and the setting's end-times metaplot.

## Chapter Three: The Ways of Magic (pp. 68-100)
**Summary:** New player/Storyteller crunch: eleven new Thaumaturgy paths —
Alchemy (transmutation, explicitly *not* usable to create weapons of mass
destruction or bypass real-world material limits), Biothaumaturgy
(Frankenstein-style creature animation, linked to "the inscrutable Black
Hand"), the Focused Mind (a Kabbalah-derived mental-enhancement path), the
Hearth Path (haven-protection/awareness rituals), Mastery of the Mortal
Shell (body-control, originally developed to hunt Tzimisce/Gangrel and to
enforce Gargoyle servitude), Oneiromancy (Tremere dream-divination,
originally the basis for the Sabbat's Path of Morpheus, revived clan-wide
post-Gehenna-omens), Path of the Blood's Curse (a rebel path created by
Caitiff/Pander thaumaturges specifically to exploit elder vampires'
Embrace-linked weaknesses against them), Path of Curses (Maleficium/
Hexen-Kraft/Evil Eye), Path of Transmutation (a cruder, alchemy-derived
transmutation path Tremere consider "quick and dirty"), the Vine of
Dionysus (a hedonism/frenzy-inducing path practiced by self-styled
"thyrsus" cultists), and the Faux Path (a uniquely political, near-powerless
path built entirely around bluffing other Kindred into believing a
thaumaturge is more dangerous than she is). Followed by rules for learning,
researching and creating new rituals/paths, and a full Level One–Five
ritual catalog (illustrative examples include Brand of the Paramour,
Encrypt Missive, and Craft Bloodstone). This is reference/crunch material;
query the chunk database directly for specific power text.
**Key themes:** Thaumaturgy's open-endedness (new paths/rituals as an
ongoing clan project); several paths explicitly tied to political factions
within the clan (Path of the Blood's Curse as Caitiff/Pander rebellion; the
Faux Path as a commentary on Thaumaturgy's declining secrecy in the
information age).
**Key terms/entities:** Alchemy, Biothaumaturgy, Focused Mind, Hearth
Path, Mastery of the Mortal Shell, Oneiromancy, Path of the Blood's Curse,
Path of Curses, Path of Transmutation, Vine of Dionysus/"thyrsus" cultists,
Faux Path (pp.70-87); ritual research/creation rules (pp.88-90).
**Notable claims/data points:** the book explicitly warns Storytellers
against letting Alchemy or Conjuring be used to create weapons of mass
destruction (p.71) — an unusual direct authorial aside; Oneiromancy is
tied directly to the same Gehenna-anxiety established in Chapters One-Two.

## Chapter Four: Eldritch Sorceries (pp. 102-134)
**Summary:** Non-Tremere blood-sorcery traditions, explicitly framed as
*not* superseded or unified by Thaumaturgy. Opens with a history of Western
Necromancy from Classical antiquity (Apollonius of Tyana, Lucius Apuleius,
Iamblichus' theurgy) through its Christian-era reframing around the
principles of Taboo (power through violating the era's strongest social
prohibitions, especially around corpses) and Authority (power through
symbols of mastery/imprisonment/identity), and its refinement by the
Giovanni after Augustus Giovanni's diablerie of a Cappadocian Antediluvian.
The chapter's most developed narrative is the origin of **Voudoun
Necromancy**: Gisele Hemmet, a Lasombra with Giovanni ties in colonial
Haiti, seeks to become a houngan (priest) of the loa Baron Samedi expecting
a naive, gratifiable spirit — and instead finds Samedi an ancient,
mocking, vastly superior consciousness who grants Cainite houngans power
specifically *because* it amuses him to watch vampires delude themselves
about their own significance in the face of death. The chapter continues
with **Setite Sorcery** (rooted in Osiris/Set mythology and the Egyptian
Book of the Dead, with sendings built around jars representing organs/
concepts, e.g. First Taste, Thrones Crumble), **Assamite Sorcery** (the
reclusive viziers of Alamut, meditation with stones from the mountain
itself, the diablerie ritual Rite of Marduk), and **Koldunic Sorcery**
(Tzimisce land-magic organized into "Ways," including the Way of Wind and
the Way of the Dead Water, explicitly noted as reawakening "with the
destruction of the Tremere antitribu" — tying this chapter's timeline to
the same vanishing-antitribu omen from Chapters One-Two, across a
different credited author, suggesting it was a deliberate book-wide plot
thread rather than one writer's invention).
**Key themes:** blood sorcery as culturally specific and non-unifiable
(each tradition has its own cosmology and cannot simply be absorbed into
Hermetic Thaumaturgy); higher powers using vampires for their own
amusement/purposes rather than being controlled by them (Baron Samedi's
relationship to his Cainite houngans inverts the usual "vampire as master
sorcerer" framing).
**Key terms/entities:** Taboo and Authority as necromantic principles
(pp.104-105); Gisele Hemmet and Baron Samedi (pp.108-112); Setite
Sorcery's Osiris/Set cosmology (pp.115-121); Assamite Sorcery and Mount
Alamut, Rite of Marduk (pp.122-127); Koldunic Sorcery's Ways of Wind and
Dead Water (pp.128-135).
**Notable claims/data points:** the Giovanni's necromantic rituals (Chair
of Hades, Rape of Persephone, Drink of Lethe's/Styx's Waters) draw
explicitly on Greek/Roman underworld mythology rather than Christian
cosmology, consistent with the existing library's note that the eldest
Giovanni "felt a greater sympathy for the grand pagan ethos of the
Classical era than they ever did for Christian parables"; Koldunic
Sorcery's reawakening is explicitly dated to "the destruction of the
Tremere antitribu" — the same event referenced in Chapters One and Two.

## Appendix: The Faces of Magic (pp. 135-142)
**Summary:** A bestiary of Thaumaturgy-created creatures and two revenant
families. Creatures: Blood Brothers (Koldunic-Sorcery-plus-Thaumaturgy
group-Embraced vampires sharing a psychic hive-mind, limb-sharing, and the
ability to merge into a "coagulated monstrosity" — Sabbat-associated),
Corpse Minion, Demon-Bound, Gargoyle (game stats and creation-ritual
cross-reference only; full lore left to other books), Homunculus, Razor
Bat and Stone Hound (both Tremere-crafted animal-Gargoyle hybrids used as
spies/guards). Two previously undocumented revenant bloodlines round out
the appendix: the **Ducheski** (a dwindling revenant family that
abandoned its original Tzimisce masters generations ago to serve the
Tremere instead as mechanical-genius artificers; heavy inbreeding caps
their Social Traits at 2) and the **Rafastio** (hedge-magic witches once
patronized by the now-annihilated Black Hand, now masterless and hunted;
moon-cursed — Disciplines are harder to use on a new moon and easier on a
full moon — and their blood actively rejects the Embrace, killing anyone
who tries).
**Key themes:** Thaumaturgy's creations as a form of Tremere infrastructure
(spies, guards, disposable soldiers) rather than dramatic set-piece
monsters; revenant families as a recurring device for showing blood
sorcery's reach into non-vampire servant lineages.
**Key terms/entities:** Blood Brothers, Corpse Minion, Demon-Bound,
Homunculus, Razor Bat, Stone Hound, Ducheski, Rafastio.
**Notable claims/data points:** Ducheski and Rafastio do not appear
(under these names) in any other book currently in this library — they are
this book's unique contribution to the setting's revenant-family roster.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Unreliable/plural narration as the book's structuring device**: every
  chapter of this book is narrated by an unreliable in-character voice
  (anarch impersonator, escaped Tremere, etc.), several with a second
  layer of hostile in-universe editorial marginalia contradicting the
  narrator in real time. This is the same authorial technique the existing
  library's `v20-lore-clans`, `v20-lore-bloodlines`, and `v20-dark-ages`
  use, but this book pushes it further by making the narrator's own
  *identity* a twist withheld until the closing byline of Chapters One and
  Two — the reader only learns after finishing Chapter One that its
  "Tremere" narrator is actually a Brujah/Malkavian antitribu hoax.
- **Gehenna omens as a book-wide thread across multiple authors**: the
  Assamite blood-curse breaking and the Sabbat Tremere antitribu vanishing
  without explanation are raised in Chapter One, reprised explicitly with
  the word "Gehenna" at the end of Chapter Two, and referenced again in
  Chapter Four's Koldunic Sorcery section by a different credited author —
  suggesting this was a coordinated line-wide plot thread for the book
  rather than one writer's aside.
- **Thaumaturgy explicitly does NOT unify all blood magic**: Chapter One
  states this directly, and Chapter Four's separate treatment of
  Necromancy, Setite Sorcery, Assamite Sorcery, and Koldunic Sorcery
  enacts it structurally — each tradition keeps its own cosmology,
  principles, and practitioners rather than being folded into Hermetic
  Thaumaturgy.
