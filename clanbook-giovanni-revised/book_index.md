## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Giovanni,
the "Necromancers" — a closed, nepotistic Italian merchant-family clan
built on necromancy and blood-family exclusivity rather than open
recruitment. Distinctively structured as a family business as much as a
vampire clan: most members are Embraced only after long ghoul service,
and outsiders are rarely admitted. Chapter One is voiced in a crude,
cynical, first-person modern narrator's voice. Covers the clan's
founding via the violent overthrow of the Cappadocian clan, family
branch politics, the Wraith-crossover "Maelstrom" crisis, and character
material.

## Introduction: Kissing Cousins (pp. 5-12)
**Summary:** Fiction piece: a young ghoul, Primo, awaits possible
Embrace amid tense family politics (Accorri, Isabel, Eric, Diego), with
the family's elders debating whether he's being rushed into it too
young — illustrating the clan's characteristic blend of genuine familial
intimacy and cold calculation about bloodline value.
**Key themes:** The Giovanni family as literally a family, with Embrace
treated as inheritance/succession politics.

## Chapter One: A Familiar Conceit (pp. 14-34)
**Summary:** Narrated in a blunt, profane modern voice explaining clan
history to a new listener.
- **Roman origins and the Jovians:** The family's precursors as "Jovian"
  privateers/refugees fleeing the fall of Carthage, later reinventing
  themselves in an Italian (rather than Roman) context; a sidebar
  identifies **Dis Pater** (the Roman underworld god) as the true, largely
  unknown spiritual legacy behind the family's necromantic bent.
- **The Cappadocian betrayal and coup:** The clan's founding trauma and
  triumph — the Cappadocians (an older necromancer clan) abandoned the
  Jovian ancestors' pleas for aid when most needed; this betrayal became
  the pretext for the Giovanni's violent overthrow/absorption of the
  Cappadocian clan (helped along by two disloyal Cappadocian toadies,
  Japheth and Constancia, who argued against admitting the Giovanni in
  the first place — an irony the clan still savors).
- **The Premascines:** A sidebar on **Pochtli**, an unsettlingly
  Cappadocian-esque figure linked to zombie-raising and a portal built
  into the Giovanni's own haven, hinting at a deeper, still-unresolved
  supernatural entanglement predating the clan's known history.
- **Historical arc:** Renaissance-era patronage rivalry with the
  Toreador and entanglement with the early Tremere; the clan's spread
  through banking, trade, and organized crime into the modern era.
- **Family branches:** Introduces several named branches with distinct
  reputations — the **Capuchin** branch (mysterious, led by "the
  Capuchin"), the **Rosselini** (prone to committing atrocities far
  beyond necessity), the **della Passaglia** (controversial modern
  necromancy research, disturbingly including study conducted during
  the Khmer Rouge's killing fields in Cambodia), and the **Dunsirn**
  (a northern European branch with icy relations with the main family).
**Key themes:** Betrayal and ruthless opportunism as the clan's founding
and ongoing modus operandi; family branch rivalry as a structural
element of clan life; casual, unresolved horror (the della Passaglia's
Khmer Rouge research, the Premascine portal) treated matter-of-factly by
the narrator.
**Key terms/entities:** Dis Pater, the Cappadocian coup, Japheth and
Constancia, Pochtli/the Premascines, the Capuchin/Rosselini/della
Passaglia/Dunsirn branches.

## Chapter Two: It Runs in the Family (pp. 36-80)
**Summary:**
- **Family structure and the Embrace:** The **Proxy Kiss** tradition
  (ghouls understand it's often better to receive the actual Embrace
  from an elder rather than their immediate domitor) and the clan's
  strong preference for testing loyalty through long ghoul service before
  Embrace; internal politics around blood bonds and generational status.
- **The Maelstrom:** A major present-day crisis crossing over with the
  *Wraith: The Oblivion* setting — a "spirit storm" that unleashed vast
  numbers of ghosts (**spiriti**) into the world, some of which anchor
  to physical locations or objects, giving Giovanni necromancers both new
  power and new danger; **Anziani Appasionati** ("Eager Elders") view
  this as validating the clan's apocalyptic ambitions ("Ending the world
  is our job").
- **Spettri:** Ghosts consumed and remade by hostile "ghostly archfiends"
  into spirit slaves, resistant to standard Necromancy and a significant
  new threat category.
- **Family branches in more detail:** The **Pisanob** (a Mexican branch
  thrown into disarray by regional supernatural chaos), the **Dunsirn**
  (northern European, prideful about "pure" Giovanni blood, tension with
  those Embraced rather than family-born), the **Ghiberti** (co-developed
  a necromantic ritual referenced in the text), and Russian mafiya
  contact/rivalry.
- **Tithing:** The clan's system of using expendable childer or ghouls to
  satisfy dangerous or unwanted obligations.
- **Relations with other clans:** Sharp, dismissive in-character
  commentary (voiced by "Accorri") — grudging respect for Toreador as
  the clan's strongest Camarilla allies, contempt for Gangrel and
  Malkavians (open wish for Malkavian "extinguishing"), wary tactical
  notes on Nosferatu, Setites, Ventrue, Brujah, and Tremere.
- **Game material:** Extensive **Necromancy** ritual and Path material
  (including the Sepulchre Path, Bone Path effectiveness against
  Spettri, the Ritual of the Smoking Mirror, Ritual of Xipe Totec, Hand
  of Glory), new Backgrounds (Spirit Slave), and clan-specific Merits/
  Flaws.
**Key themes:** The Maelstrom as a genuine present-tense crisis/
opportunity reshaping clan power dynamics; family-branch rivalry and
"purity of blood" snobbery as an ongoing internal tension; casual
contempt for most other clans as characteristic of the family's
insularity.
**Key terms/entities:** the Maelstrom, spiriti/Spettri, Proxy Kiss,
Tithing, the Pisanob/Ghiberti branches.

## Chapter Three: Shareholders and Charnel Houses (pp. 82-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Single-Blood Prodigy**, **Hurried
Embrace**, **Bitter Matron**, **Diplomat Provocateur**, **Dunsirn
Bully**, **Aide-De-Camp**, **Pisanob Grave Robber**, **The Degenerate**,
**Fiduciary Wizard**, **Failure**. Closes with detailed elder profiles,
including **Paolo** (a broker who earned his Embrace through decades of
extraordinary mortal service between 1866-1914, later staked and later
revived), **Emilio** (thrives on cycles of attraction/repulsion), and
**Claudia** (deliberately kept as a ghoul rather than Embraced by her
domitor Guillaume Giovanni, for strategic reasons).
**Key themes:** Range from sympathetic family strivers to genuinely
monstrous figures (Dunsirn Bully, The Degenerate), reflecting the
family-business tensions established in Chapters One and Two.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Family as institution**: unlike clans organized around shared
  philosophy or open recruitment, Giovanni identity is explicitly
  nepotistic and business-like — Embrace as inheritance, branches as
  competing corporate divisions, ghoul service as a probationary period.
- **Betrayal begets betrayal**: the founding Cappadocian betrayal (Ch.1)
  sets a tone of ruthless opportunism that recurs in the clan's internal
  branch rivalries and cold, transactional worldview (Ch.2's "Accorri"
  commentary on other clans).
- **Necromancy as escalating, dangerous power**: the Maelstrom crisis
  treats the clan's signature Discipline not as a static toolkit but as
  something actively growing more dangerous and consequential in the
  present-day setting.
