# Wind from the East — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A crossover sourcebook bridging **Vampire: The Dark Ages** and **Kindred
of the East** (with material also drawing on the *Blood & Silk*
historical supplement), covering the rise and fall of the Mongol Empire
(c. 1167-1368) and its impact on both Cainite (Western vampire) and Wan
Kuei ("Cathayan"/Eastern vampire) society. Introduces the **Anda**, a
long-lost Gangrel-descended bloodline native to the Mongol steppe, and
details how the Empire's explosive rise and rapid collapse brought
Western and Eastern vampire societies into direct, unprecedented
contact. The book's stated theme is "the bright candle" — power that
burns twice as hot lasts half as long, mirrored in the Anda's own rise
and near-total extermination alongside the Mongol Yuan Dynasty's fall.

**Cross-reference note:** this book is also relevant to a separate
*Kindred of the East* library (built independently of this V20/Dark
Ages library) since roughly half its content — the Wan Kuei material in
Chapter Two, the Five August Courts, the Wu Zao, and related content —
is Kindred of the East-side lore. This index (and the chunk database)
covers the book in full, so it can be queried usefully from either
library context.

## Front Matter / How to Use This Book (pp. 1-16)
**Summary:** A short fiction vignette (an Anda vampire's internal
monologue on the Volga steppe) followed by publisher's guidance:
suggested timeframe 1167-1368 (Temujin's birth through the Yuan
Dynasty's fall), usable for Dark Ages, Blood & Silk, or mixed
Cainite/Wan Kuei chronicles, plus theme ("the bright candle") and mood
(energetic, morally unsentimental) guidance and a chapter-by-chapter
overview.
**Key themes:** Explicit framing of the book as a bridge product between
two game lines, with the Anda bloodline's rise-and-fall as the
thematic anchor for both.

## Chapter One: Empire of the World-Conqueror (pp. 18-47)
**Summary:** A detailed, mostly real-world-historical account of the
Mongol Empire's rise and fall, interwoven with vampire-specific material.
- **Steppe culture and Mongol history:** Nomadic tribal/clan (*ulus*)
  structure, the *ger* (yurt), steppe ponies, shamanism (Tengri/Itügen
  religion), the rise of Temujin/Genghis Khan, the **Great Yasa** (the
  Mongols' formalized law code), the Chinese conquests (against the Jin
  and Xixia), the invasion of Russia and Eastern Europe (the Golden
  Horde under Batu Khan and Subetai), the death of Ogadei and subsequent
  succession crises, Kublai Khan's founding of the Yuan Dynasty and
  construction of Daidu, and the Mongols' failed naval invasions of
  Japan and Java.
- **Vampires within the Empire:** The **Anda** bloodline riding with the
  horde (using the Mongols' own herds/camps as their feeding base);
  Cappadocian intelligence-gathering via their necromantic ties to the
  dead; Malkavian rumor-spreading; reluctant Tzimisce sires in occupied
  Russia (e.g. **Raiya Ironhammer**); Ventrue/other Camarilla-precursor
  clans' political responses to the invasion; the Assassins/Alamut
  connection during the Mongol conquest of Persia; and a dedicated
  section on **the Anda and the Wan Kuei** as the two vampire societies
  first come into sustained contact via the Empire's westward and
  eastward expansion.
- **Timeline sidebar:** A full chronological chart of the Mongol era for
  Storyteller reference.
**Key themes:** Real historical detail treated as load-bearing setting
material, not mere backdrop; vampire factions reacting opportunistically
(or fearfully) to a genuinely world-altering mortal historical event.
**Key terms/entities:** Temujin/Genghis Khan, the Great Yasa, Batu Khan,
Subetai, Kublai Khan, the Anda, Raiya Ironhammer.

## Chapter Two: Monsters on the Steppe (pp. 48-67)
**Summary:**
- **Cainite clan reactions:** Individual clan responses to the Mongol
  phenomenon — Brujah Zealots debating its significance, Malkavian
  activity, reluctant Tzimisce Embraces of Mongols, and a Followers of
  Set claim of foreknowledge/positioning advantage.
- **The Anda in detail:** Full write-up of the bloodline as "long-lost
  cousins to the Gangrel," their relationship to Mongol tribal life, and
  a mechanical note on diminishing returns for repeated actions in the
  same location.
- **Minor bloodlines:** Brief coverage of enslaved Gargoyles encountered
  in the region.
- **Wan Kuei material:** The **Wu Zao** (a Wan Kuei group descended from
  a barbarian figure, Zao-lat, largely uninterested in rejoining western
  Wan Kuei society), the **Black Tortoise Court**'s outsized role in
  producing certain Wan Kuei types given its relative freedom/privacy,
  the **Yama King Dokhor** and "the Hell of Spiked Chains," the
  **Black Hoof Riders wu** (a north-oriented corpse-family group
  opposing the "Barren Wind"), a sidebar on **Genghis Khan's afterlife**
  fate as it pertains to Wan Kuei cosmology, and a section on **Wan Kuei
  in the West** as Mongol expansion draws Eastern and Western vampire
  societies into unprecedented contact.
**Key themes:** Structural parallelism between Cainite and Wan Kuei
material — both "sides" of the setting get comparable narrative
treatment, reflecting the book's bridging purpose.
**Key terms/entities:** the Anda, Wu Zao, Black Tortoise Court, Yama
King Dokhor, Black Hoof Riders.

## Chapter Three: Gifts of the Sky and Earth (pp. 68-87)
**Summary:** Game material for running Mongol-era chronicles.
- **Character creation:** Guidance for Anda and other Mongol vampire
  characters, including modified Via Bestiae rules, Discipline
  adaptations, and Backgrounds guidance (e.g. Anda rarely take
  Retainers, reflecting their self-reliant culture).
- **Rites and Paths:** The **Road of the Yasa** ("Via Yasaq"), a
  Mongol-specific Path of Enlightenment tied to the Great Yasa's legal/
  moral code, and rites such as **Commune with Tengri**.
- **Practical steppe-life rules:** Travel speed and long-distance
  movement mechanics, feeding on animal blood versus human blood on the
  open steppe, guidance on sacking a city from a vampire's-eye view, and
  advice on Mongol coteries and mixed Cainite/Wan Kuei ("wu") groups.
- **Storyteller guidance:** Structuring an "Eastern chronicle" and
  general advice for running games that span both vampire societies.
**Key themes:** Practical, systemically grounded support for the
nomadic, horse-based, non-urban vampire lifestyle the Anda represent —
a significant departure from the city-bound assumptions of most Vampire
sourcebooks.

## Appendix: Kahns of the Night (pp. 88-98)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Steppe Warrior**, **Temple Thief**,
**Unliving Scout**, **Wise Woman**, **Yam Courier** (a courier for the
Mongol Empire's famous relay postal system), and **Favored of the...**
(a named elder/patron figure). Includes a detailed profile of **Aajav**,
an Anda who distinguished himself during the Russian campaign and drew
the attention of a wandering Gangrel elder named **Oderic**, who
believed (rightly or wrongly) that he was quietly directing Mongol
armies against Assamite strongholds in the east, paralleling rumored
Ventrue/Lasombra direction of the European Crusades. Also covers the
historical mass sacrifice/burial of servants at the Great Khan's burial
site. Closes with back-cover promotional copy.
**Key themes:** The Aajav/Oderic material exemplifies the book's
recurring suggestion that major mortal historical events (the Crusades,
the Mongol conquests) may be secretly vampire-directed — a classic
Jyhad-flavored conspiracy thread tying this book's history back to the
core game's themes.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **The bright candle**: the book's explicit central theme — intense,
  rapid rise followed by near-total collapse — applies equally to the
  Mongol Empire itself and to the Anda bloodline's fate, giving the
  historical and vampire-specific material a unified emotional arc.
- **East meets West**: structurally, the book treats Cainite and Wan
  Kuei material as equally important, using the Mongol Empire's real
  geographic reach as the natural vehicle for bringing the two game
  lines' cosmologies into contact.
- **History as vampire-directed conspiracy**: recurring suggestions
  (the Aajav/Oderic material, clan-specific reactions in Ch.2) that
  major Mongol-era events were secretly influenced or exploited by
  vampires of various clans, consistent with the core Jyhad theme found
  throughout the Vampire line.
