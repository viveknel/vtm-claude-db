# Clanbook: Ravnos (Revised) — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Ravnos,
the "Wanderers" — a clan closely tied to the real-world Roma (Rroma)
diaspora, stereotyped as thieves and tricksters, and unique among these
clanbooks for depicting a major, ongoing present-day catastrophe: its
own Antediluvian's death. Chapter One directly narrates the "Week of
Nightmares" event referenced elsewhere in this library (in Clanbook:
Malkavian and Clanbook: Gangrel) from the clan that actually experienced
its epicenter. Covers a Hindu-inflected origin myth, the clan's caste
(jati) social structure, the shattering present-day crisis, and
character material.

## Introduction: The Devil's Due (pp. 5-14)
**Summary:** Fiction piece: Gwen, a university student, is drawn by the
Ravnos Johann into a life of theft and eventually the Embrace, escaping
mortal authority (a Dean named Wellesley, "Queen Anne") for the freedom
and danger of unlife.
**Key themes:** Ravnos recruitment via charm, illicit thrill, and
transgression of respectable mortal institutions.

## Chapter One: Agreed Upon Lies (pp. 16-38)
**Summary:** Sourced partly from an in-clan text called the
**Karavalanisha Vrana** and partly from a present-day first-person
narrator caught up in the clan's ongoing crisis.
- **Origin myth:** The Ravnos Antediluvian, **Zapathasura** (also called
  **Ravana** or **Dracian** in different traditions — explicitly
  echoing the Hindu epic villain Ravana), Embraced with aid from fallen
  angels called **asuratizayya**, who could not learn the Embrace's
  secret themselves but taught Zapathasura's line related knowledge.
  His childe **Chandraputra the General** led Ravnos armies against
  Enoch/the First City in a near-apocalyptic clash (a sidebar candidly
  discusses uncertainty over what/where "Enoch" even was in this
  telling), and the Antediluvian later entered prolonged torpor. A
  separate, untitled text called **the Gangrel Heresy** claims a
  historical relationship in which Zapathasura counseled the Gangrel
  clan on their "svadharma" (true purpose).
- **Diaspora and history:** Post-clash Ravnos scattering; Roman-era
  decadence; the clan's fraught relationship with the founding of the
  Camarilla (Ravnos and Setites were unpopular among the "founding
  clans"); the Inquisition; New World slaving operations (a source of
  real in-text moral discomfort/no sympathy from the narrator);
  Sabbat recruitment drawing disproportionately from non-Rroma Ravnos
  branches; WWII-era material; the 1960s counterculture.
- **The Week of Nightmares (the book's central event):** A first-person
  account of the Ravnos Antediluvian's death — described as a
  clan-wide, multi-night psychic catastrophe of uncontrollable rage and
  hunger, traced to a battlefield in Bangladesh where Zapathasura fought
  a final battle against what the evidence suggests were nuclear
  weapons (or comparably apocalyptic force). The narrator and companions
  physically visit the site afterward, finding it deliberately, though
  incompletely, covered up. Nearly all the eldest Ravnos (especially in
  India) were destroyed in the event, explicitly framed as "the first
  battlefield of Gehenna." A closing "Prophecy" section raises doubt
  about whether all the eldest truly died, citing persistent rumors
  about the legendary elder **Durga Syn** and a mysterious source who
  mailed the narrator's group 600-800-year-old historical research
  shortly before their trip to India.
**Key themes:** This chapter directly cross-references and grounds an
event (the Week of Nightmares) that other clanbooks in this library
(Malkavian, Gangrel) treat as a distant, half-understood disturbance —
here it is the clan's own defining catastrophe, narrated close to the
source; deep uncertainty about whether the crisis is truly over.
**Key terms/entities:** Zapathasura/Ravana/Dracian, the asuratizayya,
Chandraputra the General, the Karavalanisha Vrana, the Gangrel Heresy,
the Week of Nightmares, Durga Syn.

## Chapter Two: On the Art of Being Ravnos (pp. 40-70)
**Summary:**
- **The Embrace and numbers:** Ravnos Embrace practices and reasons;
  the catastrophic post-Week-of-Nightmares population crisis, especially
  in Asia, where nearly all elders were destroyed and surviving Ravnos
  must maintain Masquerade with drastically reduced numbers and
  leadership.
- **Jati (caste) structure:** A Hindu-caste-influenced social
  organization, including the **Sudras** (not a "true" jati — those
  without formal caste standing) and other named castes governing status
  and role within the clan.
- **"Born Criminals" and Ravnos antitribu:** Discussion (and pushback
  against) the stereotype that Rroma-descended Ravnos are compelled
  toward crime; sharp criticism of Ravnos antitribu as self-righteous
  "born-agains" of criminality.
- **Relations with other clans:** Brief, often personal-voice notes on
  Toreador (contrasted against Ventrue as behavioral opposites), Gangrel
  (old resentment/rivalry), and general commentary on being blamed for
  others' cleverness.
- **Paths of Enlightenment:** Clarifies Ravnos are not limited to the
  clan-associated **Path of Paradox** (difficult to follow in practice)
  — the **Shilmulo** philosophy is discussed as an alternative
  moral framework for adopting an unlife-suited code distinct from
  mortal morality.
- **Game material:** Extensive **Chimerstry** Discipline material
  (illusion powers including Shared Nightmare, Eastern Mask, and a
  sidebar clarifying what Chimerstry actually represents/how "real" its
  illusions are), a new derangement (Dissociative Perceptions Syndrome),
  and practical Lupine-avoidance advice.
**Key themes:** A clan visibly reorganizing under crisis (population
loss, leadership vacuum) rather than operating at a stable status quo;
pushback against reductive "criminal Gypsy" stereotyping from within the
clan's own voice.
**Key terms/entities:** jati/Sudras, Path of Paradox, Shilmulo,
Chimerstry.

## Chapter Three: Orphans in Gehenna's Wake (pp. 72-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Cleaner**, **Courier**, **Devil
Slayer**, **Gun Runner**, **Immigrant Smuggler**, **Inside Trader**,
**Prince's Advisor**, **Psychological Warfare Specialist**, **Sabbat
Heretic**, **Thuggee**. Closes with a **Sample Coterie** (including
Gwendolyn and Johann from the introduction fiction, now archaeologists/
art dealers entangled with an elder named Jayakumar and a Dr. Mayhew who
"learned too much") and profiles of notable Ravnos, including
**Anjalika** and further material on the vanished elder **Durga Syn**,
last confirmed active in Russia investigating the Week of Nightmares'
cause before disappearing.
**Key themes:** "Orphans" in the title reflects the chapter's characters
largely operating without elder guidance in the crisis's aftermath,
echoing Chapter One and Two's themes of a clan in genuine disarray.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **A clan in active crisis, not stable status quo**: unlike most other
  clanbooks in this library, Ravnos depicts an ongoing, unresolved
  catastrophe (the Antediluvian's death and its fallout) as the present-
  day baseline, not distant history — directly connecting to and
  grounding references in Clanbook: Malkavian and Clanbook: Gangrel.
- **Stereotype vs. lived reality**: the book repeatedly pushes back
  against the "thieving Gypsy" stereotype other clans (and even some
  in-clan voices) apply to Ravnos, while not denying the real cultural
  and historical Rroma connection.
- **Uncertainty about survival**: the persistent, unresolved question of
  whether truly all the elder Ravnos died in the Week of Nightmares
  (the Durga Syn rumors, the mysterious mailed research) keeps the
  clan's core crisis open-ended rather than resolved.

## Glossary / key terms index

- Chandraputra the General — Zapathasura's childe, led war on Enoch (p. 20-22)
- Durga Syn — legendary elder, vanished; rumored still alive (pp. 37-38, 100-101)
- Jati — the clan's caste-influenced social structure (p. 44)
- Karavalanisha Vrana — named in-clan historical text (p. 17)
- Path of Paradox — the clan's associated (but not exclusive) Path (p. 59)
- Week of Nightmares — the Ravnos Antediluvian's death, this book's
  central event (pp. 36-38)
- Zapathasura / Ravana / Dracian — the Ravnos Antediluvian (pp. 19-20, 36)
