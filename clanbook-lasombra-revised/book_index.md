## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Lasombra,
the "Keepers of Tradition" — the Sabbat's aristocratic, shadow-wielding
leadership clan. Distinctively, Chapter One is framed as a skeptical
elder's philosophy lecture to neonates, deliberately deconstructing
Noddist mythology (including the Caine/Abel story itself) rather than
retelling it uncritically — fitting a clan whose defining trait is
intellectual, aristocratic self-possession. Covers a demythologized
origin discussion, the clan's historical arc through the Sabbat's
founding, internal factions and justice systems, and character material.

## Introduction: The Darkness Claims You (pp. 5-10)
**Summary:** Fiction piece: a paralyzed accident victim is confronted
and manipulated by an unseen, taunting Lasombra voice while trapped in
an overturned van, blending psychological horror with the clan's
signature darkness-based predation.
**Key themes:** Suffering and helplessness as an entry point for
Lasombra manipulation; darkness as both literal and psychological
weapon.

## Chapter One: An Unmirrored Corridor (pp. 12-40)
**Summary:** Framed as a series of "Lessons" delivered by a skeptical
elder instructor to Lasombra neonates.
- **On origins:** Rather than retelling the Caine/Abel myth as fact, the
  narrator explicitly deconstructs it — noting linguistic anachronisms
  ("Caine" vs. period-accurate spellings), reframing Caine and Abel as
  symbols of Paleolithic vs. Neolithic human societies, and speculating
  that "Cainites" may have existed as predators before recorded human
  history in forms unrelated to the standard myth. Also questions the
  very concept of "Antediluvian" as presumptive/unproven mythology rather
  than fact, and raises the possibility (via widespread goddess-worship
  imagery) that Caine's earliest victims/childer were female.
- **Antiquity through Rome:** The (probably real) **Lasombra
  Antediluvian**, the eruption of Thera and its effects on the clan's
  maritime "First Dispersal," and Roman-era intrigue.
- **Medieval Italy and the Sabbat's founding:** The elder **Gratiano**'s
  bid for direct political rule over Italian principalities and his
  eventual downfall; the founding-era chaos that led to the Sabbat's
  Vaulderie-based unity project (described with the narrator's
  characteristic skepticism about how well it actually worked); the
  **Amies Noir** as supporters of philosophical/Noddist codification.
- **Modern era and global reach:** 19th-20th century material, a note
  that the clan does not develop separate bloodlines outside Europe
  ("One Clan, One Blood"), brief coverage of African Lasombra
  (acknowledged as a minor footnote, referencing Laibon "Abombwe" as a
  parallel gift-name), and Chinese/Asian contact (the Taiping Rebellion
  era).
**Key themes:** Deliberate, in-character skepticism toward the setting's
own foundational mythology — a structural choice reflecting Lasombra's
self-image as clear-eyed intellectual aristocrats unimpressed by
comforting stories; power and ambition (Gratiano) as a recurring
cautionary pattern.
**Key terms/entities:** Gratiano, the Amies Noir, Thera's eruption, "One
Clan, One Blood."

## Chapter Two: The Keepers' Unlit Halls (pp. 42-75)
**Summary:** Covers internal Lasombra culture, justice, and philosophy.
- **The Embrace:** Multiple thematic emphases Lasombra sires use when
  Embracing childer; contrast between Camarilla childer's longer
  survival in "gilded cages" versus Sabbat Lasombra's harsher path.
- **Factions:** The dominant **reactionary** faction (including a
  hardline subfaction, **"the Distinguished,"** who prosecute clan
  members with real severity) and the record of at least three
  experimental **mass Embrace** efforts styled after Sabbat "creation
  rites."
- **The Courts of Blood:** The clan's internal justice system, including
  the rule that a Cainite who was one of a defendant's judges almost
  always faces destruction if later found guilty of the same crime
  themselves.
- **The Friends of the Night:** A secretive Lasombra-linked spy/
  information network, "secret" in the same limited sense as mortal
  secret police.
- **Relations with other clans:** The **Tzimisce** as closest allies and
  most bitter rivals simultaneously; dismissive/hostile notes on
  Gangrel, Toreador ("poetry!"), Followers of Set, and a stated hatred of
  the Ravnos; brief mention of occasional Lupine deal-making viewed with
  suspicion.
- **The Path of Night:** The clan's claimed signature Path of
  Enlightenment, presented alongside guidance on Path mechanics
  generally ("Shedding the Inner Self").
- **Game material:** Extensive **Obtenebration** Discipline coverage
  (including light-based counter-effects), Dominate notes, and a
  Flaw ("Death's Reflection").
**Key themes:** Institutional self-policing (the Courts of Blood, the
Distinguished) as a check on the clan's aristocratic ambition; genuine
factional complexity beneath the Sabbat's monolithic reputation.
**Key terms/entities:** the Courts of Blood, the Distinguished, Friends
of the Night, Path of Night, Obtenebration.

## Chapter Three: New Shadows (pp. 76-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **The Accuser**, **The Angry Young
Man**, **The Baleful Avenger**, **The Successful Mass-Embracee**, **The
Pirate**, **The Revolutionary**, **Broker for the Damned**, **The
Crusader Out of Time**, **The Harpy**, **The Student of the Abyss**.
Closes with a detailed **Sample Pack/Brood** (including Geoffrey, a
member who underwent real-world 1984 chemotherapy for leukemia before
his Embrace) and profiles of named elders, including **Lucita** (the
clan's most famous independent member, noted for meeting with promising
neonates and sending out childer/grandchilder), **Zarathustra** (a
fifth-generation elder with a documented lineage down to the 14th
generation), and other Sabbat-affiliated figures tied to Vinculum bonds
and packs.
**Key themes:** Range from institutional enforcers (Accuser, Harpy) to
independent operators (Lucita), echoing the tension in Chapters One and
Two between aristocratic hierarchy and individual ambition.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Intellectual skepticism as clan identity**: uniquely among the
  clanbooks in this library, Lasombra's own foundational myth is
  presented as something the clan's elders actively deconstruct and
  doubt, rather than merely contested legend — reinforcing the clan's
  self-image as clear-eyed aristocrats rather than true believers.
- **Ambition checked by institution**: Gratiano's downfall (Ch.1), the
  Courts of Blood, and the Distinguished (Ch.2) together establish a
  recurring pattern of individual Lasombra ambition being reined in by
  the clan's own justice systems.
- **Darkness as psychological, not just supernatural, weapon**: from the
  introduction's manipulation of a trapped victim through Obtenebration's
  mechanical coverage in Ch.2, darkness functions as much as a tool of
  fear and control as a combat Discipline.
