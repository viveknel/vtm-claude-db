# The Hunters Hunted II — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
`book_chunks.db` (see this bundle's `README.md` for query examples)
rather than relying on the summaries below.

**A note on page numbers:** all page numbers in this file are PDF page
numbers (page 1 = the front cover), matching the `page` column in
`book_chunks.db` and what `--page N` will retrieve. The book's own
printed page numbers (visible in each page's footer) run one lower —
e.g. this file's "p. 16" is footer-numbered "15." Use the PDF numbers
given here when querying the database.

## Overview
*The Hunters Hunted II* (White Wolf/Onyx Path, 2013) is the V20-line
sourcebook for playing and running mortal vampire hunters in *Vampire:
The Masquerade*. It updates the classic *Hunters Hunted* line for V20's
modern-nights rules, giving full character creation, investigation and
combat tactics, a "Numina" supernatural-powers system for mortals, deep
Storyteller advice for running hunter-focused chronicles, and detailed
write-ups of the real-world organizations — religious, governmental,
academic, and criminal — that hunt (or unknowingly enable) the Kindred.
It is the modern-nights counterpart to this library's `dark-ages-
inquisitor` bundle, which covers the same "mortals who fight vampires"
premise for the classic Dark Ages line — see "Cross-cutting themes"
below for a direct character link between the two books.

## Here Goes Everything (pp. 6-10)
**Summary:** Framing fiction, not rules content. An unnamed
newspaperman-turned-amateur-hunter writes a letter/testament to a
stranger he shared a coffee shop with, recounting how he stumbled onto
a nest of vampires while researching a book, gathered video evidence
over months, and — unable to convince anyone else, including the
police — resolves to raid the nest alone at dawn while the vampires are
torpid. The letter ends mid-resolve, "Time to make my stand. Here goes
everything," leaving his fate unstated.
**Key themes:** the amateur, isolated origin of a hunter; evidence
left behind as testament for a stranger — the same device recurs to
close Chapter Six's Epilogue (p. 158-160), bookending the whole volume.
**Key terms/entities:** the unnamed narrator; no named vampires or
organizations (the nest is described only by location and behavior).

## Introduction (pp. 12-14)
**Summary:** States the book's premise — even Kindred have predators —
and previews its two headline themes, **Personal Horror** and
**Victory at a Cost** (both developed fully in Chapter Five). Frames
hunter stories as a shift from the default V20 Kindred's-eye view to
the mortal victims'/opponents' perspective, and notes the moral
complexity of a hunter who destroys a vampire that was, by Kindred
standards, relatively humane. Closes with a one-paragraph roadmap of
Chapters One through Six and the Appendix.
**Key themes:** Personal Horror; Victory at a Cost; the hunter's
"double-edged" knowledge (knowing the truth both empowers and damns
you to isolation).
**Key terms/entities:** none beyond the two named themes.

## Chapter One: Alone in the Night (pp. 16-30)
**Summary:** The entire chapter is a single piece of framing fiction —
an experienced hunter's monologue to a hunter he has bound to a chair,
about to induct (or execute) him — used to teach the chapter's actual
content in-voice rather than as dry text. Covers, through the
narrator's rant: **why people hunt** (motive categories: Revenge,
Righteousness, Duty, Power, Curiosity, Thrills, Insanity); **who
becomes a hunter** (six looser character-type categories the narrator
calls "the Desperate," "the Driven," "the Discredited," "the
Disenfranchised," "the Different," and "the Deranged," each with a
short list of examples); solo survival tactics (exploiting fire,
sunlight, and the near-impossibility of a clean stake-through-the-heart
kill; using crowds and a vampire's own hubris against it; a full
sidebar, "The Cops," on staying ahead of law enforcement); and building
a hunting cell (how to find other hunters, a checklist of "Orders of
Business" a new group should settle immediately — function, chain of
command, decision-making process, division of labor, alibi, and,
critically, a protocol for what happens if a member is compromised or
turned — plus four types of group bond: Trust, Cooperation, Ideological,
Local Interest, and four tactical group structures: Observe and Report,
Militarized Cells, Networking, and hunting-as-a-pack combat tactics).
A dedicated section, **"Not Killing Each Other,"** flags infighting and
paranoia as a leading cause of hunter deaths, on par with vampires
themselves. A "Big Leagues" section gives the narrator's dismissive,
in-character first impressions of larger hunter organizations —
**Arcanum** ("books with legs"), **Society of Leopold** (garbled by the
narrator as "the Society of Leonard," dismissed as zealous "tin
soldiers"), and **Project Twilight** (government spooks, "six wasted
months and a shitload of paperwork") — all given fuller, accurate
treatment later in Chapter Six. A "What We Know" section sketches
vampire society purely from an outsider's vantage, using the
narrator's own euphemisms rather than V20 terminology: **"the C
word"** for the Camarilla (bureaucratic, rule-bound, led by
city-by-city "Princes") and **"the Other Ones"** for the Sabbat
(zealous, unpredictable, unconcerned with hiding their nature). A
closing "All That Goes Bump Ain't Vampires" section briefly flags
other World of Darkness threats a hunter might stumble into —
werewolves, wizards, ghosts, demons, zombies, rival hunters, and
unclassifiable horrors the narrator calls "I Can't Even" — plus a
vague, Gehenna-adjacent unease ("Some End-of-the-World-Type Shit") that
even the vampires seem to be feeling lately.
**Key themes:** ordinary-person origin of hunters; isolation and
paranoia as occupational hazards; the deliberate asymmetry between
what a fledgling hunter (and the player) actually knows about Kindred
society versus what a seasoned V20 reader already knows.
**Key terms/entities:** the two unnamed narrators (captor and
captive); Arcanum, Society of Leopold ("Society of Leonard"), Project
Twilight; "the C word" (Camarilla), "the Other Ones" (Sabbat).
**Notable claims/data points:** three to six is given as the
narrator's personally preferred hunting-party size, not a game rule. A
sidebar ("Out of Your Depth," p. 68, technically Ch. 3 but thematically
continuous) claims vampire society is "named after the First Vampire,
Carmilla" — this contradicts V20's own Caine-centered cosmology
(`v20-core`) and should be read as an in-character error, not setting
fact; see "Cross-cutting themes" below.

## Chapter Two: Character Creation and Traits (pp. 32-46)
**Summary:** Walks through collaborative chronicle-theme selection
(six example chronicle premises given in full: Science vs. the
Occult, A Crusade Against Vampires, Protecting the Hood, Black Ops:
Best of the Best, Family Business, and Thrill Killas — the first
expanded into a full worked "Chronicle Synopsis Example," p. 35,
featuring recurring NPC Dr. Philip Zukera and sample player character
Midori) and mood selection (Sacrifice, Redemption, Community, Revenge,
Faith, Addiction, each posed as questions to the players). Then gives
the five-step hunter character-creation process, closely mirroring
V20's own: Concept/Motivation/Nature-Demeanor, Attributes (6/4/3 dot
spread, or an optional more generous 7/5/3), Abilities (13/9/5), Advantages,
and Freebie Points (21). This is largely mechanical reference
material — new sample concepts (Burnout, Crusader, Scientist), three
new Backgrounds (**Armory**, **Base of Operations**, **Guide** — a
mysterious spirit patron), the three Virtues (Conscience/Self-Control/
Courage, with an optional **Conviction** substitute for religious
fanatics), a set of hunter-specific Merits and Flaws (including
**Addicted to Vampire Blood**, **Poisonous Blood**, **Pale Aura**, and
**Combat Novice**), Health, and Experience rules — none of it is
catalogued exhaustively here; query the database by page or use
`--chapter "Chapter Two: Character Creation and Traits"` for exact
costs and dice pools.
**Key themes:** hunters are deliberately built to be outmatched by
vampires (the Attribute spreads and freebie-point budget are explicitly
calibrated for a "gritty, high-lethality" game); the temptation to
become a ghoul for power is treated as a recurring moral hazard (see
the "Sleeping with the Enemy: Becoming a Ghoul" sidebar, p. 45).
**Key terms/entities:** Dr. Philip Zukera and Midori (worked chronicle
example); Armory, Base of Operations, Guide.
**Notable claims/data points:** starting Humanity = Conscience +
Self-Control; starting Willpower = Courage — standard V20 mortal math
extended to hunters without modification.

## Chapter Three: Tools and Tactics (pp. 48-68)
**Summary:** Opens by categorizing how hunters actually learn about
vampires — popular culture (unreliable but not worthless, since in-world
fiction can leak real information), occult research (more reliable but
rarer and harder to verify), and direct experience (most reliable, most
dangerous) — and warns against **overgeneralizing** from a single
vampire's Clan-specific traits, illustrating three real cognitive
biases (Recall Bias, Confirmation Bias, Causation/Correlation) that get
hunters killed. The chapter's mechanical core is the **Plan Dice**
system: a six-step planning process (Info, Step by Step, Assign Jobs,
Poke Holes, Tool Up, Affairs in Order) that converts investigation and
preparation into a pooled dice resource the group can spend during the
actual confrontation, plus rules for what happens when the plan goes
wrong (Outmanned, Outgunned, Loose Cannon, Failure) and four named,
trainable **group combat tactics** (Same Damn Spot, Light 'em Up,
Staking, Beast Baiting), each requiring in-fiction practice together
before they can be used. The remainder is an extensive gear catalog —
low-tech and improvised weapons, vehicle modifications, explosives and
chemical weapons (including real, legally-purchasable compounds like
Tannerite and thermite), restraints, traps, and high-tech surveillance
gear — annotated throughout with three recurring in-character sidebar
"authors" (James Warder, Nadia Lukkari, Jonas Hansson) giving
first-person cautionary flavor rather than straight rules text. Closes
with "Out of Your Depth" (p. 68), warning that vampire society will
close ranks and may send an Assamite assassin after hunters who become
too successful, but also noting that Kindred infighting can be turned
to a hunter's advantage.
**Key themes:** information and preparation, not firepower, as the
deciding factor in a hunt; police/legal consequences as a constant
background pressure, continuing Chapter One's "The Cops" thread;
continued use of unreliable in-character sidebar narrators.
**Key terms/entities:** James Warder, Nadia Lukkari, Jonas Hansson
(sidebar voices, not chronicle characters).
**Notable claims/data points:** fire from gasoline is difficulty 7 to
soak, thermite difficulty 10 (p. 66); staking a stationary, restrained
vampire is difficulty 9 and requires 3+ levels of damage after soak to
pierce the heart (p. 62).

## Chapter Four: Numina (pp. 70-86)
**Summary:** Introduces **Numina** (from the Latin motto *numen
lumen*, "God is the light") as the catch-all term for rare
supernatural or psychic abilities some hunters develop, framed through
three competing in-world explanatory theories that are never resolved
as fact: the **Light of God** theory (a divine gift; some believers
call recipients **Nephilim**), the **Numen Theorem** (a scientific
proposal that humanity is evolving psychic defenses against vampires
as a predator), and outright skepticism. Splits Numina into three
mutually-exclusive classes a mortal can almost never combine: **Hedge
Magic** (superstition-fueled folk practice — Path of Curses, Path of
Divination, Path of Healing), **Psychic Phenomena** (trained or innate
mental gifts — Astral Projection, Cyberkinesis, Psychometry,
Pyrokinesis, Telekinesis), and **True Faith** (a single Path, capped
at 1 dot at character creation, tied to sustained religious devotion
and easily lost through hypocrisy). All of this is dense system
content; query the database for exact activation rolls, difficulties,
and level-by-level effects rather than relying on this summary.
**Key themes:** power always carries a structural cost or limit
(Numina paths can't usually be mixed; True Faith is fragile and must
be actively maintained); Numina-wielding hunters are viewed with the
same suspicion other hunters reserve for ghouls — a power gained from
proximity to the supernatural rather than pure human effort.
**Key terms/entities:** *numen lumen*; Light of God theory; Numen
Theorem; Nephilim.

## Chapter Five: Storytelling (pp. 88-116)
**Summary:** The book's Storyteller-facing chapter, restating and
developing the Introduction's two named themes — **Personal Horror**
and **Victory at a Cost** — and adding five optional "vampires as
metaphor" lenses a Storyteller can foreground: **Predator**,
**Parasite**, **Addict**, **Manipulator**, and **Monster**, each with
guidance on which vampiric traits to emphasize. Contrasts a hunter
chronicle against default Kindred-focused V20 play (Perspective —
hunters start ignorant of Clan/Sect terminology and should be kept
that way; Nightly-and-Daily Struggle — hunters operate on a normal
schedule, unlike vampires; Power — hunters must out-think, not
out-muscle, their prey) against what's the same (Horror, Desperation,
Survival). Walks through story structure (Prelude, opening hook,
intermediary scenes, confrontation, climax, aftermath) and four
**game-structure options** with explicit pros and cons: Linear
Narrative, One-Shot, Open-Ended Story, and Sandbox. Gives four
**genre toolkits** — Action, Adventure, Investigation/Mystery, and
Thriller — each with story-seed sidebars and film/book inspiration
lists (*The Blade* films, *The Lost Boys*, *Fright Night*, and others
are cited directly). Offers pacing advice and roughly a dozen named
**"bending the rules" GM techniques** to raise tension without
changing outcomes (Powerless, Real Time, Fish Out of Water, Every Tool
is a Weapon, Paranoia, Hidden Rolls, Tricks of the Light, among
others), each flagged as something to use sparingly.

The chapter's second half, **"In the Crosshairs,"** is a
faction-by-faction antagonist guide written specifically from a
hunter-story angle: the Camarilla (bureaucratic, negotiation-prone),
the Sabbat (unapologetically monstrous, pack-based), the Anarchs
(youth/rebellion themes), the Tal'Mahe'Ra (secretive, uses hunters as
disposable pawns against the Sabbat), the Inconnu (aloof, possibly
useful allies against other elders), and the four Independent Clans —
Assamites (contract assassins, may be hirable against a hunter's own
target once a hunter cell has made a name for itself), Followers of
Set (predatory "helpful" informants who profit either way), Giovanni
(a supernatural mafia — versatile antagonists spanning "almost any
kind of story"), and Ravnos (con artists and illusionists) — plus
Ghouls and Elders/Methuselahs as escalating antagonist tiers. Extends
the same treatment to non-vampire World of Darkness creatures
(Werewolves, Magi, Ghosts, Demons, Zombies, and a teased rival faction
of "the Others" — mortals with unexplained, possibly angelic powers)
and to **Mortal Law**, giving a police-investigation subsystem that
tracks an accumulating evidence trail back to the hunters over the
course of a chronicle. Closes with a **"To Gehenna or Not To
Gehenna"** sidebar (p. 116) on running an apocalyptic endgame, and
notes that in such a scenario hunters and vampires might find
themselves forced into uneasy cooperation.
**Key themes:** [see above — Personal Horror, Victory at a Cost, the
five vampire-metaphor lenses]; this chapter is unusually reusable
across the whole library, since it restates every major Sect and Clan
specifically from an outside, hostile vantage point that a Storyteller
running any other book in this collection could borrow for a hunter
subplot.
**Key terms/entities:** the five metaphor lenses (Predator/Parasite/
Addict/Manipulator/Monster); "the Others" (unresolved rival hunter
faction, deliberately left a mystery hook rather than statted).

## Chapter Six: Organizations and Resources (pp. 118-160)
**Summary:** The book's largest, most setting-dense chapter — an
extended expansion of the "Witch-Hunters" material that `v20-core`
only sketches in a few paragraphs (see Cross-cutting themes below).

**The Society of Leopold** (pp. 118-127) is the chapter's centerpiece:
a modern, interfaith-but-Catholic-rooted heir to the historical
Inquisition, currently under hardline Inquisitor-General **Ingrid
Bauer** ("the Iron Maiden"), who reinstated torture and opened combat
colleges after her predecessor **Monsignor Amelio Vittore** was
incapacitated. Covers the parent Catholic religious orders that supply
its membership (Benedictines, Dominicans, Franciscans, Jesuits); its
four official sub-orders — the militaristic **Condotierri**, the elite
"Sword of God" **Gladius Dei** (invitation-only, undefeated in its
sanctioned actions), the increasingly powerful internal-affairs
**Office of the Censor** (now the Society's real center of power), and
the semi-autonomous, women-only **Order of Saint Joan** (currently in
an unresolved standoff with Bauer, its Convent of Chartres gone
silent); its ideological sects (Brethren of Albertus — sanctioned
"good magic" practitioners; Children of Lazarus — the largest sect,
vampire-focused; Fathers of the Good Death; the heretical,
vampire-sympathizing **Sanbenito**; Sisterhood of Saint Claire; Order
of Saint Michael; Sons of Tertullian and its suicide-cult splinter the
Sect of Saint James); and its sanctioned Theurgist "**Judas
Witches**," who practice Hedge Magic reflavored as Christian ritual
(Via Geniorum for spirits and demons, Via Necromantiae for the dead).
New Backgrounds: **Mob**, **Reliquary**, **Status**. The Society traces
its founding ideology to **Leopold of Murnau**, author of "The
Testament of Leopold" and killed by a vampire — see Cross-cutting
themes below for this bundle's direct link to `dark-ages-inquisitor`.

**The U.S. Government** (pp. 127-136) covers three mutually unaware
hunter cells. The **NSA** runs a small "chaoscope" program (detecting
vampires and ghosts as "negative bodies") under **General Shivers**
and two colonels (Riley, Johnstone), compromised from within by two
vampire moles and a shadowy human group called the **Star Chamber**
that orchestrated the downfall of Shivers's predecessor, General
Arthur Clifford. The **FBI's Special Affairs Department (SAD)** has
been drastically shrunk (from an open institutional secret to three
skeleton field teams) following an internal shakeup, now run by
**Thomas Little**. The **CIA** has exactly one true believer, the
increasingly marginalized **Bob Schnoblin**, and the **DHS** maintains
an unofficial "Watch List" mailing group for agents who've noticed too
much. New Backgrounds: **Rank**, **Requisition**. New Psychic Numina:
**Animal Psi**, **Anti-Psi** (the latter a rare talent that suppresses
other psychics' powers nearby).

**The Arcanum** (pp. 137-144) is an openly public academic society
(it publishes a peer-reviewed journal, the *Annual Proceedings of the
Arcanum*) built around a hidden **Foundation House** in rural
Cambridgeshire, England, housing its great library the **Axis
Mundi**, with regional Chapter Houses and traveling field-research
Lodges organized by academic College. Officially non-violent since the
1910 Boston Chapter House Fire (a vampire reprisal), but an
increasingly active internal faction of "scholar-spies," pushed by
Chancellor **Sandeep D'Souza** of New Delhi, now feeds intelligence to
more aggressive hunters. New Backgrounds: **Artifact**, **Library**,
**Mentor**. Includes the **Path of Alchemy** (a Hedge Magic Path aimed
at eventual personal transmutation/immortality, not just potion-brewing).

**Criminals and Organized Crime** (pp. 144-152) profiles
vampire-entangled criminal groups — Italian Mafia families, the
Russian Organizatsiya/Bratva (including a rumored vampire-blood
"performance enhancement" lab called Arca), Central American cartels,
Chinese Tongs, and Japanese Yakuza — then details a specific ongoing
metaplot centered on **Detroit**: cartel boss **Santos "Sapa Inca" de
Sanza** has been waging an increasingly successful war on Kindred
influence across several US cities, secretly fed information by
**Kerberos**, a coterie of five Caitiff vampires actually created and
secretly led by a hidden Tzimisce elder, **Nevesa "Zek" Zekistraya**,
who deliberately botched her own childer's Embraces to keep them
disguised as clanless outcasts. **This resolves an open mystery left
dangling in `v20-core`** — see Cross-cutting themes below.

**Minor Factions** (pp. 152-156): **The Young Bloods** (an LA ghoul
biker gang holding a captive vampire, Eugene "Romulus" Bargen, as a
renewable, unbonded blood source); **Ikhwan al-Safa** (a Middle
Eastern Muslim hunter brotherhood allied with Djinn-binding **Taftâni**
mages); **Akritai** (Eastern Orthodox, border-guard-descended solo
hunters, recently damaged destroying an ancient seer named Tryphosa);
**Knights of St. George** (a large, old, international Christian
knighthood known by different regional names); and **The Judges** (an
increasingly centralized Jewish hunter network, currently fighting the
Lilith-worshipping **Bahari** vampire cult on two fronts).

Closes with **Hunting Gear** (a Requisition-point equipment catalog:
ammo, armor, forensics, imaging, raids, records, surveillance,
weaponry) and named **Relics and Artifacts** (magic items tied to the
Society of Leopold and the Arcanum, including "The Keys of Leopold" —
jailer's keys worked from three bone splinters believed to be
Leopold of Murnau's own), then an **Epilogue** (pp. 158-160): a
Society of Leopold priest's guilty, second-person confession to a
vampire he has been torturing for information, closing the book with
the same "testament left for a stranger" device that opened it (see
"Here Goes Everything," p. 6).
**Key themes:** institutional corruption and mission drift as a
hazard common to every organization in the chapter, not just the
Kindred they hunt; "who's really pulling the strings" as a structural
irony — vampires (Kerberos) secretly fund one side of a hunter/mafia
war, and Chapter One's narrator openly mocks the very organizations
this chapter treats seriously and sympathetically.
**Key terms/entities:** Ingrid Bauer, Amelio Vittore, Leopold of
Murnau; Condotierri, Gladius Dei, Office of the Censor, Order of Saint
Joan; General Shivers, Colonel Riley, Colonel Johnstone, Bob
Schnoblin; Sandeep D'Souza, the Axis Mundi; Santos de Sanza/Sapa Inca,
Kerberos, Nevesa "Zek" Zekistraya; the Young Bloods/Romulus, Ikhwan
al-Safa, Akritai, Knights of St. George, The Judges/the Bahari.
**Notable claims/data points:** a page-141 passage names "The
Inquisition," "Project Twilight," "Crucible Genetics Amalgamated," and
"the Society of Leopold" as four separate entities the Arcanum feeds
information to — since "the Inquisition" and "the Society of Leopold"
are the same organization everywhere else in this chapter, this reads
as a minor internal naming slip rather than a distinct fifth group.
"Crucible Genetics Amalgamated" itself is never elaborated anywhere
else in the book — treat it as an unresolved loose thread, not a
group this bundle can answer further questions about.

## Appendix: Template Characters (pp. 161-175)
**Summary:** Seven ready-to-play pregenerated hunters, explicitly
framed as consequences of specific Kindred carelessness or cruelty
("every action has consequence," p. 161): the **Obsessed Detective**
(a forensics case gone cold after teeth-mark evidence surfaced), the
**Paranoid Surveyor** (found a vampire's boarded-up daylight refuge
during a redevelopment survey), the **Destitute Crusader** (a homeless
zealot burning havens by day), the **Ruined Alderman** (a Dominated
city-hall pawn seeking revenge on an unknown manipulator), the
**Vengeful Wife** (unknowingly fed information by a rival vampire cult
to attack "the Swords of Caine" on their behalf), the **Genius Doctor**
(a self-interested cancer researcher who vivisects captured vampires
for data), and the **Jaded Journalist** (asthmatic and unable to fight
directly, instead compiling dossiers and anonymously tipping off more
capable hunters).
**Key themes:** each character concretizes one of Chapter One's
"Who Does This" archetypes; the Ruined Alderman and Vengeful Wife both
dramatize the book's recurring "unknowing pawn of a vampire" motif,
also seen in the Detroit/Kerberos storyline in Chapter Six.

## Hunters Hunted II Kickstarter Supporters (pp. 176-183)
Not narrative or rules content — a printed backer-name list from the
book's crowdfunding campaign. No thematic content; noted here only so
the page range is accounted for.

## Cross-cutting themes

### Leopold of Murnau: a direct character link to `dark-ages-inquisitor`
This is the single most concrete cross-book connection this bundle
offers. `dark-ages-inquisitor`'s Prelude introduces **Brother Leopold
von Murnau**, a Dominican inquisitor of the House of Murnau, last seen
in that book's Chapter One confronting his own corrupted cousin — his
ultimate fate is left open in that sourcebook. *This* book's Society
of Leopold reveres a **"Leopold of Murnau"** as its founding
ideological figure: author of "The Testament of Leopold" (quoted at
the head of the Society of Leopold section, p. 118), explicitly killed
by a vampire, and commemorated centuries later in a named relic ("The
Keys of Leopold," p. 159) made from bones believed to be his. Nothing
in either book states outright that these are the same continuity
event dramatized twice, but the name, order (House of Murnau), era
(13th-century Church), and cause of death (killed by a vampire) all
line up — read together, this library's two hunter-focused sourcebooks
supply a beginning (a living, struggling inquisitor) and an end/legacy
(a martyred founder-saint) for the same figure. If a question concerns
Leopold specifically, note this connection explicitly rather than
treating the two books' Leopolds as unrelated coincidences of naming.

### This book resolves an open mystery from `v20-core`
`v20-core`'s brief "Criminals" sidebar (Chapter Nine) mentions
mysterious informants — who all self-identify as "Caitiff" — feeding
intelligence to organized crime and inciting gang wars against
vampire-controlled criminal networks in Detroit, Miami, Los Angeles,
and Chicago, and states outright that their true identity and motive
is "an open mystery." This book's Chapter Six answers that question in
full: the informants are **Kerberos**, a five-vampire coterie secretly
created and led by a Tzimisce elder, Nevesa "Zek" Zekistraya, posing
as one of her own disguised Caitiff childer, working through Detroit
cartel boss Santos de Sanza. If a question concerns that specific
`v20-core` mystery, this book's Chapter Six ("Fighting for Control,"
p. 149) is the definitive follow-up, not a separate or contradictory
account.

### The Society of Leopold: consistent with, and a full expansion of, `v20-core`
`v20-core`'s own "Witch-Hunters" sidebar already names Ingrid Bauer
("The Original Iron Maiden") as the Society's current Inquisitor
General following Monsignor Amelio Vittore's stroke, and this book is
consistent with that framing throughout — it is a direct, non-
contradictory expansion rather than a divergent retelling, unlike the
Dark Ages/modern-books relationships tracked elsewhere in this
library's synthesis file.

### Unreliable narration, hunter edition
Consistent with this library's broader "Unreliable, plural history"
pattern (see `library_index.md`), this book adds hunter-side examples
of the same device: Chapter One's narrator garbles "Society of
Leopold" into "the Society of Leonard" and dismisses several
organizations Chapter Six later treats with real depth and sympathy; a
Chapter Three sidebar claims vampire society is "named after the First
Vampire, Carmilla," directly contradicting `v20-core`'s Caine-centered
cosmology. Treat both as characterization of an under-informed narrator,
not as alternate setting canon — this book does not intend "Carmilla"
as a rival origin myth to Caine.

### A hunter's-eye restatement of every major Sect and Clan
Chapter Five's "In the Crosshairs" section is unusually reusable
across this whole library: it restates the Camarilla, Sabbat, Anarchs,
Tal'Mahe'Ra, Inconnu, and all four Independent Clans specifically from
a hostile mortal vantage point. A Storyteller running any other book
in this collection who wants a hunter subplot or antagonist can pull
directly from this chapter rather than reasoning about mortal
perception from first principles.
