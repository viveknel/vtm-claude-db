## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Assamite,
the "Children of Haqim" or "Assassins" — a caste-structured clan
historically bound (until a recent, epoch-making event) by the Tremere's
curse against drinking other Kindred vitae. The book is written
in-character as a compiled clan history by a member with access to
Haqim's own writings, deliberately presenting multiple, sometimes
contradictory accounts (an allegorical "Parable" origin story vs. a
more grounded "history" section). Covers the three-caste social/
religious structure, the historic schism between loyalists and
reformists, the recent breaking of the Tremere's curse, and character
material.

## Chapter One: How Quickly We Forgot (pp. 8-30)
**Summary:**
- **Origin (the Parable of the Hunter):** An allegorical account of
  **Haqim** as a reformed warrior-turned-scholar in the First City, named
  "the Hunter" by his cousins for his pursuit of Truth, who reluctantly
  agrees to found a second, judge caste to police his siblings' unruly
  childer — then, worn down by centuries of conflict between his
  scholarly first family and the headstrong judges, abandons his house
  entirely after one final petty dispute, leaving the judges in charge.
- **Historical account:** A more grounded parallel version — Haqim as a
  warrior-hunter Embraced by (likely) Enoch/En'esh, a steadfast but
  neutral defender of the First City who later founds his own line in
  the Second City after observing it for decades, deliberately declaring
  neutrality in the coming "War of Ages." Notably, Haqim's line is the
  only Great Clan with no blood-curse traceable to Caine's displeasure —
  raising the open question of whether Caine was simply pleased with him
  or more subtly displeased than with, e.g., the Nosferatu progenitor.
- **The Second City and the Adversary (Baali):** Establishment of the
  three castes (**warriors, viziers/sorcerers, judges**) and their
  conflicts with the Baali; the clan's diaspora to the mountain fortress
  of **Alamut** after Haqim's own departure/disillusionment, delivering
  the clan's founding charge to "gather, to watch, to remember."
- **Classical/Crusades-era history:** Assamite dealings with Carthage,
  Rome, and later the Crusades; the historic prohibition (the Tremere's
  curse) against feeding on other vampires' vitae, forcing Assamites to
  subsist on mortal blood alone and shaping the clan's mercenary/tithe-
  based economy.
- **Recent upheaval:** The Tremere curse's breaking is treated as a
  historic turning point — the text notes factional splits over how to
  respond, some Assamites seeking peace or truce with the Camarilla,
  others redoubling efforts against other clans now that the restriction
  is gone.
**Key themes:** Deliberately doubled/contradictory origin narratives
(allegory vs. history) as an explicit authorial device; the founding
charge of neutrality/judgment as a recurring, unstable ideal that the
clan's warrior caste has since largely overridden; the recent lifting of
the Tremere curse as a destabilizing present-day crisis.
**Key terms/entities:** Haqim, Alamut, the three castes, the Baali/"the
Adversary," the Tremere curse (and its breaking).

## Chapter Two: Prayers to Broken Stone (pp. 32-77)
**Summary:** Details clan internal structure and the current schism.
- **Leadership hierarchy:** The **Eldest** ("the Old Man," a title held
  by both men and women historically), the **Caliph**, the **Amr**
  (bridging Caliph and Vizier duties), and the **Council of Scrolls**
  (combined assembly of viziers and sorcerers).
- **The schism:** A doctrinal split between **loyalists** and
  **schismatics** dating back to a duel between the elders al-Ashrad and
  ur-Shulgi; schismatic viziers have found uneasy accommodation with the
  Camarilla, while loyalists remain more insular; both factions unite
  only in despising the Sabbat **antitribu** (Assamites who broke from
  the clan's caste discipline entirely).
- **Caste life:** Warrior training (a seven-year apprenticeship
  culminating in a final field test), vizier life (more mortal-society
  contact, information-brokering, some parallels noted to the Toreador
  "clan of the rose"), and organizational units like **the Web**
  (seniority-based cells) and the "Thousand-Meter Club" (specialist
  snipers).
- **Relations with other clans:** Notes on Malkavians (extended charity
  despite unpredictability), the schismatic viziers' Toreador-like
  cultural role, and cooperation with Tzimisce koldun sorcerers.
- **Game material:** Extensive Discipline coverage of the clan's
  signature **Celerity**, **Obfuscate**, and **Quietus** (blood-magic/
  assassination Discipline) powers, plus vaults/grimoires, rituals, and
  Merits/Flaws (including "UnBroken," for Assamites for whom the Tremere
  curse persists longer than for their kin).
**Key themes:** Institutional schism as the clan's central present-day
tension, playing out across caste, faction, and generational lines; the
warrior caste's dominance over the originally-intended judge caste
established in Ch.1.
**Key terms/entities:** the Eldest, Caliph, Amr, Council of Scrolls,
loyalists vs. schismatics, the Web, Quietus.

## Chapter Three: Sons and Daughters of Haqim (pp. 80-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Bogeyman**, **Circuit Rider**,
**Tactical Coordinator**, **Contract Lawyer**, **Character Assassin**,
**Archaeologist**, **Pariah Dog**, **Witch-Hunter**, **Prophet of
Caine**, **Cleaner**. Closes with "The Heirs of Haqim" — profiles of
named elder Assamites, including **Tariq** (suspected by some
archbishops of involvement in a bloodline's destruction), **Fatima** (a
warrior who underwent a year's meditation near Mecca and commands
respect across generations, immune to Dominate/Presence/blood bonds via
mastery of Theft of Vitae), and other historically active elders (one
tied to Byzantine-era intrigue, another lost to Final Death after a
storm-spirit ritual went wrong).
**Key themes:** Range from field operatives and specialists to
scholarly/spiritual figures, reflecting the three-caste structure
established in Chapters One and Two.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Doubled, contested origin narrative**: the explicit pairing of an
  allegorical "Parable" account with a more grounded "historical" one in
  Chapter One signals throughout that clan self-knowledge is mediated
  and uncertain, similar in spirit (though structurally distinct) to
  other clanbooks' unreliable-narrator techniques.
- **Neutrality betrayed by faction**: Haqim's founding ideal of
  impartial judgment is established early and then shown eroding —
  first into caste conflict (judges vs. scholars) in Ch.1, then into the
  loyalist/schismatic schism in Ch.2.
- **A clan in genuine flux**: unlike most other clanbooks in this
  library, this one treats a major setting-shaking event (the breaking
  of the Tremere curse) as current, unresolved history actively
  reshaping clan identity in the present.
