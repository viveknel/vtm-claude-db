# Guide to the Sabbat (WW2303) — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes, stat blocks, or precise rules
text, query `book_chunks.db` (see `README.md` in this bundle for query
examples) rather than relying on the summaries below.

**Edition note:** This is a classic **Revised Edition** (c. 1998–2002)
Vampire: The Masquerade sourcebook — White Wolf product WW2303, published
1999/2000 (Justin R. Achilli et al.). It predates V20 by over a decade and
is not itself V20 material, but it can be assumed to **generally agree**
with V20's own Sabbat write-ups unless a V20 book explicitly contradicts
something stated here — V20 always takes canon priority when the two
differ. This was checked directly, not assumed: against `v20-core`, the
Code of Milan text (p. 20 here) matches `v20-core`'s own reproduction
almost verbatim; sect titles/hierarchy and the Black Hand correspond
directly; six of this book's 13 auctoritas ritae (Blood Bath, Blood Feast,
Creation Rites, Monomacy, War Party, Wild Hunt) reappear by name in
`v20-core`'s appendix; seven of this book's seven Paths of Enlightenment
carry forward into V20's eleven, and `v20-core` explicitly notes its "Path
of Death and the Soul" is mechanically identical to this book's write-up;
and the Salubri antitribu's leader, Adonai, is named identically in both
`v20-core` and `v20-lore-bloodlines`. No direct contradiction turned up in
this check — where V20 is silent on a specific detail this book states
(e.g. it never names a current Sabbat regent), that's a gap, not a
contradiction.
Page citations below are PDF page numbers, which match `book_chunks.db`'s
`page` column exactly (front matter runs pages 1–5, so content pages equal
printed-book page + 1).

## Overview
The sect-focused companion to *Guide to the Camarilla*, this book presents
the Sabbat — the "Sword of Caine" — as a holy-war sect of vampires who
openly embrace their monstrous nature rather than clinging to Humanity or
hiding behind a Masquerade. It covers sect history and organization
(Chapter One), the sect's exclusive clans/bloodlines ("antitribu") (Chapter
Two), Sabbat-specific character creation (Chapter Three), high-level
Discipline powers and Dark Thaumaturgy (Chapter Four), the Paths of
Enlightenment and the sect's ritual life — the *ritae* (Chapter Five),
Storytelling advice for running Sabbat-focused or Sabbat-vs-Camarilla
chronicles (Chapter Six), guidance for building a Sabbat-held city and
running crusades/espionage (Chapter Seven), and an appendix of NPC
templates, ghouls, revenant families and weapons. A recurring editorial
note (p. 40, p. 98) is that this volume deliberately downplays
infernalism/Dark Thaumaturgy relative to earlier Sabbat books, treating it
as a moral-hazard subplot rather than a default character option.

The book opens with a fiction piece, "Smart Money's on Vegas" (credited in
front matter as "Fait Accompli," p. 2, suggesting a working title), narrated
by a Sabbat anarch-recruiter, Adam Stiers, and his companion Ellum, whose
casual cross-country violence and scouting of Las Vegas for a Sabbat
takeover sets the sect's tone before any rules text appears.

## Front Matter (pp. 1–5)
Half-title, credits (design: Justin R. Achilli et al.; © 1999 White Wolf),
and table of contents. Notably the TOC (p. 4) lists Chapter Seven under an
apparent working title, "In the Fiends' Lair," which does not match the

## Introduction: "Smart Money's on Vegas" (pp. 6–11)
Fiction. First-person account by Adam Stiers (Sabbat anarch recruiter) and
his childe/partner Ellum: robbing a convenience store, a deadly "pink
slip" drag race that turns into a forced Embrace and murder ("Judas"), and
a botched scouting trip to Las Vegas where they run into Ventrue
territorial security (a Tzimisce-esque enforcer "Montrose" and a ghoul
"Alexander Cantor") working for a Prince Benedic. Ends with the pair
reporting back to "Bishop Mark" that Vegas will fall to the Sabbat within
weeks. Establishes the sect's voice: gleeful, casual brutality married to
genuine strategic purpose (territorial scouting for a coming siege).

## Chapter One: The Sword of Caine (pp. 12–51)
**Summary:** The sect's central worldview, history and organizational
chart. Opens with "Young Cainites' Opinions" vs. "Elder Cainites' Opinions"
sidebars (pp. 12–13) on the Antediluvians, Camarilla, Inconnu, Lupines,
Mages, Wraiths, Changelings, mortals and each other — establishing
generational disagreement even within the sect as a feature, not a bug.

**Sect history (pp. 13–19):** Traces Sabbat origin through the Anarch
Revolt — the Lasombra's diablerie of their Antediluvian by the anarch
Gratiano (13th–14th c., contested details), the Tzimisce anarch diablerie
of "the Eldest Fiend" via leader Lugoj Blood-breaker, and the Assamite/anarch
war on elders that produced the **Convention of Thorns** (23 Oct. 1493, full
text reproduced pp. 15), which nominally ended the Anarch Revolt but which
the Sabbat views as elder betrayal. Sect history continues through Old
World vs. New World colonization (the Sabbat used the American Revolution,
French-Indian War and frontier violence as cover), the **Purchase Pact**
(1803, full text p. 17, ending open war among Sabbat), three **Sabbat Civil
Wars** (18th c. Lasombra-vs-Tzimisce war over New World territory; a
second civil war culminating in the **Code of Milan**, reaffirmed 1933,
full text p. 20, and the recognition of the **Panders** as a clan-equivalent
after the Brujah antitribu-backed 1957 coup attempt), down to modern
geographic influence (North America, Central/South America — Mexico City
as spiritual/political heart under regent **Melinda Galbraith**, a
Toreador antitribu — Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia).

**Sect organization (pp. 29–37):** Titles and hierarchy (Regent → Cardinals
→ Prisci → Archbishops → Bishops → Ducti → Pack Priests → Templars/Paladins
→ Pack Members; full title/address table p. 30). Factions: **the Black
Hand** (elite militia/"sect within a sect," led by four **Seraphim**
reporting to the regent, drawn heavily from Assamite and Gangrel antitribu;
distinguishing palm-brand); **the Sabbat Inquisition** (heresy-hunters, esp.
targeting infernalism, doubled from 15 to 30 members); the **Loyalist
faction** (radical individual-freedom purists, disorganized, young-skewing);
**Lesser factions** — the Status Quo, Moderates, Ultra-Conservatives, and
the extinct Sabbat Fabians; plus internal dissent groups (rogue Old World
Tzimisce who abstain, rogue Lasombra antitribu who secretly support the
Camarilla, the **Order of St. Blaise** — a Catholic-infiltrating secret
society — and the **Children of the Dracon**, a contrarian Tzimisce
knightly order).

**Unlife topics (pp. 40–49):** Creation Rites (the ritual that makes a
Sabbat recruit "True Sabbat" — distinct from the Embrace itself); Packs
(the sect's basic social/combat unit, replacing solitary vampire norms);
Havens as shared "parishes," plus nomadic "oases" (supply caches); a
capsule cross-reference to ritae (detailed in Chapter Five); Sect Justice
(a crime/punishment table — treason, murder of a fellow Sabbat, deceiving
leaders, revealing secrets, cowardice, etc., pp. 45); "Fun and Games" —
sanctioned vampire pastimes like The Races, Drive-By, Football, Trick or
Treat, Human Gladiators; and a full **Sabbat Lexicon** (auctoritas ritae,
Black Hand, Cainite, Jyhad, Kindred, Loyalist, Monomacy, pack, Vaulderie,
Vinculum, etc.) plus "Vulgar Argot" slang and "Old Form" archaic terms
(pp. 46–49) — useful as a standing glossary for the whole book.

## Chapter Two: Around the Fires (pp. 52–81)
**Reference material — clan/bloodline catalog, not summarized power-by-power.**
Presents the 14 clans/bloodlines that make up the Sabbat outside Lasombra
and Tzimisce (covered in the core *Vampire: The Masquerade* rulebook):
**Assamite antitribu** ("Angels of Caine" — Black Hand-heavy, addicted to
vampire vitae), **Blood Brothers** ("Frankensteins" — artificially
engineered hive-mind shock troops, Storyteller-only bloodline, created via
Sanguinus by vanished Tremere antitribu/Tzimisce sorcerers),
**Brujah antitribu** ("Brutes"), **Gangrel antitribu** ("Hunters," split
into Country and City sub-lines), **Harbingers of Skulls** ("Lazarenes" —
recent, powerful, corpse-faced necromancers, Storyteller-only), **Kiasyd**
("Weirdlings" — fae-tainted scholar bloodline linked to Lasombra),
**Malkavian antitribu** ("Freaks"), **Nosferatu antitribu** ("Creeps" —
surprisingly humane info-brokers), **Panders** (clanless "Mutts," True
Sabbat despite no bloodline pedigree), **Ravnos antitribu** ("Rogues" —
gorgio, code-bound to their own clan), **Salubri antitribu** ("Furies" —
recent anti-Camarilla convert bloodline led by the Seventh Generation
Adonai, wields the reforged Discipline Valeren), **Serpents of the Light**
("Cobras" — Haiti-based Setite splinter, bitter enemies of the Followers of
Set), **Toreador antitribu** ("Perverts"), and **Ventrue antitribu**
("Crusaders" — chivalric, Inquisition-heavy). Each entry follows a fixed
template (Nickname, Appearance, Haven, Background, Character Creation,
Clan Disciplines, Weaknesses, Organization, Quote, plus in-character
"Stereotypes"/"View From Without" sidebars) — go to `book_chunks.db` for
any specific clan's exact Discipline list, weakness text or quote.

## Chapter Three: Sons and Daughters (pp. 82–97)
**Reference material — Sabbat-specific character creation, not summarized
step-by-step.** Walks through building a Sabbat PC: concept, clan/antitribu
choice, pack type (nomadic "pilgrims" vs. founded "coven"), Sabbat-specific
Nature/Demeanor Archetypes (Capitalist, Chameleon, Creep Show, Enigma, Eye
of the Storm, Guru, Sadist, Sociopath), the seven Paths of Enlightenment
(cross-referenced to Chapter Five) as an alternative to Humanity, and
Sabbat chargen deltas from the core rules (4 free Discipline dots from
clan Disciplines only; 0 starting Background dots — Creation Rites strip
mortal ties; 5 Virtue dots instead of 7 if Humanity is kept; Path-follower
minimums; Vinculum-rolling instead of starting Backgrounds). New Secondary
Abilities specific to the sect (Fortune-Telling, Panhandling, Fire Dancing,
Black Hand Lore, Underworld Lore, Vamp); new/adapted Backgrounds
(Alternate Identity, Black Hand Membership, Rituals, Sabbat Status); and
new Merits/Flaws (Early Riser, Vulnerability to Silver, Lazy, Infertile
Vitae, Sanctity, Special Responsibility, Introspection, Flashbacks).

## Chapter Four: The Gifts of Caine (pp. 98–127)
**Reference material — high-level (Discipline levels 6–9) power catalog,
not summarized power-by-power.** Covers **Dark Thaumaturgy** in detail
(explicitly framed as "a Bad Thing" the Storyteller need not allow, pp.
98–99) with two full paths — **Fires of the Inferno** and **the Path of
Phobos** — plus five Dark Thaumaturgical rituals (Bind the Interloper,
Call Forth the Host, Felis Negrum, Plague's Secret Domain, Close the Ways,
Into the Abyss) and a demon-tier table (Imp/Shade/Pit Lord/Lesser Demon
Noble/Greater Demon). Also details elder-level powers for **Dementation**
(including the setting-notable "Lunatic Eruption," said to have been used
at the fall of Carthage), **Mytherceria** (Kiasyd), **Necromancy** (the
Harbingers of Skulls' own Mortuus Path, distinct from Giovanni paths),
**Obtenebration** (Lasombra), **Sanguinus** (Blood Brothers — Gestalt
hive-mind, Coagulated Entity fusion power), **Thaumaturgy** (two Sabbat-
exclusive paths: the martial **Path of Mars** and the Book of
Nod-referencing **Path of the Father's Vengeance**, plus a Koldunic Sorcery
sidebar on Tzimisce-exclusive blood magic), **Valeren** (Salubri antitribu),
and **Vicissitude**, including an optional "Tainted Blood" subsystem
(pp. 123–125) that treats Vicissitude as an infectious disease with
randomized, Storyteller-secret learning costs and derangement risk for any
non-Tzimisce who tastes Tzimisce blood.

## Chapter Five: Codes of the Night (pp. 128–165)
**Summary:** The sect's moral and ritual life — the highest-value chapter
for understanding Sabbat philosophy and conduct, and narrative/thematic
rather than purely mechanical.

**Paths of Enlightenment (pp. 128–145):** Full write-ups (Nickname, Basic
Beliefs, Ethics, Virtues, History, Current Practices, Description of
Followers, Following the Path, Common Abilities, Preferred Disciplines,
plus a Hierarchy of Sins table) for the seven paths the book identifies as
mainstream Sabbat morality — **Path of Caine** ("Noddists," scholarly
students of Caine's example), **Path of Cathari** ("Albigensians,"
Manichean-derived hedonists who see vampires as agents of a corrupting
"evil creator"), **Path of Death and the Soul** ("Necronomists," the
sect's oldest path, Tzimisce-founded death-scholars), **Path of the Feral
Heart** ("Beasts," predator-purists; sidebar pp. 138 explains this path
split from the now-non-Sabbat **Path of Harmony** over the Sabbat's
environmental/mortal-abuse tactics), **Path of Honorable Accord**
("Knights," the sect's chivalric backbone, grown from the Code of Milan),
**Path of Lilith** ("Bahari"/"Lilins," a heretical pain-as-enlightenment
mystery cult built on an alternate Creation myth), and **Path of Power and
the Inner Voice** ("Unifiers," control/domination-focused, founded by the
Lasombra Lord Marcus). A short sidebar (p. 145) also covers the heretical,
Inquisition-hunted **Path of Evil Revelations** ("Corrupters"/"Slaves,"
infernalist demon-servants) — the "eighth path," explicitly outside sect
sanction. A "Transcendence and Beyond" sidebar (p. 145) notes Golconda is
theoretically possible but culturally rejected by Sabbat philosophy.

**Sabbat Ritae (pp. 145–163):** The sect's full ritual life. The 13
**auctoritas ritae** practiced sect-wide: the Binding (Winter Solstice oath
of fealty, raises Vinculum scores sectwide for a month), the Blood Bath
(confirms a new title-holder), the Blood Feast (ritual feeding on bound
victims — sidebar "Transubstantiation: Myth or Madness" links it to a
rumored Tremere-antitribu-derived ceremonial-magic origin), Creation Rites
(detailed further here vs. Chapter One), Festivo dello Estinto ("Festival
of the Dead," a week-long March city-wide revel), Fire Dance (mastering
Rötschreck by leaping bonfires), Games of Instinct (Cowboys and Indians,
Demolition Derby, Rousing the Beast, the Rat/Bat Race, Dogtagging — sanctioned,
priest-blessed violent "sports"), Monomacy (ritual duel to the death, often
ending in diablerie of the loser), Palla Grande (the Grand Ball, All
Hallows' Eve masquerade combining public-facing revelry with a hidden
archbishop's Blood Bath), Sermons of Caine (Book of Nod recitation/debate),
the Vaulderie (the pack-bonding blood ritual that produces **Vinculi**
—full scoring/mechanics and a worked example, pp. 154–155), the War Party
(multi-pack hunt-and-diablerize-an-elder ritual hunt), and the Wild Hunt
(pursuit and destruction of a Sabbat traitor who has leaked sect secrets).
A wide sampling of **ignoblis ritae** (pack-specific, non-universal)
follows: Acceptance Ritus, Allegiance Ritus (for Camarilla defectors, marks
them via Vicissitude), Contrition Ritus, Welcoming Ritus, Ritus of
Thanksgiving, Martial Ritus, Spilling of Blood, Stealth Ritus, Sun Dance,
Tests of Pain (Indian Stick trial, Trial by Fire, the Gauntlet), the Asp's
Blessing (snake-handling), and Truth Revealed — plus sect-recognition
practices (Sabbat Oath of Loyalty, the inverted-ankh Symbol of the Sabbat,
Test of Verification, and a rumored secret Vaulderie-breaking ritual,
sidebar "Breaking the Bond," p. 158).

**New Derangements (pp. 161–163):** Berserk, Blood Sweats, Gluttony,
Nymphomania/Satyriasis, Overcompensation, Phobia — sect-flavored mental
scars from Sabbat unlife.

## Chapter Six: Chronicles of Blood (pp. 166–193)
**Summary:** Storyteller-facing advice for running Sabbat-focused (or
Sabbat-vs-Camarilla) chronicles — narrative/advice content, not mechanical.
Opens by directly challenging the "Sabbat = mindless villain" assumption,
arguing the sect has a coherent (if inhuman) ethical logic rooted in
Caine's mythic choice of freedom over servitude to God (pp. 167–169), and
offers six framing "concepts" for running Sabbat antiheroes with real
stakes: the Sabbat believes itself right and the Camarilla wrong;
antiheroes follow codes and can be held to them; beliefs motivate action;
actions have consequences even for the faithful; antiheroes have flaws;
antiheroes need enemies of equal or worse evil (infernal-tainted NPCs are
suggested as a release valve for players uncomfortable with unmotivated
Sabbat cruelty). A substantial section (pp. 176–178) on calibrating mature
content — matching graphic intensity to troupe comfort, logical vs.
illogical settings for depraved rites — reflects genuine table-management
concerns of the era. Also details the **Sabbat's internal political
factions** as story tools (Status Quo, Moderates, Ultra-Conservatives,
Loyalists, Black Hand, Inquisition, Panders — each with a paragraph on how
to place them dramatically) and how Sabbat rise through pack, mid-tier and
high-tier status. Covers propaganda/misinformation between Sabbat and
Camarilla, a worked actual-play transcript demonstrating ritae as plot
device (a Fire Dance scene, pp. 185–187), a reference table matching each
of the 13 auctoritas ritae to story uses (pp. 187–188), guidance on
running **city crusades** and the long-term "Great Jyhad" as episodic/epic
structures, Lupine-hunting and diablerie as plot devices, pack dynamics
(inter/intra-pack rivalry, differing philosophies), and a closing list of
Sabbat-suited chronicle themes (Damnation, Existence, Freedom,
Individualism and the Struggle for Self, Infernal Corruption, Intrigue,
Mercy, Power) and moods (Fear, Lust, Paranoia, Anger, Black Comedy).

## Chapter Seven: Building a Sabbat City (pp. 196–205)
**Summary:** Storyteller-only toolkit (explicitly marked as such, p. 196)
for constructing a Sabbat-controlled city and running the siege/crusade
process. Establishes that Sabbat cities run on barely-controlled anarchy
(archbishop or bishop-council nominal oversight, real cohesion coming only
from the Vaulderie), tolerate much higher crime/violence than Camarilla
cities as deliberate cover, and support roughly double the "normal"
vampire population density (~1 per 50,000 mortals) due to loose feeding
and wartime mass-Embracing. Walks through the **crusade process** in
sequence: "Starting the Revolution" (scout/espionage phase — Vicissitude-
enabled spies, Dominated moles, revenant infiltrators), "The Sword of
Caine" (the attack itself — deliberately provoking the Camarilla into
Masquerade-breach cover-up work, then striking Tremere chantries and
princely leadership), "Recruitment" (mass-Embrace "cannon fodder" tactics),
"Crushing Resistance" (mopping up partisan holdouts), and "The Agony of
Defeat" (how a well-run Camarilla defense — influence, justicars/archons,
Tremere counter-magic — actually beats the Sabbat). A **"Deep Cover"**
section (pp. 201–203) details long-term Sabbat espionage tactics:
"Manchurians" (Dominate-brainwashed sleeper agents unaware of their own
loyalty), double agents posing as rival clans, Vicissitude-based
"Replacement Vampires" (fleshcrafted impersonation, possibly used
historically by "Dracula"), Harbinger-of-Skulls-enabled "Soul-Swapping"
(Bone Path Necromancy), and how deep-cover agents maintain their Vinculum
from a distance (smuggled Vaulderie blood, or — reluctantly — a blood
bond).

## Appendix: Allies, Antagonists and Others (pp. 206–222)
**Reference material — full NPC stat blocks, ghoul/revenant templates and
weapons tables, not summarized entry-by-entry.** Provides ready-to-use
Sabbat character templates spanning the hierarchy (Bishop, Archbishop,
Priscus, Cardinal, Templar/Paladin, three Black Hand ranks — Agent,
Remover, Dominion — Sabbat Inquisitor, New/Experienced/Veteran Coven
Member, New/Experienced/Veteran Pilgrim, Novice/Expert Scout, Interrogator,
Diablerie Addict), each with full Traits, Disciplines, a Path of
Enlightenment score, and Roleplaying Hints/in-character Image text. Covers
Sabbat attitudes toward and templates for **ghouls** (treated as disposable
labor/bait/food) and details the **four surviving Tzimisce revenant
families** — **Bratovitch** (feral rural hound-breeders), **Grimaldi**
(political/corporate go-betweens, secretly plotting a contingency defection
to Camarilla Ventrue or the Giovanni), **Obertus** (reclusive scholars
secretly experimenting toward a post-human "Homo obertus"), and **Zantosa**
(decadent socialites guarding a mysterious missing torpid Tzimisce elder,
recently purged internally) — each with Disciplines and a family
weakness. Closes with new combat maneuvers (Chainsaw Gouge, Hollywood
Shooting, flamethrower Spray) and a weapons appendix (firearms traits;
blunt weapons — bottle, sledgehammer; edged weapons — board with a nail,
chainsaw, cleaver, machete, meathook, straight razor).

## Cross-cutting themes

**Freedom vs. structure, in constant tension.** The sect's founding
identity is anti-authoritarian ("freedom" is a Code of Milan tenet, p. 20,
and the central Loyalist rallying cry), yet nearly every chapter documents
the sect building the very structures it claims to reject: titles (Ch. 1),
the Code of Milan and Purchase Pact (Ch. 1), auctoritas ritae (Ch. 5), and
Storyteller advice built explicitly around this contradiction (Ch. 6, pp.
179–183, on the sect's political factions arguing over exactly this
question). No book-internal resolution is offered — it's presented as the
Sabbat's permanent internal fault line, mirrored in the Status Quo vs.
Moderate vs. Ultra-Conservative vs. Loyalist factional split.

**The pack as surrogate family/unit of account.** Introduced structurally
in Chapter One (pp. 41–44) and mechanically reinforced everywhere after:
Vaulderie/Vinculum bonding (Ch. 5) is *the* loyalty mechanism (replacing
blood bonds, which Sabbat disdain), Sabbat chargen (Ch. 3) assumes a
shared-pack party, and Storytelling advice (Ch. 6) treats packs, not
individuals, as the sect's real dramatic unit.

**Deliberate rejection of the Masquerade, but not of secrecy.** The Sabbat
scorns the Camarilla's Masquerade in principle (Ch. 1, p. 21) yet still
practices a de facto "nondisclosure" policy in modern/Western cities (Ch.
1, p. 33) and treats Masquerade-breach as a *tactical weapon* against the
Camarilla specifically (Ch. 7, "The Sword of Caine," p. 199) rather than a
universal restraint. Sabbat cities are messier and higher-crime than
Camarilla ones by design, not by indiscipline alone.

**Infernalism is present but deliberately minimized.** Both the
Dark Thaumaturgy chapter (Ch. 4, pp. 98–99) and the Path of Evil
Revelations writeup (Ch. 5, p. 145) go out of their way to frame
infernalism as a hunted, marginal heresy rather than a default Sabbat trait
— an explicit authorial break from earlier-edition Sabbat books, called
out directly in a sidebar ("The Devil's Due," p. 40).

**Every institution has a shadow/dissenting mirror.** Rogue Old World
Tzimisce who abstain from the sect; Lasombra antitribu who secretly favor
the Camarilla; the Grimaldi revenant family's secret contingency plan to
defect; the missing torpid elder the Zantosa guard; the Loyalist
faction's rejection of leadership itself. The book consistently pairs each
sect institution with an internal crack, reinforcing Chapter Six's framing
that Sabbat "flaws" (not just Camarilla ones) are the intended source of
dramatic conflict.

## Glossary / key terms index
- **Antediluvians** — 3rd-generation vampire elders; the Sabbat's ultimate
  enemy and the reason the sect exists (p. 13ff, Ch. 1 throughout)
- **antitribu** — "anti-clan"; Sabbat-aligned offshoot of a Camarilla/
  independent clan (Ch. 2 throughout)
- **auctoritas ritae** — the 13 sect-wide high rituals (Ch. 5, pp. 145–158)
- **Black Hand** — the sect's elite militia/sub-sect, led by four Seraphim
  (p. 34–36)
- **Code of Milan** — the sect's closest thing to a written law code,
  reaffirmed 1933 (p. 20)
- **Convention of Thorns** — the 1493 treaty ending the Anarch Revolt,
  full text (p. 15)
- **Creation Rites** — the rite that makes a recruit True Sabbat, distinct
  from the Embrace (p. 40, p. 147)
- **ignoblis ritae** — pack-specific lesser rituals, highly variable (Ch. 5,
  pp. 158–161)
- **Manchurians** — Dominate-brainwashed sleeper spies unaware of their own
  true loyalty (Ch. 7, p. 201)
- **Monomacy** — ritual duel to the death, often ending in diablerie (p. 151)
- **Palla Grande** — the Grand Ball, All Hallows' Eve (p. 152)
- **Panders** — clanless True Sabbat, recognized as a clan-equivalent after
  the 1957 coup (p. 20, p. 68)
- **Purchase Pact** — 1803 internal non-aggression pact among Sabbat (p. 17)
- **regent** — the sect's nominal supreme leader; Melinda Galbraith
  (Toreador antitribu) holds the post as of this book (p. 31)
- **Sabbat Civil Wars** — three internal wars (18th c.; culminating 1933;
  1957) (pp. 17–20)
- **Seraphim** — the four vampires who command the Black Hand (p. 36)
- **Vaulderie / Vinculum** — the pack-blood ritual and the loyalty-bond it
  produces (Ch. 5, pp. 153–155)
- **War Party** — ritual multi-pack hunt to diablerize a non-Sabbat elder
  (p. 156)
- **Wild Hunt** — ritual pursuit/destruction of a Sabbat traitor (p. 157)
