# Rites of the Blood — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A 2014 V20 (modern-nights) sourcebook covering blood magic across every
Kindred faction — Camarilla/Tremere, Sabbat, Anarch, the Independent clans
(Assamite, Setite, Giovanni), the Inconnu and Tal'Mahe'Ra, and Infernalism
— organized by *who practices it and why* rather than by discipline. Its
explicit theme is "the cost of power": every tradition covered exacts a
price from its practitioners, from Tremere social isolation to literal
soul-selling. A sidebar states the book deliberately departs from other
V20 material and from strict metaplot continuity "to dig into the most
interesting parts of the rich history of the World of Darkness" — treat
its more surprising claims (a surviving Telyavelic Tremere lineage, a
still-active True Black Hand) as this book's own take, not necessarily
reconciled with other sourcebooks. A framed fiction piece, "The Price,"
opens the book and is thematically load-bearing throughout (see Cross-
cutting themes).

## The Price (fiction prelude) / Introduction (pp. 7-16)
**Summary:** A Tzimisce elder, Kosczecsyku, and his childe Vrisa interrogate
and ultimately transform a captured Tremere antitribu — Jurgin Shaulis, who
claims membership in "the Telyavelic line" — into a living, sentient
spellbook made from his own skin, bones, and blood. The frame device
(Kosczecsyku showing Vrisa his collection of similarly-made tomes from a
Setite and others) sets up the book's recurring image of knowledge
literally extracted at the cost of a body and a self. The Introduction
explains the book's organization (one chapter per faction) and gives a
Lexicon of practitioner terms (ashipu, houngan, lector priest, nahualli,
sadhu, thaumaturge, thrall, wangateur, etc.).
**Key themes:** knowledge extracted at mortal/personal cost, set up as the
literal embodiment of the book's "cost of power" theme.
**Key terms/entities:** Kosczecsyku, Vrisa, Jurgin Shaulis (Telyavelic
Tremere antitribu) — see Chapter Two for the Telyav bloodline's fuller
treatment.

## Chapter One: The Ivory Tower (pp. 17-33)
**Summary:** Tremere/Camarilla politics. Traces the Tremere's rise from
diablerist outsiders (having stolen immortality via Tzimisce vitae) to
indispensable Camarilla pillars — disrupting the Inquisition's paperwork,
curing plague-tainted herds during the Black Death, cursing their own
Sabbat antitribu and the entire Assamite clan at the Treaty/Convention of
Thorns to prove loyalty. Covers Tremere political roles (Seneschal,
Sheriff/"Dresden" deputy, Scourge, Harpy, Archon) and "The Economy of
Thaumaturgy" — the clan's Black List of Kindred denied thaumaturgical aid,
boon-pricing by ritual level.
**Key themes:** blood magic monopoly as the Tremere's only real security
within the Camarilla; the Tremere as tolerated but resented "rabid dog."
**Key terms/entities:** the Black List; Camarilla resentments (Nosferatu
watching for the chance to avenge the Gargoyles made from their dead).
**Notable claims/data points:** frames the Assamite curse and the Tremere's
Camarilla admission as directly linked events at Thorns — consistent with,
and adding Camarilla-side detail to, this library's existing Tremere/
Assamite material.

## Chapter Two: The Sword of Caine (pp. 34-53)
**Summary:** Sabbat sorcery and religion. The Sabbat is framed as an
end-times church ("By Caine, the Regent, and the ritae") rather than a
"dark Camarilla," built around the Book of Nod and other scriptures
(Erciyes Fragments, Ubar Chronicles, Gospels of Irad & Adah, the heretical
Canticle of Japheth claiming Caine is dead). Surveys Sabbat sorcery by
type — Koldunism (tied to the elemental entity Kupala), Tremere
Thaumaturgy, Setite Sorcery (the Serpents of the Light, a schism from
Egyptian Setite orthodoxy), Assamite Al-Aziz (a splinter Assamite-sorcerer
lineage using the drug kalif), Necromancy (rare; the Harbingers of Skulls),
and Abyss Mysticism (Lasombra "Shadowed," possibly originated by the
antitribu Lord Aludian Thex — see `v20-tome-of-secrets`, which also
credits Thex-adjacent Abyss Mysticism lore). Details the Ritae system in
full: Auctoritas ("High") Ritae vs. Ignoblis ("pack") and Faction Ritae;
Sabbat history of the ritae (ancient Tzimisce greeting customs formalized
by successive Regents at ecumenical Synods); three Black Cathedrals
(Mexico City, Montreal, Bucharest); the Binding, Rite of Contrition,
Creation Rites, High Holidays (Palla Grande, Festivo Dello Estinto,
Walpurgisnacht, the Sacrificial Revel, the Eides of Caine), Monomacy
(ritual dueling), the Vaulderie, War Party, and Wild Hunt. Covers the six
Sabbat political factions in detail — Ultra Conservative (centralizing
hardliners; the ritus Irad's Wall, rediscovered by Livia Yorke), Orthodox
(pro-ritae religious revivalists; Atra Sacramentum, the Last Rites), Loyalist
(shaped by Native American shamanic contact; Purity of Babel, Calling the
Directions), Order of St. Blaise (mortal-facing infrastructure specialists;
Martyr's Eyes, developed by Pyotr Stanislav), Pander (the clanless faction
founded by Joseph Pander after the third Sabbat civil war; the Renaming
Ceremony), and Status Quo (change-averse mediators; Laurel of Concordance)
— plus apolitical "Moderates."
**Key themes:** the ritae as faith-powered magic distinct from thaumaturgy;
Sabbat hypocrisy as a deliberate, acknowledged feature of the Sect; internal
factionalism as the Sabbat's primary form of "plural, unreliable" politics
(paralleling this library's existing "unreliable, plural history" theme,
here expressed through faction rather than narrator).
**Key terms/entities: The Telyav** — this section reveals that some
Tremere antitribu never received the Betrayer's Sigil: a splinter lineage
of Telyavelic Tremere faked their deaths to escape the parent clan
centuries ago, and their distinct vitae (native Presence instead of
Dominate) let them pass as ordinary Tremere antitribu even now. **This
directly complicates `v20-core`'s "Telyavic Tremere" bloodline entry**,
which states that bloodline's remnant survivors were "reported destroyed
by the 16th century" — this book's antitribu are the same lineage,
alive and hiding in the Sabbat in the modern nights, and the Chapter Four
Necromancy section and this book's own fiction prelude both return to
this same thread (see Cross-cutting themes).
**Notable claims/data points:** Black Cathedral locations; the specific
wording of the Sabbat Oath and the Renunciation oath are given in full.

## Chapter Three: The Movement (pp. 54-71)
**Summary:** Anarch occultism, organized into "Four Waves"/schools rather
than a single tradition. **Old Skool** (multicultural folk-magic
traditionalists, named as an insult by "Punk Tremere" in the 1970s and
reclaimed). **New Age** (West Coast, Blavatsky/Gurdjieff/Vivekananda-
descended, popular with Malkavians and Toreador). **Punk Sorcery** (born
from 1970s-80s chaos magic; personalized, never-repeated rituals; hostile
to Tremere-style codification). **Hacktivist Thaumaturgy** (the newest
school, founded when a Washington D.C. Tremere coterie defected en masse
to the L.A. Free States in the early 2000s, stealing the Technomancy
research database; the defectors call themselves the "Digital Draculas,"
funded by "The Red Question," and disseminate rituals via shareware and a
Fangbook social-media ritual; the fate of the path's original inventor,
Masika St. John, is left an open mystery — "recalled to Vienna" being a
euphemism the Tremere use for Final Death). Named all-sorcerer Anarch
coteries: the Maenads (radical feminist Old Skoolers worshipping the
Malkavian Antediluvian as Dionysus), the Unity Brethren (New Age gestalt-
intelligence cult), Mary's Kids (Punk Sorcery cultists claiming to have
bound "Bloody Mary"). Discusses the uneasy Convention-of-Thorns status of
Anarch magicians within Camarilla domains ("chameleons") and the largely
cash/task-based economics of Anarch magical services.
**Key themes:** eclecticism and improvisation as the Anarch approach to
magic, mirroring the Movement's general anti-hierarchy ethos; rivalry
between the four schools mirrors (in miniature) the wider setting's
"unreliable, plural" factionalism.
**Key terms/entities:** Digital Draculas, Masika St. John, The Red Question,
Fangbook; Maenads, Unity Brethren, Mary's Kids.

## Chapter Four: The Independents (pp. 72-97)
**Summary:** Three unaligned clans' sorceries. **Assamite Sorcery** (Dur-
An-Ki, "Master of Heaven and Earth," a covenant-based Sumerian/Mesopotamian
magic worked by ashipu who climb a visionary "Ladder of Heaven" in search
of a way to break the Tremere-laid blood curse; the drug kalif — vitae-fed
hashish — opens the way; the three Assamite castes are Warriors, Viziers,
and Sorcerers, with rising Warrior impatience at centuries of Sorcerer
"progress" that hasn't broken the curse). **Setite Sorcery** (explicitly a
family of practices, not one system, each tied to a regional "mask of Set":
Akhu/Egyptian — Lector Priests who desecrate corpses to spite Osiris;
Wanga/Caribbean Voudoun, practiced heavily by the schismatic Serpents of
the Light; Sadhana/Hindu, practiced by the Daitya bloodline devoted to
Shiva; Nahuallotl/Mesoamerican, practiced by the Tlacique bloodline —
described here as displaying Protean rather than Serpentis, raising
unresolved doubts about their true descent. Setite factions detailed:
the fundamentalist Cohort of Wepwawet, the Scandinavian Setites, the Cult
of Taweret, the all-female Sisterhood of Sekhmet, and the Cult of Typhon
Trismegistus. A named Tremere subplot: Sylvia Kilver, regent of the Cairo
chantry and a member of the secret Tremere order the "Eye of the Serpent,"
is trading away Tremere secrets to a Setite sorceress named Kahina to
learn Akhu.) **The Giovanni and Necromancy** (traces Necromancy from
ancestor-worship through the Cappadocian founder's quest to answer "what
comes after death," to the Giovanni's post-Endless-Night ambition to
become gods by collecting 100 million souls; surveys regional Necromancy
variants — Western/Giovanni, Ghiberti/West African, Pisanob/Aztec,
Nagaraja, Voudoun, della Passaglia/Eastern — each with different wraith
"aspects" and compatibility penalties; describes Giovanni research into a
lost Telyavelic Tremere path called **Sielanic Thaumaturgy**, including a
partial recreation of its "Army of Souls" power as a new Necromancy
ritual — a third and independent appearance of the Telyavelic Tremere
thread in this book, this time from the Giovanni's outside perspective).
**Key themes:** every Independent clan's sorcery is framed as older than,
and structurally different from, Tremere Thaumaturgy — the book
consistently uses "the Tremere think this is inferior, but it works
anyway" as a rhetorical device across factions.
**Key terms/entities:** Dur-An-Ki, Ladder of Heaven, ashipu, kalif; Akhu,
Wanga, Sadhana, Nahuallotl; Sylvia Kilver, Eye of the Serpent, Kahina;
Sielanic Thaumaturgy, Path of the Shadow World, Army of Souls.
**Notable claims/data points:** the near-genocide of the Tlacique
bloodline by the Camarilla and Sabbat shortly after their New World arrival
is stated as fact, with the bloodline's true nature left deliberately
ambiguous (no elders survive to confirm whether they're truly Setite-
descended or a "dangerously ambitious Gangrel bloodline" in disguise).

## Chapter Five: The Unusual (pp. 98-111)
**Summary:** The Inconnu and the Tal'Mahe'Ra. **The Inconnu** are presented
with four internal philosophies on reaching Golconda — Seekers (faith-based),
Purifiers (scientific/biological), Immaculate (embrace vampiric nature
fully, often via Paths of Enlightenment), and the Haunted (treat the Beast
as a literal separate possessing entity). Organizational roles: Monitor
(surveillance/information-gathering), Steward (record-keeper/safehouse,
holding deliberately-fragmented thaumaturgical lore so no single loss is
total), and Equite (traveling scout/recruiter). A secret Council of Twelve
leads the society; one member is said to be a Setite ritualist who has
mastered the entire library. The Inconnu communicate via an unlearnable,
unwritable enchanted language called kashaph. **The Tal'Mahe'Ra** ("True
Black Hand") are a death-cult/historian society based in the hidden
Shadowlands city of Enoch, ruled from beneath by four sleeping, unidentified
entities called the Aralu (Nergal, Ninmug, Loz, Al-Marhi) whose aura warps
and empowers magic unpredictably; the Rafastio revenant family (also
described in `secrets-of-thaumaturgy-revised`'s appendix as a masterless,
Black-Hand-linked hedge-witch bloodline) serve as caretakers of the tombs
beneath Enoch, immune to the Aralu's mind-fraying influence. States that
the **Nagaraja** bloodline originated from Tal'Mahe'Ra experiments mixing
vampiric blood with the Aralu's aura in pursuit of immortality, producing
cannibalistic vampires who are now the Sect's primary necromancers. Closes
with "Created and Changed Creatures": the **Montmartre Pact of 1489**
(the Tremere's formal Camarilla-admission treaty, forcing them to free
enslaved Gargoyles and cease creating new ones, under threat of being cast
out and hunted by Gangrel and Nosferatu even by their own Clan) and the
ritual origin of **Blood Brothers** (a Tzimisce/Tremere-antitribu Sabbat
collaboration, explicitly a refined "lesson learned" successor to the
Gargoyle-creation ritual, this time fully infertile).
**Key themes:** secrecy and fragmentation as institutional survival
strategies (the Inconnu's deliberately-split library, the Tal'Mahe'Ra's
hidden city) — the same "unreliable, plural" pattern the wider library
already tracks, here institutionalized as policy rather than emerging from
unreliable narration.
**Key terms/entities:** Seekers, Purifiers, Immaculate, the Haunted;
Council of Twelve, kashaph; Tal'Mahe'Ra, Enoch, the Aralu, Nagaraja
origin; Montmartre Pact (1489), Meerlinda and Calderon; Blood Brothers.
**Notable claims/data points:** names Meerlinda and Calderon as the
Tremere leaders who negotiated the Montmartre Pact — Meerlinda recurs
later in this book (Chapter Six) as the Tremere who allegedly coined the
name "Josians" for the Camarilla's anti-infernalist archon order in 1764,
tying two separate chapters' worth of Tremere political history to the
same named elder.

## Chapter Six: The Damned (pp. 112-129)
**Summary:** Infernalism. Distinguishes demonologist (theoretical
researcher), thrall (anyone who's made any bargain, however small), and
diabolist (an active summoner/worshipper) as escalating categories, all
sharing the premise that no infernal bargain can ever be undone. Names
five Archdukes of Hell (Belial the Warlord, Abbadon the Devourer/Diseased,
Dagon/Vassago/Leviathan/Behemoth, Asmodai/Asmodeus, Azrael the Slayer) plus
a long list of lesser named demonic entities. Describes the mechanics of
summoning (a demon's Celestial name vs. its far more dangerous True Name),
the three tiers of infernal bargains (bartered service, one-time
Empowerment, and permanent foedus/True Pact), and lists notable
demonological texts (the Gospel of the Consoler/Menahem; the Book of
Thoth; Dreams of Angra Mainyu, journal of the first High Priest of Ba'al,
revered by the Baali; and a heretical version of the Book of Tobit
annotated by **Troile and her lover Moloch** — connecting directly to this
library's central, permanently-contested Carthage disagreement, since this
book treats Troile as historically real and personally involved with
Carthage-era infernal lore). Gives the **Baali origin story**: a mystery
cult near ancient Ashur/Jerusalem sacrificed victims into a well; a
vampire drove tormented priests into the well with them, and three
emerged as the first Baali, later founding the hidden fortress-city of
**Chorazin** under the Methuselah Shaitan. Introduces the **Veneficti**
(the Circle of Red, a once-independent infernalist mage coven enslaved by
the Baali after a losing war, now lich-like servant-scholars) and the lost
demonological codex the *Voleurs d'Enfer*. Covers the **Sabbat Inquisition**
in detail — its authority under the Code of Milan, its rivalry with the
Black Hand, its list of formally heretical Paths (Evil Revelations,
Typhon, Sutekh, Lilith, Blood), heretical texts (the Cainite Heresy's
*Book of Shining Blood*, the *Gospel of Laodice*), trial procedure, and the
Iron Reliquary badge of office — and **the Josians**, a much older
Camarilla anti-infernalist order whose founding is traced back to Carthage
itself: the Malkavian seer **Tryphosa** had visions of Baali corruption in
Carthage and convinced Ventrue, Malkavian, and Toreador elders to destroy
the city specifically to root out demon-worship, a coterie that later
(1764) organized into the formal Josian archons at the Tremere Meerlinda's
suggestion.
**Key themes:** demons as agents pursuing their own inscrutable ends
rather than servants — even a successful bargain leaves the demon in
control; the Inquisition and the Josians as structural mirrors of each
other (Sabbat vs. Camarilla anti-infernalist police, each entangled in the
politics of the sect they're meant to keep pure).
**Key terms/entities:** the five Archdukes of Hell; foedus; Baali/Shaitan/
Chorazin; Veneficti/Circle of Red; Sabbat Inquisition, Iron Reliquary; the
Josians, Tryphosa.
**Notable claims/data points:** **Tryphosa's Carthage-destroyed-to-purge-
Baali-corruption account is a new, seventh angle on this library's central,
permanently-unresolved Carthage disagreement** (alongside the Brujah-
utopia, Ventrue-"delenda est," Zamra-letters, Nosferatu-skeptical, classic-
Brujah, and classic-Toreador accounts already tracked in `library_index.md`)
— it gives Carthage's destruction a specifically anti-infernal religious
motive that none of the other accounts mention, framed matter-of-factly by
this book's Ventrue/Malkavian/Toreador-descended narrative voice rather
than contested by another source within this chapter.

## Chapter Seven: Secrets of the Blood (pp. 130-172)
**Summary:** A "revisits a wealth of magical practices from prior editions"
chapter — largely a V20-updated reprint/consolidation of classic-era
Thaumaturgy, Koldunic Sorcery, Setite Sorcery, and Dark Thaumaturgy paths
and rituals (including Mastery of the Mortal Shell and the Path of the
Focused Mind, both close V20 updates of material also found in
`secrets-of-thaumaturgy-revised`, here re-attributed to a named inventor,
"Melvin Liebknecht"). Opens with a unified theoretical framework — the
four Major Principles (Blood, Will, Knowledge, Identity) and the
Sympathy/Contagion principles of sympathetic magic — explicitly presented
as underlying *every* tradition covered in the book, not just Tremere
Thaumaturgy, with a difficulty-modifier table for how strong a sympathetic
link needs to be. This is mechanically dense reference material; query the
chunk database directly for specific path/ritual text (Path of the
Levinbolt, Path of Spirit Manipulation, Koldunic Sorcery Ways, Setite
Sorcery rituals across Akhu/Sadhana/Wanga, and Dark Thaumaturgy paths
including Fires of the Inferno and the Path of Phobos all appear here).
**Key themes:** this chapter functions as the book's unifying coda —
having spent six chapters treating each faction's magic as structurally
distinct, it closes by showing all of them share the same four Principles
underneath, mechanically validating the book's implicit argument that
blood magic is one phenomenon wearing many cultural masks.
**Key terms/entities:** the four Major Principles; Sympathy/Contagion
difficulty-modifier chart; Melvin Liebknecht (credited inventor of the
Path of the Focused Mind in this version).

## Cross-cutting themes
- **The Telyavelic Tremere as a three-chapter connecting thread**: this
  book's fiction prelude (a captured Telyavelic antitribu, Jurgin Shaulis),
  Chapter Two (the bloodline's full reveal — undetectably passing as
  ordinary Tremere antitribu in the Sabbat), and Chapter Four (the Giovanni
  researching the bloodline's lost "Sielanic Thaumaturgy") all independently
  return to the same lineage. Taken together, they directly complicate
  `v20-core`'s claim that the Telyavic Tremere bloodline's remnants were
  "reported destroyed by the 16th century" — this book's position is that
  a hidden branch survived into the modern nights specifically by
  concealing itself within the Sabbat's own Tremere-antitribu population.
  Any question about the Telyavic/Telyavelic Tremere's fate should note
  both accounts rather than treating either alone as settled.
- **Carthage gets a new, distinct account**: Chapter Six's Josian origin
  story gives Carthage's destruction an anti-infernal religious motive
  (purging Baali corruption) driven by the Malkavian seer Tryphosa — see
  that chapter's "Notable claims" for how this sits alongside the other six
  Carthage accounts already tracked in this library's `library_index.md`.
- **This book explicitly departs from strict continuity**: the "Thaumaturgy
  and Metaplot" sidebar (p.15) states outright that the book "moves around
  the metaplot... to explore topics most germane to a discussion of blood
  magic" and that some details "are changed or omitted here" intentionally,
  citing the Tremere antitribu and the True Black Hand's continued
  existence as examples. Treat this book's more surprising claims as this
  book's own take rather than assuming automatic consistency with other
  V20 sourcebooks in this library — though where its claims *do* connect
  cleanly to existing library threads (Carthage, the Gargoyle revolt/
  Montmartre Pact, the Rafastio revenant family), they're worth citing
  as additional data points rather than contradictions.
- **"The cost of power" as the book's explicit unifying theme**: stated
  directly in the Introduction and enacted structurally — every chapter
  shows its faction's sorcery extracting some toll (Tremere social
  isolation and clan servitude, Sabbat priests' faith-dependency, Anarch
  magicians' social marginalization even among their own kind, Assamite
  Sorcerers' centuries of unrewarded sacrifice, Inconnu secrecy-unto-death,
  infernal bargains that can never be undone).
