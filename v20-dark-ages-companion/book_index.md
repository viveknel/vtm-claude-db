# V20 Dark Ages Companion — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
V20 Dark Ages Companion (White Wolf/Onyx Path, 2017) is a 133-page
supplement to Vampire: The Dark Ages 20th Anniversary Edition. Rather
than expanding the core rulebook's cosmology, it profiles six specific
domains scattered across the 1242 CE Dark Medieval world — Rome, Bath,
Bjarkarey, Constantinople, Mogadishu, and Mangaluru — each with named
Cainite rulers and rivals, a signature political conflict, a folkloric
monster, and one or two short pieces of "Clan Apocrypha" (in-character
fiction spotlighting a specific Clan's mysteries within that domain).
The book closes with two mechanically-focused chapters: Storyteller
rules for building and running a domain using pooled Backgrounds
(Chapter Seven), and period-accurate weapons, armor, and a streamlined
combat system (Chapter Eight). Every domain chapter and both mechanical
chapters end with a Clan Apocrypha vignette, so the book functions as
much as a collection of 13 short in-character stories (one or two per
Clan) as it does a geography supplement. As the Introduction states
explicitly, each domain has a distinct theme and mood: Rome (fallen
greatness/grandeur), Bath (blood loyalty to a fading ideal/desperation),
Bjarkarey (savagery/isolation), Constantinople (faith/hopeful
expectation), Mogadishu (shadow war/sweltering tension), and Mangaluru
(resolve/amor fati). This bundle's chunk database preserves page
numbers as printed in the book (not PDF page indices).

## Front Matter (pp. 1-5)
Credits, dedication (to Phil Hartman), table of contents. No lore content.

## Introduction (pp. 6-8)
**Summary:** Frames the book's purpose (a Storyteller resource for
setting chronicles in any of the six profiled domains, or for pulling
elements from each to build an original one) and states each domain's
theme and mood in a short paragraph apiece (see Overview above). "How
to Use This Book" then previews each chapter's setting and which Clans
receive Apocrypha within it, functioning as a roadmap for the rest of
the book.
**Key themes:** domains as distinct dramatic "moods," modularity
(Storytellers are explicitly invited to mix and match elements rather
than treat any domain as canon-locked).
**Key terms/entities:** the six domains and their themes (p.6-7).

## Chapter One: The Domain of Rome (pp. 9-24)
**Summary:** Rome is ruled by the returned Methuselah **Camilla**
(Fifth Generation Ventrue, "Pontiff of Sin"/self-styled Antipope), who
presides over a decadent Road of Sin cult from a black-bishop-robed
court while First Senator **Cretheus** (Fifth Generation Ventrue,
Mithras' childe and the last public member of the Ignoti/Inconnu)
secretly works to expose Rome's vampires to Camilla's vice so the
Church's inevitable retaliation will "purify" survivors into Golconda
(a Saulot-inspired scheme laid out in the epistolary "Our Fallen State"
and "To Dash Them Against the Stones" sections, addressed by Cretheus
to his absent sire Mithras). Other named Cainites: **Maginardo** (Tenth
Generation Nosferatu Aedile, master of Rome's undercity), **Otto the
Pretender** (Seventh Generation Malkavian who genuinely believes
himself the Holy Roman Emperor, secretly a puppet of Camilla and
Cretheus), the **Bride of Night** (Fifth Generation Lasombra emissary
from the Castle of Shadows, of unclear loyalty), and **Thrax the Rebel**
(Seventh Generation Brujah, made praetor urbanus as a joke, now
sincerely trying to reform the city and lead her "Katharoi" followers).
Political structure: Prince (Camilla), First Senator, Pontifex,
Praetors (urbanus/peregrinus), Aedile, and Patricians governing
Rome's Thirteen Regions, each administered by a specific Clan (Ventrue,
Brujah, Followers of Set, Gangrel, Lasombra, Nosferatu, Tzimisce,
Toreador, Cappadocians, Ravnos, Assamites, Malkavians, and a
region held in trust for the Salubri "and no other"). The chapter's
two Apocrypha are **Brujah Apocrypha: The Age of Purification** (an
epistolary account of Thrax's "Katharoi"/Path of the Pure movement — a
Gnostic-dualist offshoot of the Road of Heaven opposed to the
Cainite Heresy's "Rex Mundi" theology, with a full Hierarchy of Sins;
a sidebar notes the Path historically degenerates into a hedonistic
"Path of Cathari" by later centuries) and **Nosferatu Apocrypha: The
Elder Tunnels** (a letter from an unnamed Cainite to Maginardo
describing "bracchia" — carved stone/bone/wood 3-D tunnel maps — and
surveying who else claims the vast Elder Tunnel network: Cappadocian
catacombs, Fae mounds, ghosts, Methuselah-built Labyrinthine Havens,
Lupine "Spiral Clan" mazes, surviving mystery cults, and unexplained
"Orphic Beasts").
**Key themes:** decline dressed as continuity (Rome's mortal and
Cainite institutions alike are hollow survivals of Imperial forms),
self-justifying schemes for redemption through vice, the Ignoti/Inconnu
as a body already fragmenting toward Golconda-seeking mysticism.
**Key terms/entities:** Camilla (p.9-10), Cretheus/Ignoti (p.10, p.15),
Maginardo/Elder Tunnels (p.10, p.21-23), Otto the Pretender (p.10-11,
p.15), the Bride of Night (p.10), Thrax/Katharoi/Path of the Pure
(p.10, p.18-20), the Thirteen Regions (p.12-13), bracchia (p.23).

## Chapter Two: The Domain of Bath (pp. 25-42)
**Summary:** Bath (Aquae Sulis) is a walled, avowedly secret-free
"domain of truths" ruled by Consul **Arcadius** (Sixth Generation
Ventrue), grandchilde of the Methuselah **Mithras** (Fourth Generation,
Prince of London and the Baronies of Avalon) and Pater of Bath's
Mithraic faith. The chapter traces Bath's history from the pre-Roman
goddess Sul through Roman conquest and centuries of Anglo-Saxon and
Norman rule, then profiles Arcadius' inner circle: **Erebus** (Ninth
Generation Cappadocian adviser, secretly loyal to the Cappadocian
Harbingers faction and working to destabilize the domain), **Gwenllian
ferch Arwyn** (Seventh Generation Brujah peacekeeper), **Judikael**
(Tenth Generation Malkavian Heliodromus and Arcadius' former mortal
enemy turned confidant), **Evnissyen the Bloodied** (Tenth Generation
Lhiannan Promethean, tolerated despite plotting against Mithraism),
and **Bethany of Ely** (Eighth Generation Salubri Healer who **led the
Cainite Heresy's believers in Ely before Tremere persecution drove
her to fall on Mithras' mercy**, now sheltering in Bath and drawing
other Salubri refugees). Cainite factions: the ruling Mithraists,
the Prometheans (Evnissyen's faction), the Winchester Tremere
(courting access to Bath's Salubri refugees), the unaffiliated
Pilgrims, and the Cappadocian Harbingers (Erebus' faction, secretly
working toward apocalyptic chaos). The **Mithraic Mysteries** section
details the full cult hierarchy (Corux → Nymphus → Miles → Leo →
Perses → Heliodromus → Pater) and Unconquered Praetorian Guard
initiation. The **Black Dog** (a folkloric death-omen hound) is this
chapter's monster. Apocrypha: **Cappadocian Apocrypha: Death Cults**
(the Harbingers vs. the Necromancers vs. the "Pathological" faction —
the Florence-based Fratres Plaga cult of Holiman/Gentilman/Carnifex,
who study plague as theology, natural philosophy, and Harbinger-adjacent
apocalypticism respectively) and **Ventrue Apocrypha: The Importance of
Lineage** (a catalog of Fourth Generation Methuselah bloodlines — the
Lines of Alexander, Antonius, Arakur, Mithras, Nefer-meri-Isis, Orthia,
and Tinia — each with a short Optional System mechanic reflecting the
ancestor's signature trait).
**Key themes:** absolute loyalty as fragile stability (Bath's peace
depends entirely on faith in Mithras/Arcadius, which multiple factions
are quietly undermining), refuge that breeds its own instability (the
same sanctuary drawing Salubri and pilgrims also strains Bath's
resources toward collapse), Ventrue bloodline pride as both social
currency and mechanical trait.
**Key terms/entities:** Arcadius (p.26, p.29), Mithras/Baronies of
Avalon (p.26, p.33-35), Bethany of Ely/Cainite Heresy in Ely (p.29-30),
Erebus/Harbingers (p.29, p.32), Mithraic hierarchy (p.34-35), Black
Dog (p.35), Fratres Plaga (p.38-39), Fourth Generation Lines (p.40-41).

## Chapter Three: The Domain of Bjarkarey (pp. 43-56)
**Summary:** Bjarkarey is a small, isolated Norwegian island domain
ruled by **Queen Valborg Crooked-Eye** (Eighth Generation Gangrel), an
unrepentant pagan reaver who conquered the island generations after
Norway's forced Christianization and now runs it as a raiding
stronghold devoted to Thor and the Æsir, alternating seasonal Viking
raids (often disguised as Crusading) with absentee rule left to her
lieutenants. Her hirð (a Gangrel warband tradition with kertilsveinr/
hirðmen/skutilsveinr ranks, bound by the blood-sacrifice blót ritual)
includes **Kindling-Ásgeirr** (Eleventh Generation Toreador diplomat),
**Mikkel** (Eleventh Generation Gangrel, feared for berserk rages),
and **Kearte** (Ninth Generation Brujah, secretly disloyal and
sympathetic to the suffering kine). Also present: **Guðrunn** (Fifth
Generation Cappadocian völva who secretly sabotages Valborg for her
own amusement), **Ormr** (Eighth Generation Nosferatu who secretly
leads Bjarkarey's hidden Cainite church), and **Israel** (Tenth
Generation Follower of Set posing as an archangel and offering
vitae-laced "communion"). External rivals include the Setite
"Hlaðirjarl" Ráðúlfr and the Christian-vs-pagan Gangrel factional
split between the "Monstrous Few" and "Honorborn." The **Fossegrim**
(a fiddle-playing water spirit born from a Tzimisce koldun's
experiment, and consequently hostile to all Tzimisce) is this
chapter's monster. Apocrypha: **Gangrel Apocrypha: A Wanderer's
Nocturne** (a boastful, blackly comic letter from the knight-errant
Sir Thibault d'Armagnac recounting a dragon-slaying and casual
atrocities across Central Europe) and **Malkavian Apocrypha: A Soul
to Bleed** (cryptic prophetic letters from a sire posing as "Saint
Thomas Aquinas" to a childe hunting a corrupt bishop, framing the
chapter's discussion of Malkavian madness as either "the Bent" —
Seers whose insight and sin-affinity are indistinguishable — or "the
Broken" — prophets and heresiarchs feared and hunted by the Church).
**Key themes:** isolation preserving what the wider world has already
abandoned (Bjarkarey is one of the last places the old Norse gods are
openly worshipped), rule sustained more by absence than presence
(Valborg's domain runs largely without her), Malkavian madness framed
through period religious categories (sin/sanctity) rather than clinical
illness.
**Key terms/entities:** Valborg Crooked-Eye (p.43-44), the Gangrel hirð
and blót (p.48), Guðrunn (p.44, p.47), Ormr/hidden Cainite church
(p.44), Israel (p.44), Fossegrim (p.48-49), the Bent/the Broken
(p.54-55).

## Chapter Four: The Domain of Constantinople (pp. 57-74)
**Summary:** Forty years after the Fourth Crusade, Latin Prince
**Alfonzo** (Seventh Generation Lasombra) rules a paranoid, contracting
Constantinople under the nominally Christian but bankrupt Latin Empire
(Emperor Baldwin II). The chapter's core myth is the fall of **"the
Dream"** — the lost golden age when three Methuselahs (**Michael the
Patriarch/Archangel**, a Toreador; **Antonius**, a Ventrue; and
**the Dracon**, a Tzimisce — this last figure also appears in
`v20-dark-ages`'s Appendix B, where the Obertus family is described as
"fostered by the Methuselah Dracon" and "scattered" after
Constantinople's fall, matching this chapter's account) ruled the city
as a Cainite-Kine utopia before Michael's madness and Final Death, the
Dracon's disappearance, and the 1204 sack unraveled it. Survivors
include **Malachite** ("the Rock of Constantinople," Seventh
Generation Nosferatu, secretly rebuilding toward a new Dream from the
undercity), **Bishop Paul Bathalos** (Fifth Generation Toreador,
leader of the **Nephilim** bloodline/cult of Michaelite "Martyrs" on
the **Path of the Archangel**, with a full Hierarchy of Sins),
**Josselin the Blessed** (Tenth Generation Ventrue running a
self-styled blood-saint cult), **Gabriella of Genoa** (Eighth
Generation Lasombra rival plotting to unseat Alfonzo and secretly
selling Emperor Baldwin's son Philip to the Lasombra Amici Noctis —
laid out across an epistolary "Byzantine Intrigue" sequence), and
**Scholarch Elaiodora Sophida** (Ninth Generation Tzimisce, last
Obertus loyalist, building a scholarly "Akademia" with her Salubri
lover **Rakhama**, who is secretly searching for the **Skull of
Saulot** rumored to be held by the Cappadocian Harbingers). The
**Kallikantzaros** (a shapeshifting, worship-feeding goblin) is this
chapter's monster. Apocrypha: **Lasombra Apocrypha: Shadow Play**
(the Byzantine Intrigue letters), **Toreador Apocrypha: Lost in
Reverie** (the Nephilim's Path of the Archangel plus a drug-and-vitae
"Constantine Reverie" experience), and **Tzimisce Apocrypha: The
Akademia** (Elaiodora, Rakhama, and three named "Points of Interest"
for further exploration — Ghosts of Byzantium, Abyssal Shrines
tied to a pre-Constantine Lasombra cult of Aphrodite, and the
demon-haunted House of Broken Lamps).
**Key themes:** a fallen utopia as active political ideology (nearly
every faction claims to be restoring "the Dream," meaning something
different by it), religion as a vacuum opportunistic Cainites fill
(Orthodox/Catholic schism, the Nephilim, Josselin's cult), the direct
tie between this chapter and `v20-dark-ages`'s brief Dracon/Obertus
reference — this is the library's fullest account of that Methuselah
and the Trinity's fall.
**Key terms/entities:** Alfonzo (p.58), Michael/the Dream/Antonius/the
Dracon (p.58-59), Malachite (p.58, p.63-64), Nephilim/Path of the
Archangel (p.69-70), Gabriella/Byzantine Intrigue/Philip (p.61-62,
p.67-69), Elaiodora/Rakhama/Skull of Saulot (p.58, p.72-73),
Kallikantzaros (p.66).

## Chapter Five: The Domain of Mogadishu (pp. 75-90)
**Summary:** The "White Pearl" twin cities of Hamar and Shangani are
ruled by **Farnaza** ("the Na'ib," Seventh Generation Assamite), who
displaced the matriarchal Ramanga (Clan descended from a bargain with
an Abyssal entity called **the Faceless**, not from Caine) using
Islam's political momentum — a conversion so successful that Farnaza,
herself privately Zoroastrian, now cannot safely abandon the Muslim
faith she used as a tool. Her rival **Mazigha** (Sixth Generation
Ramanga) works to restore Ramanga rule via the matriarchal cult of
**Arawelo**, House Adeer (a Muslim mortal proxy family), and a rival
"first" masjid — the two competing mosque-building projects (Assamite
Fakr ad-Din vs. Ramanga Arba'a Rukun) generate a citywide **"miasma"**
that inflicts an Obsession derangement on anyone who fails an extended
Road roll. **Aphobis** (Tenth Generation Follower of Set) quietly
foments unrest between both factions and the Toreador/Lasombra
newcomers Fatimah al-Lam'a and Atilia Priscian. The ancient, city-
founding Laibon **Sarapion** ("of the line of Loz") lurks beneath the
city; a subplot follows **Jakuta**, an amnesiac elder diablerist
(claiming past identities including Changó/Xangô/Shango) whom only
the Na'ib can safely approach. Other threads: the Ravnos information
broker Amaria (who also appears as Sridhar's sire in the Mangaluru
chapter, running a parallel slave-trade operation), the pirate
"Liberty Fleet" of disenfranchised neonates, and a lone Wan Kuei (Bai
Qi) largely ignored by local Cainites. Apocrypha: **Assamite Apocrypha:
The Followers of Zarathustra** (the Zoroastrian Assamite sect —
already noted only in passing in `v20-lore-clans`' religion survey —
developed here into a full dual **Road of Zarathustra** system: every
Zoroastrian starts on the **Road of Angra Mainyu** and may switch once,
penalty-free, to the **Road of Ahura Mazda**; includes Zarathustra/
Tehmina backstory and five Sadhana-style rituals) and **Ramanga
Apocrypha: Beholden to Darkness** (the Faceless entity and the
**Aizina** Discipline — Compelling Darkness, Obscurity of Shadows,
Shadow Communion, Weight of Shadows, Whispered Passions — a
domain-scale mind-control power distinct from Obtenebration).
**Key themes:** conversion as double-edged political weapon (Islam
serves and then constrains the Na'ib), competing "authenticity" claims
over sacred space (the twin-masjid miasma as literalized political
rivalry), an African vampire history independent of and prior to
Caine's curse (the Ramanga's separate Faceless-based origin, echoing
`clanbook-nosferatu-revised`'s and `clanbook-followers-of-set-revised`'s
non-European bloodline material elsewhere in the library).
**Key terms/entities:** Farnaza/the Na'ib (p.75-76), Mazigha/Arawelo
cult (p.75-76, p.81), Aphobis (p.76, p.79-80), Sarapion (p.76, p.82),
Jakuta (p.78-79), the miasma/twin masjids (p.78), Road of
Zarathustra (p.83-84), the Faceless/Aizina (p.86-89).

## Chapter Six: The Domain of Mangaluru (pp. 91-104)
**Summary:** On India's Malabar coast, the spice-trade port of
Mangaluru is the library's only domain where the Salubri remain an
openly ruling, undivided power. A triumvirate of **Salubri** (led by
**Rodriel**, Sixth Generation Healer and "Prince" in all but name, a
Saint Thomas Christian who walks the Path of Kings), **Danava**
(blood-sorcerer "gods," internally split between **deva** and
**asura** factions, led by the weakening **Kiaan**, Seventh
Generation), and **Ravnos** (organized by **jati**/lineage rather than
mortal caste) has ruled for centuries via the shared **Sadhana**
blood-magic tradition, demanding a "tribute" mortal sacrifice from
every visiting Cainite. The secretive, necromancer **Nagaraja**
(narrated by Malsang) operate as tolerated outsiders protecting the
apocalypse-city of Enoch. Encroaching **Tremere** (led by Amichai bani
Tremere, Ninth Generation) exploit the Danava schism and ally with the
Ravnos and asuras, while **Chaitra** (Tenth Generation Ravnos,
blood-bound to Rodriel) grows isolated for her loyalty. **Pabrakhar**
(Tenth Generation Salubri Watcher) is one of the few active members of
that rare caste. A letter from **Bethany** (the Bath chapter's
Cainite-Heresy-founder-turned-Mithraist) to her exiled Warrior friend
Matthias frames the **Salubri Apocrypha: The Clan of Three Ways**
section, which details the **Blooding of Samiel** ritual (inducting
Warrior Caste "Noddists"/Via Dolorosa adherents) and two new Merits
(Tracker's Mark, Blooded by the Code). **Ravnos Apocrypha: Caste and
Karma** (framed by a letter from Amaria — the Mogadishu chapter's
Ravnos information broker — to her childe Sridhar) catalogs named jati
(Alexandrites, the offshoot Phaedymites, Bashirites/Lambs of Bashir,
Sybarites, Yoryari) and develops the **Sadhana** blood magic in full:
Blood Nectar (grants a recipient temporary use of a Discipline) and
the five-level **Power of Karma** path (Threads of the Past, Weave of
the Future, Certain Fate, Past Lives, Master of Samsara). The
**Pishacha** (flesh-eating, disease-spreading vampiric cousins,
mechanically Thirteenth Generation) is this chapter's monster.
**Key themes:** a domain where the modern books' "settled" clan
hierarchy doesn't apply (Salubri here are rulers, not a hunted remnant
— directly extending the library's existing "Salubri as Clan vs.
bloodline" timeline-contingency theme with a third, geographically
distinct data point), intra-Clan schism as the real threat (Danava
deva/asura split, not external conquest, is what lets Tremere in),
sacred reciprocity (the tribute-sacrifice system) as the basis of
Cainite-kine coexistence rather than predation.
**Key terms/entities:** Rodriel (p.94), Kiaan/deva-asura split (p.94,
p.96), Nagaraja/Enoch (p.92-93, p.96), Bethany's letter (p.99),
Blooding of Samiel/Noddists (p.99-100), Ravnos jati (p.100-101),
Sadhana/Power of Karma (p.101-103), Pishacha (p.97-98).

## Chapter Seven: Building a Domain (pp. 105-118)
**Summary:** A Storyteller-facing systems chapter expanding V20 Dark
Ages' pooled-Background domain rules into a full feudal toolkit:
Liege/Vassal Background-lending mechanics (including a new **Vassal**
Merit and "phantom" Background dots for Cainite or martial vassals who
offer reputation instead of resources), inheritance customs (including
a sidebar on Cainite absolute-inheritance-by-Blood-age contrasted with
mortal primogeniture), and a detailed **Anatomy of a Domain** breaking
the Domain Background into Area, Defense, Prosperity, and Secret Ways
aspects, plus a Herd/Populace/Territory table and a new **Settlement**
Merit (Hamlet through Great City). "Peopling the Domain" catalogs
Ally/Retainer archetypes (Armigers, Courtiers, Foresters, Scholars)
and a new **Servants** Background with a period "Types of Servants"
glossary (Administrators, Cottagers, Guards, Hunters, Kitchen Staff,
Minor Clergy, Serfs and Slaves). "Domain Events" provides ten
random-event templates (Curse, Disaster, Faithful One, Famine,
Invasion, Itinerant Court, Heresy, Miracle, Outlaws, Pilgrims) with
mechanical effects on domain Backgrounds. Chapter closes with
**Tremere Apocrypha: The House Militant** (an in-character Hermetic
"catechism" on House Tremere's origin as the Order of Hermes' military
faction, followed by four new Thaumaturgical rituals: Tame the
Maddening Flame, Banning Rituals [ward-like protections against
specific supernatural power categories], Wield the Spear of Damnation,
and Ravening Walls of Ceoris).
**Key themes:** domain as an extension of character (Backgrounds made
concrete and transferable), medieval feudal logic applied literally to
vampire hierarchy, Tremere self-justification through military
metaphor (loyalty-as-survival). This chapter is mechanically dense —
query the chunk database directly for specific Background costs,
Merit ratings, or ritual system text rather than relying on this
summary.
**Key terms/entities:** Vassal Merit (p.106), Domain aspects
(Area/Defense/Prosperity/Secret Ways, p.108-109), Settlement Merit
(p.110), Servants Background (p.112-113), Domain Events (p.113-115),
House Militant/Banning Rituals (p.115-117).

## Chapter Eight: Art of the Battlefield (pp. 119-131)
**Summary:** A mechanical chapter grounding V20 Dark Ages' generic
medieval arms and armor in period-accurate 13th-century specifics —
extensive weapon tables (knives/swords, bludgeons/cleavers, pole
weapons, ranged weapons) with sidebars on historical dual-wielding and
the legal obligation (per England's 1181 Assize of Arms) for
freemen to own specific arms. Armor is broken into Torso, Limb, and
Helmet tables by Class (One through Six, several 14th-century-only),
plus a Shields table. The chapter's second half introduces an
optional **"Swift as a Sword Blow"** streamlined combat system: single
initiative roll per scene, multiple-action resolution in phases,
Tagging/Delaying/Interrupting/Reacting rules, and a simplified
damage/soak procedure using fixed Bashing/Lethal/Aggravated soak
ratings and a "hardened rating" (half soak, rounded up) below which
attacks deal zero damage. Closes with **Followers of Set Apocrypha:
The Accursed Father** — a frame-narrative myth in which an elder
Nebthet reveals to her childe that Set is not "the father of all
blood drinkers" in the simple sense, but a being who traded places
with "the Defiant One" (an unnamed analogue for Caine) to bear the
world's hatred and seal away extradimensional "Strangers" threatening
creation — explicitly framed by an in-text sidebar as one possible,
Setite-filtered truth among many, not settled cosmology.
**Key themes:** the gap between generic and period-accurate medieval
gaming detail, mechanical streamlining as a practical GMing tool
offered as an alternative rather than a replacement, Setite
cosmology deliberately destabilizing rather than confirming Caine's
mythology — consistent with the library's broader "unreliable, plural
history" pattern, now extended to Set's own account of Genesis. This
chapter is mechanically dense — query the chunk database for specific
weapon/armor stats rather than relying on this summary.
**Key terms/entities:** weapon and armor tables (p.121-126), Swift as
a Sword Blow combat system (p.127-129), the Accursed Father/the
Defiant One/the Strangers (p.129-131).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **Every domain runs on a fragile, personally-maintained peace**:
  Bath's sanctuary depends on faith in Mithras/Arcadius; Mogadishu's
  peace depends on the Na'ib's now-unsustainable relationship with
  Islam; Mangaluru's depends on the Danava/Salubri/Ravnos triumvirate
  surviving its internal schisms; Constantinople's depends on
  Alfonzo's increasingly desperate grip. No domain in this book is
  stable by default — each chapter's "Cainite Factions" section exists
  to hand the Storyteller the specific pressure points.
- **Recurring named-character threads across domain chapters**: Bethany
  of Ely (introduced as a current resident in the Bath chapter's roster,
  then reappears via a letter to her exiled friend Matthias inside the
  Mangaluru chapter's Salubri Apocrypha) and Amaria of Clan Ravnos
  (Mogadishu's information broker, revealed as the Mangaluru chapter's
  Sridhar's sire and slave-trade partner) both tie two geographically
  distant chapters into a single ongoing story — worth flagging since
  neither domain chapter cross-references the other by name.
- **Every domain chapter subtly expands an underexplored corner of the
  wider V20 line**: Constantinople's Dream/Trinity/Dracon material is
  the library's fullest account of a Methuselah only mentioned in
  passing in `v20-dark-ages` (Appendix B's Obertus revenant-family
  entry); Mogadishu's Zoroastrian Assamite material develops a single
  sentence from `v20-lore-clans`' Clan overview into a full dual-Road
  system; Mangaluru's Ravnos jati material similarly expands a short
  passage in `clanbook-ravnos-revised` into named lineages and a full
  blood-magic tradition (Sadhana). None of these contradict the
  existing library entries — they fill in detail the earlier books
  gestured at but didn't develop.
- **A third telling of the Cainite Heresy**: the library's existing
  "Cainite Heresy" cross-cutting theme (see `library_index.md`) already
  tracks `v20-dark-ages`' internal-Lasombra-schism framing against
  `dark-ages-inquisitor`'s external Crimson Curia framing. This book
  adds a named individual data point: **Bethany of Ely**, an Eighth
  Generation Salubri who explicitly **led the Cainite Heresy's
  believers in Ely** before Tremere persecution broke her faith and
  drove her into Mithraist exile in Bath — the library's first example
  of a former Heresy leader's own account of converting away from it,
  distinct from either book's institutional-level description.
- **Monsters as regional folklore given teeth**: each domain's original
  monster is explicitly rooted in that region's own folklore rather
  than a generic bestiary entry — the Black Dog (British Isles), the
  Fossegrim (Scandinavian, with an explicit anti-Tzimisce vendetta),
  the Kallikantzaros (Greek/Byzantine), and the Pishacha (Hindu
  mythology, explicitly left ambiguous as to whether it's vampire-kin
  at all) — reinforcing the same "unreliable, plural, regionally-
  specific history" device used throughout `v20-dark-ages` and this
  book's own narration.
- **Apocrypha as the book's real content, not a bonus feature**: every
  chapter's roughly 40% Apocrypha allocation means the book reads as
  much like 13 short stories (one or two per Clan, several — Tremere,
  Ventrue, Salubri, Ravnos — getting new mechanical content bundled
  with their fiction) as a geography guide. A Storyteller looking for
  "what's new to play with" should check each chapter's Apocrypha
  section specifically rather than assume it's flavor-only.
