# Clanbook: Followers of Set (Revised) — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather

## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Followers
of Set, the "Serpents" or "Setites" — a clan claiming divine (not
merely Cainite) descent from the Egyptian god Set, organized around a
Gnostic-flavored religion (Theophidianism) built on exploiting mortal
vice and "corruption" as a path to enlightenment. Chapter One is
narrated by an in-character clan historian who is candid about
uncertainty and deliberate deception in the record. Covers the clan's
divine origin myth, an unusually global history (Egypt, Byzantium, the
Crusades, West Africa, Mesoamerica, India), the Theophidian religious
schism, and character material.

## Introduction: The Healing Power of Medicine (pp. 5-10)
**Summary:** Fiction piece: a runaway girl, Marla, is lured into a
seemingly-charitable "Safe Place" church and subjected to a manipulative
ritual by the Setite Renenet, culminating in Marla's own Embrace as a
replacement for a discarded acolyte — dramatizing the clan's core method
of predation through false sanctuary and manufactured desperation.
**Key themes:** Corruption disguised as salvation; predation on the
vulnerable via institutions that claim to help.

## Chapter One: A History of Lies (pp. 12-37)
**Summary:** Narrated by a clan historian who repeatedly flags his own
unreliability and the deliberate secrecy of Setite records.
- **Origin myth (Set, Osiris, and Horus):** Retells the Egyptian myth
  (using Greek names deliberately, to avoid revealing "true" Egyptian
  pronunciations) — **Set**, passed over by the aging sun-god Ra in
  favor of his brother **Osiris**, murders Osiris out of jealousy,
  dismembers him after Isis's magical resurrection attempt, and is later
  defeated and castrated by Osiris's son Horus. The narrator notes the
  Book of Nod's competing claim that Set and Osiris were originally
  mortals who both became vampires and fought directly, with Set
  eventually the victor — presented as one of several irreconcilable
  versions.
- **Ancient Egypt:** The clan's early temple network (notably at Tanis,
  built by the Hyksos), rivalry and eventual ~200-year domination of
  Egypt's nights by the Assamites during the Persian occupation, and the
  mysterious disappearance/destroyed sarcophagus of Set himself.
- **Byzantium, the Crusades, and Europe:** Setite involvement in
  Byzantine intrigue, the Crusades (including sabotage of both Muslim and
  Christian mortal war efforts for profit), the **Church of the Black
  Magdalene** (a popular medieval "Cainite Heresy" front spread through
  the Catholic Church, with an oblique nod to the Priory of Sion), and
  the clan's ambivalent survival through the Ottoman conquest of Egypt
  and the European witch-hunt era.
- **Modern era and global reach:** Colonial-era expansion, the loss of
  contact with Bactrian/Central Asian temples after Assamite hostility,
  and **the Serpents of the Light** — a schismatic offshoot that broke
  from the Egyptian temple hierarchy and allied with the Sabbat.
**Key themes:** Deliberate narrative unreliability as a clan survival
strategy (secrecy about true names/pronunciations is treated as
literally dangerous, not just narrative flavor); the clan's opportunism
in playing multiple sides of mortal and Kindred conflicts alike for
profit.
**Key terms/entities:** Set, Osiris, Horus, Isis, Tanis, the Church of
the Black Magdalene, the Serpents of the Light.

## Chapter Two: After Such Knowledge (pp. 38-73)
**Summary:** Details the clan's religious philosophy and global
bloodline variations.
- **Theophidianism:** The clan's core religious framework — mortal
  belief systems are "spiritual chains" that a Setite can help break
  through direct, extreme experiential **gnosis**, engineered via vice
  and corruption rather than doctrine. The religion splits into the
  **Path of Typhon** (encourages ecstatic excess and syncretic
  reinterpretation, linking Set to figures like Bacchus, Mars, and the
  Greek monster Typhon) and the **Path of Serpentis** (a more
  Egyptian-focused, disciplined variant).
- **Temples and regional bloodlines:** Founding temples (including Abu
  Simbel); the **Tlacique** (a Mesoamerican Setite branch entangled with
  Aztec sacrificial religion and Spanish colonization, with a unique form
  of Thaumaturgy, "Nahuallotl"); West African **Damballan** Setites
  (voodoo-linked, largely uninterested in the Sabbat); and the Indian
  **Naktanchara**, a Setite sub-caste formally declared "outcaste" by
  the rest of the clan in an unusual show of unanimity, mirroring the
  extreme caste taboos of the Brahmin social system they mimic.
- **Cosmology and relations with other clans:** A distinctive
  Setite/Gnostic cosmological frame — clans and even zodiac signs are
  mapped onto a system of ruling "**Aeons**" the Setites claim to
  secretly oppose (echoing Gnostic archon mythology); brief dismissive/
  wary notes on Brujah, Lasombra ("a studied pose of evil"), Assamites
  (the clan's oldest, most loyal rivals), Camarilla clans generally, and
  the Tremere as a potential ally against "the Aeons."
- **Cult life and Status:** Setites generally avoid mortal fame (it
  draws unwanted attention), but clan Status above 3 requires leading a
  cult and establishing a temple — tying personal advancement directly to
  successful predation/religious leadership.
- **Game material:** Extensive Discipline material for **Serpentis**
  (Form of the Cobra/Apep, venom effects), Obfuscate (Confusion of the
  Eye), Presence-adjacent corruption powers (True Will, Revelation of
  Satiety), and Thaumaturgy paths tied to the regional bloodlines
  (Spirit Manipulation for the Serpents of the Light; Tlacique
  Nahuallotl for the Mesoamerican branch).
**Key themes:** Religion as a genuinely load-bearing worldview for the
clan (not mere window dressing) built on exploiting rather than
suppressing vice; global reach and regional adaptation (Mesoamerica,
West Africa, India) presented with real specificity rather than as
afterthoughts.
**Key terms/entities:** Theophidianism, gnosis, Path of Typhon, Path of
Serpentis, the Tlacique, the Damballans, the Naktanchara, the Aeons.

## Chapter Three: Broken Images (pp. 74-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Psychiatrist**, **Mystic Artist**,
**Superspy**, **Entrepreneur**, **Schoolmaster of Sin**,
**Transhumanist**, **Gladiator**, **Voodoo Queen**, **Debunking
Scientist**, **Native Rights Activist**. Closes with a detailed **Sample
Brood: The Cult of Set** — a multi-front business operation (ground-floor
storefronts concealing vampire havens and cult ritual space) run by the
elder **Ahmose Chambers** and his cultists/childer (including the
psychologically fractured "Belle," split into two personas by ongoing
manipulation), plus profiles of notable elders including **Kemintiri**
(a disgraced master of disguise regarded poorly even by her own clan)
and **Hesha** (a wealthy, resourceful operator who owns a blood bank
among his assets).
**Key themes:** Range from mundane professional predators to openly
monstrous cult leaders, illustrating Chapter Two's Theophidian corruption
philosophy in concrete practice via the Sample Brood's manipulation of
"Belle."

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Deliberate narrative unreliability**: Chapter One's narrator
  explicitly withholds and distorts information (true names, exact
  history) as an in-character survival tactic, distinguishing this book's
  approach from other clanbooks' unreliable narration used mainly for
  mythic ambiguity.
- **Corruption as religion, not mere villainy**: Theophidianism gives the
  clan's predatory behavior a genuine, internally coherent philosophical
  and cosmological framework (gnosis through vice, the Aeons) rather than
  treating manipulation as simple evil-for-its-own-sake.
- **Global specificity**: unusually for a Camarilla clanbook of this era,
  the book gives real narrative weight to non-European bloodline
  variants (Tlacique, Damballans, Naktanchara) rather than treating them
  as footnotes to a Egypt/Europe-centered main line.
- **Institutions as predatory camouflage**: from the introduction's fake
  runaway shelter to Chapter Three's cult-front businesses, the clan
  repeatedly hides its predation inside structures that claim to help or
  serve.
