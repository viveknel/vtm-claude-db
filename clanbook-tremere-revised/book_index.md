# Clanbook: Tremere (Revised) — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather

## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Tremere,
the "Warlocks" or "Usurpers" — a clan of former mortal wizards who
willfully created their own vampirism around 1000 AD and remain the
youngest and most institutionally organized clan, governed by a strict
pyramidal hierarchy. Notably, this book treats the clan's founding
atrocities (mass forced Embrace of a wizardly order, diablerie of the
Salubri Antediluvian Saulot) matter-of-factly and even approvingly —
a striking tonal contrast to how other clanbooks (e.g. Clanbook: Salubri)
portray the same events. Covers the founding, the clan's internal
Pyramid structure, Thaumaturgy rituals, and character material.

## Introduction: Enlightened By the Darkness (pp. 5-10)
**Summary:** Fiction piece: a professional magician/skeptic who debunks
supernatural claims for a living is targeted and Embraced (via violent
blood-drinking assault) by a mysterious woman after a public
confrontation, ending mid-transformation.
**Key themes:** Rationalism confronted and overturned by the reality of
Kindred existence — thematically apt for a clan built on transmuting
mortal knowledge-seeking into vampiric power.

## Chapter One: The Price of Immortality (pp. 11-25)
**Summary:** Recounts clan history in a fairly straightforward,
institutional voice (unlike several other clanbooks' unreliable-narrator
framings).
- **Founding:** **Tremere**, a mortal wizard, foresaw the terminal
  decline of mortal sorcery around 1000 AD and directed his order's
  research toward vampirism instead. His assistant **Goratrix**
  developed a potion/ritual that induced Embrace-like transformation
  (originally intended merely to grant immortality without losing daytime
  capability); after testing it on two apprentices, the whole surviving
  order — including the reluctant **Etrius** — converted into vampires,
  aided (grudgingly) by Tzimisce and Gangrel who later turned hostile.
- **Early consolidation:** As despised newcomers without full mastery of
  Kindred Disciplines, the fledgling Tremere developed **Thaumaturgy**
  from their prior magical training, created the **Gargoyle** bloodline
  as war-servants, and expanded from the Carpathians outward.
- **Saulot's diablerie:** A sidebar states flatly that Tremere
  "discovered" and diablerized the Antediluvian Saulot, framing this as
  a clan triumph ("a boon to all Kindred") and dismissing rumors that
  Saulot arranged his own death as "misguided propaganda" — presented
  without the moral ambiguity found in the Salubri-focused material
  elsewhere in this library.
- **The Inquisition and the Camarilla:** Detailed account of Inquisition-
  era losses (chantries burned, thaumaturgical knowledge destroyed) and
  the clan's role as a driving force in founding the Camarilla for mutual
  Kindred survival.
- **Modern era:** Colonial-era expansion, industrialization, and a brief
  closing meditation on Gehenna (the clan encourages neonates to
  investigate it as a research problem rather than fear it outright).
**Key themes:** Vampirism as a deliberate, engineered transformation
rather than a mystical curse suffered passively; institutional
continuity and research-driven pragmatism as the clan's defining
character, in contrast to other clans' more mythic self-narratives.
**Key terms/entities:** Tremere (founder), Goratrix, Etrius, Saulot's
diablerie, Thaumaturgy, the Gargoyles.

## Chapter Two: Inside the Pyramid (pp. 26-71)
**Summary:** Details the clan's rigid internal hierarchy — the
**Pyramid** — from apprentice through **regent** and **pontifex** up to
the ruling **Council of Seven**, emphasizing that advancement is slow,
merit- and loyalty-based, and enforced partly through blood-magic
loyalty rituals. Covers "rogue"-hunting (the clan's harsh internal
policing of defectors), internal factions/orders (**Traditionalists**;
the **Humanus League**, Tremere seeking to "purify" their vampiric
curse; **Astors** and other named orders), the **Tremere Code** (the
formal oath sworn at Embrace, reproduced in the text), the clan's
distinctive lack of a Path of Enlightenment equivalent (their discipline
comes from institutional structure instead), and relations with other
clans/entities (wary dealings with Setites, uneasy détente with ghosts,
notes on Sabbat Tremere). The chapter's back half is dense with new
**Thaumaturgy** game material: rituals (Purify Blood, Unweave Ritual,
Mark of Amaranth, Bone of Contention, and more), Path powers (Eye of the
Storm, Night's Veil), the **certámen** dueling rite, blood contracts, and
homunculus servants.
**Key themes:** Institutional hierarchy and research/knowledge as the
clan's core organizing principle; internal dissent (rogues, Humanus
League) as a persistent but tightly managed problem.
**Key terms/entities:** the Pyramid, Council of Seven, regent/pontifex,
Traditionalists, Humanus League, the Tremere Code, certámen.

## Chapter Three: Stones of the Pyramid (pp. 72-101)
**Summary:** Character templates: **Security Consultant**, **Diplomat**,
**Labor Union Leader**, **Crowley** (an occultist provocateur), **Anarch
Thaumaturge**, **Pyramid Fanatic**, **Cloistered Researcher**,
**Nightclub Owner**, **Medical Examiner**, **Refugee-Turned-Scourge**.
Followed by a detailed **Sample Coterie** (a modified-van-driving research
team with a consultant cover identity) and profiles of named Council of
Seven-adjacent elders, including **Ravenfeather**, **Aisling** (a modern,
tech-fluent childe who avoids "old Tremere thinking"), and **Varya** (a
neonate exiled abroad after a kinslaying scandal). Also revisits the
founding-era debate between Etrius and Goratrix over the ethics/wisdom of
the vampirism potion.
**Key themes:** Range from loyal institutional operators to individual
dissenters (Anarch Thaumaturge, Crowley), reflecting Chapter Two's
tension between hierarchy and internal reform movements.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Vampirism as engineered, not mythic**: uniquely among these
  clanbooks, the Tremere's origin is a deliberate research project with
  named, documented participants (Tremere, Goratrix, Etrius) rather than
  contested legend — reinforcing the clan's institutional, rationalist
  self-image.
- **Hierarchy as identity**: the Pyramid structure, the Tremere Code
  oath, and the rogue-hunting apparatus all reinforce that individual
  Tremere identity is inseparable from clan institutional loyalty.
- **Unapologetic ambition**: the book's matter-of-fact treatment of the
  clan's founding atrocities (forced Embrace, Saulot's diablerie) marks a
  deliberate authorial choice to write the "villain" clan of the setting
  from the inside without softening its self-justifications — useful to
  contrast directly against Clanbook: Salubri's portrayal of the same
  events elsewhere in this library.
- **Research/pragmatism over ideology**: unlike clans organized around a
  Path of Enlightenment or shared philosophy, Tremere Ch.2 explicitly
  notes the clan substitutes institutional structure and knowledge-
  seeking for that role.
