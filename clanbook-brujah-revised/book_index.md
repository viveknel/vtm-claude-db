# Clanbook: Brujah (Revised) — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A modern-nights (Revised Edition, c.2000) sourcebook for Clan Brujah, the
"Rabble" or "Learned Clan" — vampires defined by passionate rebellion and
a compulsion toward social change, however contradictory their individual
politics. The book covers the clan's disputed legendary origins (Brujah
and/or Troile), the loss of their "idyllic" city-state Carthage, the
centuries-long Anarch Revolt that shaped the Camarilla/Sabbat split, the
clan's fractious internal culture (rants, raves, "iconoclasts" vs.
"idealists"), their relationships to every other clan and sect, new
Discipline powers/Merits/Flaws, and 10 ready-to-play character templates
plus a sample "brood" NPC group.

## Introduction: A Night Like Any Other (pp. 5-12)
**Summary:** Fiction piece establishing the modern Embrace from a
victim's-eye view: an unnamed mortal is stalked, provoked into self-
destructive violence, coerced into stabbing his own predator, then forced
to suicide and Embraced against his explicit initial refusal — his sire
frames the ordeal as proof he "wanted this," revealing Brujah rage as
something drawn out rather than simply inherent.
**Key themes:** The Brujah sire's belief that the childe secretly craves
transformation/rebellion; the brutality beneath the recruitment.

## Chapter One: Millennia of Conflict (pp. 13-33)
**Summary:** The clan's history, told as explicitly contested legend
throughout ("no such thing as a concise history of Clan Brujah").
- **The Second City / Troile:** Multiple incompatible legends of the
  Antediluvian Brujah (troublemaker/scapegoat vs. secretly virtuous) and
  of his own childe **Troile**, the figure most modern Brujah actually
  claim descent from via an act of diablerie against his own sire — some
  skeptics suspect Brujah and Troile were the same individual. Introduces
  the rumored separate **"True Brujah"** bloodline/"first brood,"
  associated with rumors of the Black Hand.
- **Fabled Carthage:** The clan's lost golden age and central grief — a
  Phoenician port city Brujah (with a strong Assamite minority) treated
  as a utopia of Kindred-mortal symbiosis, undercut by real decadence
  (Baalist blood-rites, child sacrifice) and destroyed in the Third Punic
  War (146 BC) via Ventrue-linked betrayal, then salted by Rome. Sidebar
  on the **Promethians**, reform-minded Carthaginian Brujah possibly
  linked to the Inconnu and Golconda.
- **The Dark Ages / Transylvania:** Irish Brujah (Clara of Cork, Fergal
  the Pious) preserve literacy via monastery libraries; Brujah migrate to
  Transylvania and clash with Tzimisce and Ventrue-backed Magyars; the
  warlord **Dominic** ghoul-arms soldiers and, embittered after Bulscu
  chooses Ventrue Embrace over alliance, launches a two-century guerrilla
  war that becomes the **Anarch Revolt**, spreading Kindred-wide and
  directly seeding the eventual Sabbat/Black Hand.
- **The New World, the Frontier, North America:** Brujah flood into
  colonial/frontier America (outlaw gangs, the Wild West), split between
  Sabbat-dominated Mexico and Camarilla/anarch North America.
- **The Anarch Free State:** Detailed modern history — Jeremy MacNeil's
  1944 uprising against Toreador Prince Sebastian of Los Angeles spreads
  up the California coast; the resulting "free state" decays into
  balkanized gang turf by the 1980s; **Prince Tara**'s 1998 defection of
  San Diego back to Camarilla recognition and a Cathayan (Kindred of the
  East) incursion that topples Camarilla San Francisco in 2000 leave the
  Free State fracturing, with MacNeil apparently lost (Final Death or
  captured) in the climactic four-way battle.
- **Russia:** Brujah dominate the early Bolshevik/Soviet-era "Brujah
  Council," are undermined by Lenin's un-Dominate-able autonomy and later
  Stalin's purges, and are ultimately annihilated (all Council members
  destroyed in eight nights, most eaten rather than killed) by the ancient
  Baba Yaga rising from torpor.
**Key themes:** History as irreconcilable, competing legend; loss
(Carthage) as the clan's defining trauma; the Anarch Revolt as the
Brujah's single largest (if largely unacknowledged) mark on all Kindred
society; cyclical rise-and-fracture of every Brujah power structure.
**Key terms/entities:** Troile, Carthage, the Promethians, Dominic, the
Anarch Revolt, Jeremy MacNeil, Smiling Jack, the Anarch Free State, Prince
Tara, the Brujah Council, Baba Yaga.

## Chapter Two: Unlife Among the Brujah (pp. 35-69)
**Summary:** Framed as the collected "Notebooks of Horace Kaplan," an
ancilla essayist — a first-person, opinionated tour of Brujah culture.
- **Social types:** Divides Brujah loosely into **iconoclasts** (young,
  rebellious, anarch-leaning), **idealists** (older, analytical,
  invoke Carthage as proof unity is possible), and a disputed third
  "individualist" label; argues the real unifying thread is *humanitas*
  — an unusually strong attachment to conscience underlying even the
  clan's most violent members.
- **Gatherings:** **Rants** (issue-driven assemblies: policy rants,
  debates, prestige rants, rally rants, and the narrator's own coinage
  "spite rants" for punitive violence) and **raves** (modern hedonistic
  parties, a genuine Brujah cultural export, both a meeting-place and a
  site of "affectionate" internal brawling).
- **The Traditions, Brujah-style:** Extended, skeptical commentary on how
  Brujah interpret/resent each Camarilla Tradition — flags Domain and
  Hospitality as the clan's own "Two Great Hypocrisies" (Brujah are
  fiercely territorial themselves despite railing against others'
  claims); covers Progeny (Embrace chosen for personal utility/kinship,
  strong sense of sire responsibility despite reputation for carelessness)
  and Destruction (illustrated via a case study from Ulster/Connachta).
- **Camarilla roles:** Brujah rarely hold princedoms but dominate the
  **sheriff** and often the **primogen** role (illustrated via a detailed
  Las Vegas case study involving primogen intrigue against a Toreador
  elder scheming with the Giovanni); also covers **scourges** (often
  disturbingly casual killers) and **harpies**.
- **Anarchs:** Explains the real fault line between Brujah and anarchs —
  shared rebellious instinct, opposed methods (Brujah favor working the
  existing system; anarchs want to overturn it outright).
- **Relations with every other clan/faction:** Detailed, opinionated
  takes on Assamites (old, respected, entangled with Carthage history),
  Gangrel ("brothers-in-arms" respect), Giovanni (mostly indifferent,
  occasional pragmatic alliances), Lasombra (Sabbat leadership, disliked),
  Malkavians (mixed — occasional real utility), Nosferatu (transactional
  respect), Ravnos ("clever," untrustworthy), Setites (a necessary evil,
  addictive to deal with), Toreador (deplorable but underestimated),
  Tremere ("very breakable" or "ungodly powerful," secretive), Tzimisce
  (openly disgusted, doesn't want to discuss), Ventrue (the core rivalry —
  framed not as hatred of authority per se but of Ventrue *complacency*
  and profiting off others' labor), the Camarilla (imperfect but
  functional, the lesser evil), the Sabbat (rejected as incoherent
  fanaticism, not freedom), and the Inconnu (doubts it even exists).
**Key themes:** Deliberate, essayistic unreliability (a single narrator's
opinionated voice, explicitly not a clan consensus); the tension between
Brujah's self-image as principled and their actual territorial hypocrisy;
Brujah as uniquely socially embedded among mortals compared to other clans.
**Key terms/entities:** Horace Kaplan (narrator), rants/raves, iconoclasts/
idealists, the "Two Great Hypocrisies."

### Game material (pp. 66-69)
New combination-Discipline combo powers (**Burning Wrath** — Celerity/
Potence multi-hit aggravated strike; **Iron Heart** — Potence/Presence
resistance to mental domination; **Pulse of Undeath** — Auspex/Potence
sense another's physical Disciplines); high-level (6th-7th dot) new
Discipline powers (**The Gentle Rebuke**, Potence 6, a shoving-force
power; **Relentless Pursuit**, Potence 6, superhuman leaping; **Stutter-
Step**, Celerity 7, multi-defense flicker); Backgrounds (Alternate
Identity, Military Force) and Merits/Flaws (Compassionate, Dynamic
Personality, Obvious Predator, Uncontrollable).

## Chapter Three: The Rabble's Ranks (pp. 71-106)
**Summary:** Ten character templates, each with quote/prelude/concept/
roleplaying hints/equipment: **The Confederate** (Civil War revisionist
historian-turned-idealist agitator), **The Harpy-to-Be** (an ambitious
social climber learning Kindred etiquette), **The Paladin** (ex-athlete
turned self-appointed protector/enforcer), **La Belle Dame Sans Merci**
(a hardened, self-interested survivor from the barrio), **The
Anachronism** (an elder out of step with modern mores), **net.kindred**
(a hacker-information-broker type), **The Small-Town Sheriff** (a
literal lawman-turned-Kindred sheriff figure), **The Digital Musician**
(an apolitical artist wary of being drafted into clan causes), **The
Pugilist** (a fighter who avoids overthinking odds), **The Anarch
Ringleader** (a nihilistic trend-subverter). Closes with a fully
statted **Sample Brood: Hezekiah's Revival** — an itinerant evangelist
Brujah "ministry" (Hezekiah the founder/preacher, plus Jack, Marion, and
Ben) that uses a genuine traveling-revival herd as cover and resource
base, illustrating a socially-embedded, mortal-facing Brujah coterie in
practice, followed by blank character sheets.
**Key themes:** Deliberate diversity of concept across the templates,
reinforcing Ch.2's point that Brujah defy a single mold; the sample brood
demonstrates the clan's characteristic deep mortal embedding as a
survival/concealment strategy.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Irreconcilable self-narrative**: nearly every historical and cultural
  claim in the book is explicitly flagged as contested, unreliable, or a
  single narrator's opinion (Ch.1's competing Troile legends; Ch.2's
  admittedly biased Kaplan essay) — consistent with the clan's core trait
  of fractiousness.
- **Passion/rage vs. conscience**: the clan's signature Curse-of-Caine
  frenzy-proneness is repeatedly paired with an unusually strong
  attachment to *humanitas*/conscience — "the horror of the conscience"
  is offered as the clan's unifying thread in the Ch.3 introduction.
- **Carthage as trauma and myth**: referenced across Ch.1 (history), Ch.2
  (idealists invoke it as proof of possible unity), and implicitly in the
  Anarch Free State's failed attempt to be "New Carthage."
- **System vs. anti-system**: recurring tension between working within
  existing power structures (Camarilla membership, primogen/sheriff
  roles) and tearing them down (anarchs, the Sabbat, the Anarch Revolt) —
  most Brujah, per Ch.2, actually favor the former despite their
  reputation.

  (pp. 37-39)

- Troile — the figure most Brujah actually claim descent from, via
