# V20 Lore of the Bloodlines — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
Lore of the Bloodlines is a sourcebook for Vampire: The Masquerade 20th
Anniversary Edition detailing nine minor vampire bloodlines that fall
outside the thirteen main Clans. Each chapter is narrated in-character by
a member of that bloodline (framed as one long dissertation/interrogation
by an elder to a chained neonate), giving each bloodline's own biased
version of its history, its relationship to other Clans and Sects, its
society and factions in the modern nights, and closing with game
material (character concepts, Merits/Flaws, combination Disciplines,
rituals). The book explicitly warns the material is unreliable
narration — "truth" is contested even within the text.

## Introduction (pp. 6-9)
**Summary:** Framed as a sire recounting the nine bloodlines to a bound
childe as a follow-up to an earlier study of the thirteen Clans. Establishes
the book's conceit: each bloodline chapter is oral history from an insider,
possibly self-serving or false. Names all nine bloodlines and gives a
one-line teaser for each.
**Key themes:** unreliable narration, oral tradition vs. written history,
bloodlines as outsiders to Clan society.

## Baali (pp. 9-18)
**Summary:** Infernalist bloodline descended from brothers Nergal and
Moloch, who devoured the buried "First People" (God's discarded
pre-human creations) and gained power to summon demons, chiefly Namtaru,
Spreader of Plagues. Nergal's demon-fueled bid for domination was put
down by an alliance of Lasombra, Nosferatu, and Toreador after Moloch's
betrayal; Namtaru was destroyed alongside him. Surviving Baali went
underground, served by a corrupted revenant family (the D'Habi), and
occasionally infiltrated institutions (the Baali agent Azaneal rose high
in the Catholic Church, producing the half-Baali/half-Lasombra Angellis
Ater hybrids). Modern Baali cultivate sacrificial "Nergals" — flamboyant,
expendable members who fit the stereotype and get hunted so the rest can
operate — and believe the end of the world is coming and see themselves
as hastening it. Baali can "Re-Embrace" vampires of other Clans, overriding
their original blood with a ritual invoking Namtaru.
**Key themes:** demonology and infernalism, nihilism/apocalypticism,
misdirection and scapegoating, corruption of religious institutions.
**Key terms/entities:** Nergal and Moloch (p.10-11), Namtaru (p.11-12),
D'Habi revenants (p.13), Azaneal/Angellis Ater (p.13), "Nergals" as
decoys (p.14), Re-Embrace ritual (p.17-18).
**Notable claims/data points:** Re-Embrace mechanics and success table
(p.18); combination Discipline Command the Swarm (Daimoinon/Animalism, p.16).

## Daughters of Cacophony (pp. 19-28)
**Summary:** A bloodline built on song and voice, publicly explained by a
folk tale of three sisters Embraced into different Clans (Toreador,
Ventrue, Malkavian) who shared blood and power in secret. The truer,
guarded history: the Tal'Mahe'Ra sect created them from Toreador
performers trained by a Malkavian, intended as a weapon to slowly drive
Camarilla leadership insane; the experiment drifted from its original
purpose, and after centuries of ambivalence the Sect let surviving
Daughters go in exchange for silence about their Tal'Mahe'Ra origins.
Each Daughter hears a personalized inner music called "the Fugue";
those who lose themselves to it become homicidal "Banshees." Modern
factions include reclusive "Soloists," tightly bonded "Choristers" (whose
secrecy worries other Daughters), the meditative "Sisters of the Fugue"
seeking prophetic insight in the music, and the "Quiet Celebrities" who
manage fame and identity for performing Daughters. The bloodline mostly
avoids Sect politics and Clan-style hierarchy, clustering loosely in
music-associated cities.
**Key themes:** hidden origins/conspiracy, art and madness, secrecy as
survival strategy, found-family vs. solitude.
**Key terms/entities:** the Tal'Mahe'Ra and the Orchestra of the Black
Hand (p.21), the Fugue (p.23-24), Banshees (p.24), Choristers/Soloists/
Sisters of the Fugue/Quiet Celebrities (pp.24-26).
**Notable claims/data points:** Elder Discipline Mosh Pit induces frenzy
in supernaturals (p.28); Calling Song can compel travel across distance (p.28).

## Gargoyles (pp. 29-38)
**Summary:** Not-quite-vampires created by the Tremere as living weapons
and servants via a ritual ("At Our Command It Breathes") invented by the
Tremere mage Virstania, who combined vitae from Gangrel (shapeshifting),
Tzimisce war-ghoul inspiration, and Nosferatu test subjects with grave
dirt and stitched corpses. Virstania came to see the Gargoyles as her
children and, appalled by their treatment, led them in a revolt centered
on Ceoris (the original Tremere chantry in Transylvania), forcing the
Council of Seven to relocate to Vienna. The 1489 Montmartre Pact
formally ended Gargoyle creation and joined the Tremere to the Camarilla,
though the ritual persists in rare "Gaslight Gargoyle" incidents. Three
variant types exist — Scouts (fast, stealthy couriers), Sentinels
(tough guardians), and Warriors (brutal soldiers) — each with distinct
Disciplines and weaknesses. Most free Gargoyles still serve Tremere
chantries out of habit, fear, or fondness; a minority side with Anarchs,
Camarilla, or (rarely) Sabbat. All Gargoyles fly, lose most human
memories upon transformation, and are vulnerable to mind control.
**Key themes:** creation and slavery vs. freedom, complicated
loyalty to abusive creators, found identity through service.
**Key terms/entities:** Virstania, "Mother of Gargoyles" (p.30-32);
Montmartre Pact 1489 (p.32); Scout/Sentinel/Warrior variants (pp.32-33,
36-37); Sixpence Pinch gang, London (p.33).
**Notable claims/data points:** combination Discipline Carry the
Mountain's Burden combines Fortitude/Visceratika for extreme toughness (p.37);
Gargoyle-specific rituals via Enchant Talisman (p.37-38).

## Harbingers of Skulls (pp. 39-50)
**Summary:** Remnants of an ancient Clan devoted to a founder called
"Father"/Ashur (also called Cappadocius, Anubis, or other names —
identity deliberately obscured), who in a fit of madness ordered the
mass destruction of his own progeny. The childe Lazarus led survivors
("Lazarenes") into the Underworld/Shadowlands to escape, where they were
further devastated when the Giovanni destroyed Ashur/Cappadocius (the
"killing blow" after Ashur's own "mortal wound"). After centuries
trapped among wraiths — losing memory, sanity, and identity in the river
Lethe — Lazarus led a return to the living world, and Harbingers now
wear identity/status masks (having lost recognizable faces) and hunt
Giovanni in a blood feud, while also maintaining alliances with Followers
of Set, Lasombra mystics, and (with more difficulty) Tzimisce. The
chapter is told through multiple named, masked narrators (Marchesa
Liliana, "The Gentleman," Abraham, "the Wearer of the Capuchin Mask")
with sharply differing, sometimes contradictory theories about the
bloodline's true nature — including one chilling claim that many
Harbingers are secretly wraith-possessed sleeper agents ("Puppeteers").
Mostly Sabbat-aligned; largely forbidden from Embracing until all
Lazarenes are recovered from the Shadowlands, though this rule is not
universally followed.
**Key themes:** trauma and memory loss, generational vendetta, unreliable
multi-narrator history, masks/identity, the Underworld as both
punishment and refuge.
**Key terms/entities:** Ashur/"Father"/Cappadocius (p.41), Lazarus and
the Lazarenes (p.42-43), the River Lethe (p.43), the masks/Disciples
(p.43), Adonai and Salubri antitribu parallel-plot mention (p.45),
Shadowlands Shellshock derangements (p.47).
**Notable claims/data points:** combination Discipline Sutekh Fathers
Anubis (jackal form, p.49); Generation of the Acheron Vortex ritual
requires Lasombra vitae (p.49).

## Kiasyd (pp. 51-62)
**Summary:** A Lasombra-descended bloodline founded by Marconius, a
nobleman-turned-alchemist Embraced for his mortal status rather than his
research. Marconius and his sister Hrotsuitha experimented with creating
"perfect beings" from vitae, changeling essence, and magi souls; when
mortal test subjects failed, Hrotsuitha sacrificed herself as the final
subject inside the Abyss, and Marconius returned transformed but never
revealed her fate. The Lasombra Amici Noctis, ashamed of his unsanctioned
experiments, imprisoned him for roughly 80 years before releasing him
(diminished, "bedlam"-afflicted) after the Anarch Revolt; the Kiasyd were
scattered across the Sabbat, limited to two per city. Kiasyd research
blood magic, the Abyss, and the fae, holding periodic "Great Symposium"
gatherings (1666 onward) to share findings — the 1866 Symposium ended in
bloodshed after inviting a Tremere guest, and a 1916 theft/betrayal
soured trust further, cancelling the Symposium for the 20th century.
Modern Kiasyd politics center on figures like Béatrice L'Angou and
Reverend McCabe elevating bloodline standing within the Sabbat, while
Marconius himself has become, oddly, Prince of Strasbourg under
Camarilla protection. Kiasyd are alien in appearance and manner, prize
"truths told with bad intent," and experiment obsessively with alchemical
vitae cocktails from other supernatural creatures.
**Key themes:** forbidden experimentation, alien otherness, factional
mistrust within a small bloodline, truth as a rhetorical weapon.
**Key terms/entities:** Marconius and Hrotsuitha (p.52-53), the Amici
Noctis and Castel d'Ombro imprisonment (p.53), the Great Symposium
1666/1866/1916 (pp.54-56), Béatrice L'Angou and Reverend McCabe (p.56),
Kiasyd Alchemy/vitae cocktails (pp.57-58, 61).
**Notable claims/data points:** Storytellers may permit Necromancy in
place of Dominate at the cost of double Clan Enmity (p.60, sidebar); combo
Discipline Sacrifice to Nightmare summons an Abyssal tentacled creature (p.61).

## Nagaraja (pp. 63-72)
**Summary:** Originally death-mages of Enoch, the first Necropolis, who
transformed themselves via blood rites to survive within the deadly
boundaries of that city and became its librarians and defenders against
spectres, rival necromancers, and the Followers of Set. Their
distinguishing trait is that they must consume flesh ("Viscus") as well
as blood — hence the epithet "Flesh-Eaters." Around 1600 CE a
catastrophic maelstrom ("the Fall of Enoch") devastated the city, killing
most Nagaraja, destroying irreplaceable lore, and scattering survivors,
who degenerated into predatory outcasts hunted by Assamites and Followers
of Set alike. Modern Nagaraja live as solitary serial-killer-like
predators, gathering roughly once a decade at large, carefully staged
"silent conclaves" (jokingly "murderer conventions") to share intel and
resources. Every Nagaraja instinctively knows the way back to Enoch
through the underworld and is expected to offer this path to their own
childer; the True Black Hand faction maintains contact with Enoch's
remnant and periodically recruits members back to a stricter, more
"indoctrinated" existence there.
**Key themes:** ritual purpose vs. survival instinct, monstrousness as
identity, isolation, flesh/blood duality.
**Key terms/entities:** Enoch/the Necropolis (p.64-65), the Fall of Enoch
c. 1600 CE (p.65-66), Viscus (p.67), silent conclaves/"murderer
conventions" (p.68), the True Black Hand (p.68-69).
**Notable claims/data points:** Soul Separation combo Discipline
(Vitreous Path/Auspex) lets a Nagaraja devour a victim's soul directly
for large blood gains (p.70-71).

## Salubri (pp. 73-82)
**Summary:** Originally one of the thirteen founding Clans, reduced to
bloodline status after the Tremere destroyed their Antediluvian, Saulot,
in 1291 (158 years after Saulot's own death) and usurped their place.
Saulot alone among the Third Generation was spared Caine's curse of
excess and sought Golconda; his childer split into three lines —
Healers (from Rayzeel), Warriors (from Samiel, created to fight the
Baali), and the more mysterious Watchers. Nearly wiped out by the
Tremere pogrom following the Convention of Thorns, Salubri scattered
globally (the Nkulu Zao in Africa, the Wu Zao — split into Scholars and
Thieves — in the East, the al-Amin among Muslim Ashirra in the
Levant). A group of seven elders ("the Seven") sought Golconda beyond the
Tremere's reach and returned as deranged false-messiahs who convince
childer to diablerize them. A resurgent Salubri antitribu faction under
a revived elder named Adonai has emerged in the Sabbat's Black Hand,
embracing martial ferocity the mainline Clan historically shunned.
Modern Salubri survive by hiding, protecting mortal herds from disease
and predation, guiding wayward Kindred toward Humanity, and quietly
brokering pacts with individual elders of other Clans. All Salubri bear
a literal third eye (visible or scarred-over) tied to their signature
Discipline (Obeah/Valeren).
**Key themes:** persecution and diaspora, purity vs. corruption/decay of
ideals, gentleness paired with concealed fury, fractured identity across
regional castes.
**Key terms/entities:** Saulot (p.74), Rayzeel/Samiel/the Watchers
(p.74-75), the Tremere pogrom and 1291 Clan-death (p.76), the Seven
(p.78), Adonai and the Salubri antitribu (p.78-79), the Third Eye (p.81
sidebar).
**Notable claims/data points:** Ending the Watch power lets a Salubri
mercifully kill and absorb a dying mortal's soul for information (p.81).

## Samedi (pp. 83-92)
**Summary:** A putrefying, corpse-like bloodline born in the Caribbean
slave trade, whose founder — "the Baron" — is identified with (or
identical to) Baron Samedi, the voudoun loa of death and resurrection.
Multiple conflicting origin stories are given: that the Baron predates
vampires and made a bargain to become one; that he is a fugitive Kindred
who adopted the loa's identity and iconography for protection; or that
the whole voudoun connection is a fabrication borrowed from human belief.
The Samedi's rise is tied to Caribbean history — colonial slavery,
Havana's mid-20th-century mob/casino era (and the Cuban Revolution, which
saw fleeing Giovanni owe the Samedi lasting debts for sanctuary), and
present-day smuggling and criminal work suited to their unsettling
appearance. Their relationship with the Giovanni is tense and
contradictory — competing over necromancy/Thanatosis, funeral industry
territory, and a disputed bargain between the Baron and Augustus
Giovanni that neither side is happy with. Samedi are widely scattered,
individually or in pairs, across the Caribbean, the American South
(New Orleans, Miami), and West Africa, without much central organization.
**Key themes:** religious syncretism, ambiguous supernatural origins,
outsider status used as professional advantage, uneasy rivalry with the
Giovanni over death magic.
**Key terms/entities:** the Baron/Baron Samedi (p.85-86), Havana Nocturne
and the Cuban Revolution (p.86), the Giovanni Connection (p.88), Thanatosis
and necromancy overlap (p.88).
**Notable claims/data points:** Grisly Gris-Gris combo Discipline lets a
Samedi grant Fortitude-based protection via an ingested body part (p.90);
Exquisite Corpse hides all visible injury (p.90).

## True Brujah (pp. 93-102)
**Summary:** The original, disciplined Brujah bloodline of scholar-
philosophers who foresaw that Kindred society was doomed to devour
itself and deliberately withdrew from the Jyhad rather than fight it.
After the fall of the Second Generation, many Brujah who drank elder
blood were overcome by uncontrollable rage; the childe Troile led this
enraged faction in slaughtering the original, disciplined Brujah at a
Clan meeting, effectively founding the modern (mainline) Brujah Clan by
force. Survivors ("True Brujah") let the "traitor Brujah" have the name
and continued their scholarship in secret, later attempting reconciliation
by building Carthage as a joint civilizing project — a project the
enraged Brujah abandoned to flame and vengeance when the city fell,
prompting the True Brujah to bury the unrepentant Troile "in sand and
time" before withdrawing from Kindred society for good. True Brujah
command Temporis (time manipulation), maintain vast hidden archive
vaults tended by a faction called the Guardians, form the Western arm of
the Tal'Mahe'Ra, and treat all Kindred relationships as transactional
and short-term to avoid entanglement in the Jyhad. A splinter faction,
the Unmakers, wants to violently exterminate the "false" Brujah and even
use time powers to try to prevent Troile's betrayal retroactively —
a plan most True Brujah view as reckless.
**Key themes:** foresight and inevitability, disciplined withdrawal vs.
enraged violence, knowledge preservation, the cost of neutrality.
**Key terms/entities:** Troile and the fall of the (true) Brujah (p.95),
Carthage (p.95), the Tal'Mahe'Ra's Western arm (p.96), the Unmakers and
the Guardians factions (pp.98).
**Notable claims/data points:** Rewind (Temporis) lets a True Brujah view
past events on a table scaling from a week to a millennium (p.101);
Old Friend combo Discipline implants false but experientially "real"
memories of friendship (p.100).

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Origin ambiguity and unreliable narration**: Every chapter is told
  by a biased in-character narrator, and several (Daughters of Cacophony,
  Harbingers of Skulls, Samedi, Kiasyd) present multiple mutually
  contradictory versions of their own bloodline's founding, explicitly
  refusing to resolve which is "true."
- **Creation via experimentation/ritual vs. natural Embrace**: Baali
  (Re-Embrace), Gargoyles (alchemical ritual), Kiasyd (Abyss-based
  transmutation), and Nagaraja (blood rites in Enoch) all involve
  bloodline-specific creation rites distinct from a normal Embrace.
- **Destruction/diminishment of a founding Clan**: Salubri (destroyed by
  Tremere), Harbingers of Skulls (their patriarch destroyed by the
  Giovanni), and True Brujah (violently supplanted by their own enraged
  childer) are all "fallen Clan" bloodlines mourning a lost golden age.
- **Withdrawal from the Jyhad as survival strategy**: True Brujah
  (deliberate withdrawal), Nagaraja (forced into isolation after Enoch's
  fall), and Salubri (hiding and quiet service) all frame avoiding
  Kindred power politics as necessary for survival.
- **The Tal'Mahe'Ra as hidden hand**: Both the Daughters of Cacophony
  (created as a Camarilla-destabilizing weapon) and the True Brujah
  (Western arm) tie back to this shadowy sect, suggesting a wider
  unseen conspiracy connecting multiple bloodlines.
- **Bodies marked by transformation**: Third eyes (Salubri), wings and
  memory loss (Gargoyles), rotting/putrefying flesh (Samedi, Nagaraja),
  masks over lost faces (Harbingers of Skulls) — physical marks of
  otherness recur as a way each bloodline is set apart from "ordinary"
  vampires.
- **Complicated relationships with the Giovanni**: Harbingers of Skulls
  (blood feud), Samedi (disputed pact, necromancy rivalry), and Nagaraja
  (historical persecution mentioned in passing) each define part of
  their identity in opposition to Clan Giovanni.
