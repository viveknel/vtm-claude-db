# Blood Sacrifice: The Thaumaturgy Companion — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.
Note: FTS5 treats hyphens specially, so searching the literal term
"Dur-An-Ki" can error out or return nothing. Search a distinctive
un-hyphenated word instead (e.g. "ashipu", "Sadhana", "wangateur").

## Overview
A 2002 White Wolf sourcebook (classic Revised Edition line, by Dean
Shomshak and Ari Marmell, developed by Justin Achilli) devoted to
non-Tremere blood sorcery: Akhu (Egyptian, practiced by the Followers of
Set), Dur-An-Ki (Middle Eastern, practiced chiefly by Assamite ashipu),
Sadhana (Indian, practiced by the Daitya and Danava "Brahmin" clans),
and Wanga (Afro-Caribbean, practiced across several clans and the Samedi
bloodline). An appendix adds Necromancy and Koldunic Sorcery rituals plus
a full writeup of Nahuallotl (the Tlacique bloodline's Aztec blood magic)
and a set of standalone "spontaneous talismans" horror vignettes. This
book is explicitly a direct companion to `secrets-of-thaumaturgy-revised`
(also in this library) — it patches an admitted omission from that book
(the missing Level Five Setite Sorcery power, "Temperance," for the Snake
Inside path) and repeatedly cross-references its Setite Sorcery, Assamite
Sorcery (Hunter's Winds), and Voudoun Necromancy material as the direct
prior installment in the same line.

## Prelude: Vendetta (pp. 9-11)
**Summary:** Fiction: the Lasombra Sabbat priestess Carlota, on a
vendetta raid against a Tremere sorcerer hiding in the Peruvian jungle,
loses two packmates to his magic before drawing on hidden Wanga
(Palo Mayombe) power — protected by the orisha/Enkisi Santisima
Muerte — to burn him alive. She wakes decapitated-but-animate, her
head kept alive on a shelf: her own ghoul Javier, newly Embraced by an
unnamed ancient sire in obsidian-knife Aztec/Mesoamerican trappings, has
betrayed and tortured her in revenge, with his new sire's magic somehow
restoring the ghoul's previously-broken wrist and keeping Carlota's
severed head sentient.
**Key themes:** vengeance and betrayal as a structuring device; blood
magic (Wanga specifically) as a source of power for characters otherwise
outmatched by Hermetic Thaumaturgy.
**Key terms/entities:** Carlota, Javier, Santisima Muerte, kisengue
(ritual shinbone implement); Javier's unnamed Mesoamerican-styled sire —
never confirmed as Tlacique in the text, but consistent with that
bloodline's aesthetic (see Appendix).

## Introduction: In Vitae Veritas (pp. 13-18)
**Summary:** Frames the book's thesis: despite Tremere self-mythology,
several other blood-sorcery traditions are just as old and potent.
Explains the book's four-chapter structure (Akhu, Dur-An-Ki, Sadhana,
Wanga) plus an appendix; discusses the social/practical difficulties of
learning non-Hermetic Thaumaturgy (finding a teacher, religious
requirements, research rules); gives full game mechanics for learning
times, creating new rituals, and creating new paths; and closes with a
glossary of terms (Akhu, Ashipu, Dur-An-Ki, Orisha, Sadhana, Sadhu,
Wanga, Wangateur, etc.) used throughout the book. Notes that "the
destruction of the Tremere antitribu" and "the civil war among the
Assamites" have broken the Tremere's Camarilla-wide magical monopoly,
forcing the Sabbat and Assamite refugees alike to seek alternative
sorcery — the same period this book's Chapters One-Four describe in
detail.
**Key themes:** Thaumaturgy as culturally specific rather than a single
unified system; the Tremere's anxious, ongoing project of absorbing every
other tradition into itself.
**Notable claims/data points:** states as established fact that "the
Assamites broke that curse after 500 years" and that the Tremere want to
learn how — i.e., in this 2002 book's continuity, the Assamite blood
curse has *already* been broken by the time of writing. This is a
significant contrast with `v20-rites-of-the-blood` (2014), where the
curse is still fully intact and Assamite Sorcerers are still centuries
into failing to break it — see Cross-cutting themes below.

## Chapter One: Akhu — The Divine Image (pp. 19-34)
**Summary:** Egyptian blood sorcery, practiced by "lector-priests"
(kher-heb) primarily of the Followers of Set. Covers Egyptian magical
theory in depth: the ethically-neutral nature of Egyptian magic; the
power of images/imitation (idols, tomb-paintings, effigies) and of
writing and True Names; the multi-part soul (ka, ba, ab, khaibit, ren,
sekhem, sahu, khu); the mythology of Osiris's murder and dismemberment
by Set as the template for Akhu's central "blasphemy-shrine" requirement
(a lector-priest must ritually desecrate a corpse and grave goods monthly
to draw power from Amenti, the Western Lands, or lose the ability to
cast Akhu magic at all); notable gods (Ra, Osiris, Isis, Horus, Thoth,
Heka, Ptah, Khnum, Amun, Anubis, Sebek, Taweret, Bes, Sekhmet, Seker) and
their magical associations; the legendary, likely-hoaxed Book of Thoth;
and the Council of Seven's 1930 ban on Tremere research into Akhu,
lifted in 1973 by Muharram Rasul ibn Babar, Pontifex of Antioch. Presents
two full paths — Divine Hand (sympathetic/voodoo-doll magic using wax
effigies, escalating from small objects to affecting spirits worldwide)
and Path of Duat (mental/hypnotic torment drawing on the Egyptian
underworld's specific torments: serpent hallucinations, blindness,
suffocation, paralysis) — plus the Ushabti servant-creation path and a
Typhon's Brew/Milk of Set alchemy ritual catalog. Explicitly
cross-references `secrets-of-thaumaturgy-revised` for the Dry Nile and
Snake Inside paths, and other supplements for several renamed-but-
equivalent Akhu versions of common Thaumaturgy paths.
**Key themes:** magic as inseparable from religious devotion (a
lector-priest's power comes from stolen Osirian/Setian power, not
Hermetic will alone); the historical marginalization of Akhu by
Tremere-dominated European magic, now reversing as Tremere seek to learn
it.
**Key terms/entities:** blasphemy-shrine, the Book of Going Forth by
Night, the Book of Thoth, kher-heb/lector-priest; ka/ba/ab/khaibit/ren/
sekhem/sahu/khu (the multi-part soul); akhu/mut/bau/sebau (spirit
classes); Ushabti (servant constructs, up to and including the Gift of
Khnum, which can raise the dead or release demons into a crafted body).

## Chapter Two: Dur-An-Ki — The Demon-Haunted World (pp. 35-53)
**Summary:** Middle Eastern blood sorcery ("Master of Heaven and
Earth"), practiced by ashipu, chiefly but not exclusively Assamite.
Traces Dur-An-Ki's roots through Sumeria, Akkad, Assyria, Babylon,
Zoroastrianism, and the Greco-Roman Mithraic mysteries (a small school
of Ventrue ashipu re-enact the Mithraic tauroctony/bull-slaying as part
of their training). Explains the covenant theory of magic (a spirit or
god's ancient promise to a mortal prophet or people, which an ashipu can
invoke as leverage — "I met your conditions, now pay up," contrasted
explicitly with an infernalist's abject "I'll give you anything...
master"); the drug kalif (vitae-fed hashish) and the visionary "Ladder of
Heaven"/"Station of Ultimate Rapture" ascent that Assamite sorcerers use
to bargain with ever-higher spirits, having so far reached only the
"Sphere of Mercury" (which grants access to global electronic
communications) after millennia of climbing; alternative trance
techniques (ecstatic dance, self-inflicted pain, meditation, art);
astrology (including the complication of the three modern "extra"
planets); and Middle Eastern amulet/talisman traditions (cylinder seals,
incantation bowls, hand-amulets, paper talismans). Names Amr al-Ashrad as
the most powerful known ashipu, commanding a cadre of djinn and afrits.
Gives a detailed catalog of Middle Eastern spirits and demons (djinn,
divs, Maskim/Rabishu/Gallu/Utukku, Girtablullû, Shedu/Lamassu, Ugallu,
Uridimmu, Shedim, Lilin) and notes the recent Tremere recruitment of the
Malkavian sorceress Madame Yacine in 1974 Lyon as a rare success story.
**Key themes:** power through negotiation and covenant rather than
submission (the ashipu's relationship with spirits is explicitly
contrasted with both Hermetic abstraction and infernal servitude);
Dur-An-Ki as historically isolated from the West by the Crusades and
Islam's rise, only now becoming accessible to Tremere study.
**Key terms/entities:** ashipu, kalif, Ladder of Heaven, Station of
Ultimate Rapture, Sphere of Mercury; Amr al-Ashrad; Madame Yacine.
**Notable claims/data points:** reprises the "Assamites broke that curse
after 500 years" claim from the Introduction, adding that "dozens of
Assamite sorcerers fled their Middle Eastern homelands" amid a "recent
schism among the Assamite clan" — an early (2002) reference to the
Assamite civil war that `v20-lore-clans` (2011) later develops fully as
the "Preservationist Conspiracy," including that book's own Amr
al-Ashrad appearance as a Sorcerer-caste leader alongside Tegyrius.

## Chapter Three: Sadhana — A Meditation Upon Blood (pp. 55-70)
**Summary:** Indian blood sorcery ("Gaining"), practiced by rakta-sadhus
(chiefly the Brahmin-caste Daitya and Danava clans, India's Followers of
Set and Ventrue equivalents). Covers Hindu cosmology (maya/illusion,
karma/samsara, moksha/liberation), the historical arc from Vedic
sacrificial Brahminism through ascetic ("tapas") traditions to Buddhism's
rejection of both; sacrificial ritual technique (fire offerings, soma,
the demand for letter-perfect execution); asceticism and raktayana (the
vampiric adaptation of pranayama breath-work, since the undead have no
prana of their own and must move blood through spinal chakras instead);
mantras, mudras, and meditation; the caste system's Kindred parallel
(only Brahmin-lineage vampires may normally learn Sadhana, though ascetic
"sannyasa" renunciates of any caste can also qualify); notable gods
(Agni, Ganesha, Varuna, Kubera, Nirriti, Brihaspati, Vishnu, Shiva/
Bhairava/Mahakala) and demon/spirit classes (asuras, rakshasas, yakshas,
pisachas, apsarases, gandharvas). Describes the "Trimira" — Indian
Tremere who defy the Brahmin caste restriction and teach Sadhana to
non-Brahmin lineages willing to accept a blood bond. Notes a centuries-
long war between India's Kindred and the "asuratizayya" ("demon horde")
— the local term for Cathayans/kuei-jin, the Eastern vampires covered
from their own side in this library's `wind-from-the-east` bundle — and
a 1999 "invasion of pisachas" that multiple supernatural factions are
working to contain.
**Key themes:** liberation/godhood as the shared goal beneath very
different ritual technique from the other three traditions; caste and
lineage restrictions as a recurring social barrier to learning blood
magic across this whole book (paralleled by Wanga's faith requirement in
Chapter Four).
**Key terms/entities:** rakta-sadhu, siddhis, tantras; raktayana, chakras;
Daitya, Danava, Trimira; asuratizayya/Cathayans.

## Chapter Four: Wanga — Legacy of the Caribbean (pp. 71-85)
**Summary:** Afro-Caribbean blood sorcery, rooted in Yoruba religion
(Olodumare, the supreme "owner of all destinies," and his orisha
intermediaries who channel ashé, the living spiritual energy vampires
drain as blood) and its New World transformations into voudoun, Santería,
Palo Mayombe, and related faiths after the slave trade. Explains how
enslaved Yoruba, Ewe, and Fon peoples adapted their religion under
Christian pressure (orishas becoming "masks" of Catholic saints) rather
than abandoning it, and traces the Yoruba Kindred's fall from regional
dominance to the transatlantic slave trade and internal warfare.
Discusses the three major traditions in turn — voudoun (loa in rada/
petro/ghede families; Legba, Damballah, Baron Samedi, Erzulie), Santería
(orishas Eleggua, Oggún, Changó, Oshún), and Palo Mayombe (Enkisi
Zarabanda, El Cristo Negro, Santisima Muerte, San Simon) — and states
that Wanga's specifically Kindred/Afro-Caribbean form (as opposed to the
older African-only Yoruba version) originated with the **Samedi
bloodline**: the houngan **Papa Zombie**, first known to other Kindred in
1764, taught it to select students without ever confirming whether he
himself had a Cainite teacher. Names Louis Doumer Millerand (a French
Tremere who studied Yoruba magic in Benin in 1794 while fleeing the
Reign of Terror) and his childe Clarice Fontaine (a leading Tremere
wangateur, known for freely mixing spirits from five different religions
in a single ritual) as the Tremere's own entry points into the
tradition. Gives full paths (the Flow of Ashé, which uniquely does not
require blood expenditure; Orisha's Fortune) and extensive tool/
vocabulary glossaries (asson, ekwele, farine, gris-gris, hounfour,
kisengue, nganga, peristyle, poteau mitan, veve).
**Key themes:** faith as a hard prerequisite for magical efficacy (a
faithless practitioner suffers a mechanical, near-guaranteed-botch
penalty) — Wanga is presented as the least negotiable, most
religiously demanding tradition in the book; the porousness between
Wanga and Voudoun Necromancy in practitioners' own minds, even though
the game treats them as fully separate Disciplines.
**Key terms/entities:** ashé, orisha, loa, Enkisi; Papa Zombie, Samedi
bloodline; Louis Doumer Millerand, Clarice Fontaine; kisengue (also
appearing in the Prelude, confirming Carlota's Palo Mayombe practice).

## Appendix: Mysterious Ways (pp. 86-98)
**Summary:** A grab-bag for Storytellers only (the book explicitly warns
players away from reading it). Opens with new Necromancy rituals suited
to Voudoun Necromancy or standard Giovanni practice (Part the Veil, Eyes
of the Dead, Garb of Hades) and new Koldunic Sorcery rituals (Reflections,
Earth's Embrace, Gaze of the Gorgon — full petrification). Gives a full
writeup of **Nahuallotl**, the Aztec/Mesoamerican blood sorcery of the
**Tlacique** bloodline (children of Tezcatlipoca): their history as
open, worshipped rulers in the pre-Conquest New World; their doomed
alliance first hoped-for with the Camarilla, then actually made with the
Sabbat against the Spanish and Camarilla; the Sabbat's subsequent
near-extermination and diablerie of the Tlacique elders once the
Camarilla had been driven out; and their current state as a
believed-extinct bloodline secretly rebuilding across Central/South
America and the American Southwest, launching guerrilla strikes on
Sabbat enclaves and cautiously exploring a still-tentative alliance with
the Pisanob (the Giovanni's Mesoamerican necromancer family) against
their shared Sabbat enemy. Gives full Nahuallotl paths (Lash of Xipe
Totec, escalating from pain-transfer to lethal ranged wounds) and
rituals (Mirror of the Gods; Shroud of Day, a stake enchantment used to
render staked vampires unaware time has passed; Severance, which can
remove and preserve a vampire's heart or head as an independently
animate, controllable vulnerability). Closes with a set of standalone
"spontaneous talismans" — in-universe reports of magic items with no
discoverable Thaumaturgical logic, framed as a Tremere researcher's
long letter to her sire: Katrina's Cross (a crucifix that slowly devours
its owner's flesh while granting Virtue/Stamina bonuses, traced back to
a murder victim's stolen heirloom) and an unfinished diary recounting a
housekeeper's obsession with a forbidden mirror in 1864.
**Key themes:** the limits of Tremere systematization (spontaneous
talismans exist entirely outside the Principles/paths framework the rest
of the book relies on); the Tlacique as a slow-burn revenge plot several
editions in the making.
**Key terms/entities:** Nahuallotl, Tlacique, Pisanob (tentative
alliance); Katrina's Cross; spontaneous talismans.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **This book is the primary classic-era source for much of
  `v20-rites-of-the-blood`'s Setite/Assamite Sorcery material.** The
  overlap is extensive and often near-verbatim in concept: Typhon's Brew,
  the Ladder of Heaven/Station of Ultimate Rapture/Sphere of Mercury
  progression, kalif, ashipu, Amr al-Ashrad, wangateur terminology, and
  Papa Zombie of the Samedi bloodline all appear in both books, with
  `v20-rites-of-the-blood`'s 2014 treatment reading as a modernized,
  condensed update of this 2002 book's much fuller treatment. When a
  question concerns the *origin* or fuller cultural/historical context
  of Akhu, Dur-An-Ki, Sadhana, or Wanga, this book is the deeper source;
  `v20-rites-of-the-blood` is the modern-nights status update.
- **The Assamite blood curse's timeline gets another data point — and it
  points the opposite direction from `v20-rites-of-the-blood`.** This
  book states plainly, twice, that the Assamites "broke that curse after
  500 years" and that the Tremere are trying to reverse-engineer *how*
  they did it — meaning that in 2002, the curse was already-broken
  continuity. `secrets-of-thaumaturgy-revised` (2000) independently
  supports this with its own "curse collapsed overnight" claim. But
  `v20-rites-of-the-blood` (2014) treats the curse as still fully intact,
  with Assamite Sorcerers centuries into failing to break it via this
  same Ladder of Heaven. Combined with `v20-lore-clans`' "three competing
  explanations" for how Assamite antitribu specifically broke free, this
  gives at least four different, unreconciled positions across three
  books spanning 2000-2014 on the Assamite curse's status — see this
  library's `library_index.md` "Assamite blood curse" section, which
  should be read alongside this entry.
- **Tremere absorption-by-conquest as the book's throughline**: every
  chapter follows the same beat — the Tremere discover a foreign
  tradition, cannot fully replicate it, and pursue it anyway through
  Embracing native practitioners, diplomacy, theft, or torture (Madame
  Yacine, the Trimira, Louis Doumer Millerand/Clarice Fontaine, the 1930
  Akhu ban and its 1973 reversal). This book's Introduction states the
  thesis outright: Thaumaturgy's claimed universality is Tremere
  propaganda, and the clan's actual relationship to these older
  traditions is closer to an ongoing, incomplete conquest.
- **The Tlacique/Pisanob connection extends this library's existing
  Necromancy thread.** `v20-lore-clans` already establishes the Pisanob
  as a Mesoamerican Giovanni family "currently under attack from the
  Harbingers of Skulls," and `v20-rites-of-the-blood` gives that conflict
  a "stalemate" status update. This book adds a third thread: the
  Tlacique's own near-extermination by the Sabbat and their tentative,
  still-unrealized alliance overture to the Pisanob — a alliance that, if
  it ever solidified, none of this library's other books currently
  confirm or deny.
