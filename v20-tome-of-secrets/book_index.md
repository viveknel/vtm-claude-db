# Tome of Secrets — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes or precise facts, query
book_chunks.db (see README.md in this bundle for query examples) rather
than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
A 2016 White Wolf/Onyx Path sourcebook, funded via the same Kickstarter
that produced `v20-dark-ages` and expanding directly on it — "a love letter
to fans of V20 Dark Ages" rather than a standalone setting book. It is a
grab-bag of Kickstarter stretch-goal content: connected serial fiction (a
crusader company that stops needing blood, chasing a moving city called
Scáth), new blood-sorcery material (Abyss Mysticism, Assamite Sorcery,
Koldunism, Necromancy, Setite Sorcery, mortal Witchcraft), a feudalism/
warfare toolkit, and a True Faith/infernalism expansion. Deeply
interconnected with named V20 Dark Ages canon figures (Etrius, Myca Vykos,
Lucita de Aragon, Jürgen von Verden, Rosamund of Islington) via its opening
fiction's in-universe letters.

## Chapter One: The Dark Medieval World (pp. 8-30)
**Summary:** Entirely narrative/fiction — no crunch. Opens with a short
"What You'll Find Inside" roadmap (pp.8-9) before "**The Bloodless
Crusade: The Creidne Diaries**" (pp.10-22), a first-person journal by a
vampire named Creidne, one of a growing coterie who — after receiving a
mysterious gift from a shapeshifting, dream-haunting entity — stop needing
blood entirely and instead hunger only "for the road, for the quest."
Guided across Dublin, Edinburgh, London (where they slaughter a
congregation persecuting them as "demons"), Toulouse, Toledo, Lisbon,
Cordoba, Granada (where they black out for a month and wake having fed
ravenously, contradicting their fast), Valencia, Marseilles, Paris,
Cologne, Magdeburg, and Nuremberg, the coterie chases rumors of a moving
city called **Scáth** and a prophesied "angel" who will end their
crusade. The diary is annotated throughout by a later researcher, Eudocia
Melachrina, whose footnotes cast doubt on Creidne's reliability (she may
have fed and simply forgotten) and who ultimately recommends the whole
dossier be sealed away as dangerous. A second document set, "**The
Derinkuyu Letters**" (pp.23-30), collects intercepted/stolen
correspondence from figures reacting to the Crusade's passage — including
Tremere's own **Etrius** (requesting battle intelligence against the
Ruthven Tzimisce and mentioning a crusader called "Umbri" who doesn't
feed), the Tzimisce elder **Myca Vykos** (twice — first fearing pursuit by
six-fingered shadow-figures after acquiring "Irad's brick," later calling
a summit of blood-sorcery scholars to study the fasting phenomenon), the
Toreador **Lucita de Aragon** (ordering the elimination of a clan-mate,
Augustine, traveling with the pilgrims — he is later confirmed dead in
Valencia), **Rosamund of Islington** (a Toreador warning a Setite convert
to flee before a coming purge of "low clans," and linking the pilgrims to
a series of end-times omens including the fall of "the Dream" in
Constantinople), a Setite named **Andreas Aegyptus** discussing the fall
of Beshter and the Dream at the hands of the Baali and involving Anna
Comnena, and **Teagan, Watcher of Zao-lat** (a Salubri-lineage figure
offering her clan's story to a mortal folklorist/vampire-watcher).
**Key themes:** the Beast/hunger as the core of vampiric identity — this
entire arc is a thought experiment about what happens to Cainites who lose
it; unreliable narration (Eudocia's footnotes) as the same structural
device the wider V20 line uses elsewhere; the fiction is deliberately left
unresolved (Eudocia can find no trace of what happened at Genoa, the
crusade's stated destination).
**Key terms/entities:** Creidne, Eudocia Melachrina, Scáth, the "angel"/
shapeshifting dream figure, "The Consumption"; Etrius, Myca Vykos, Lucita
de Aragon, Rosamund of Islington, Andreas Aegyptus, Teagan/Watcher of
Zao-lat, Jürgen von Verden (mentioned via his childe Magda), Augustine
(a Toreador pilgrim, confirmed killed in Valencia).
**Notable claims/data points:** this chapter's letters place the fall of
"the Dream" (Constantinople) and Beshter's death at Baali hands within the
same rough timeframe as the Bloodless Crusade (1242-1243) — directly
relevant to `v20-dark-ages-companion`'s fuller account of the Dream's
collapse; Myca Vykos references "Irad's brick," tying a physical artifact
to Caine's second childe and the founding of Brujah/Cappadocian/Lasombra/
Ventrue clan-blood, per the Book of Nod.

## Chapter Two: Forgotten Sorceries (pp. 32-77)
**Summary:** New blood-sorcery crunch across five traditions, each
expanding a `v20-dark-ages` core-book system rather than replacing it.
**Abyss Mysticism** (Lasombra, pp.32-38): Magisters who "use the Abyss
within" rather than merely manipulating shadow, including the Oubliette
(a new Background for constructing lightless labyrinthine prisons/
meditation-chambers) and a bestiary of native Abyss entities (the
limbless, all-grasping Blatherskite; the parasitic Infantile Bezoar that
grows in a vampire's heart after drinking in the Abyss; the riddling
Umbriferous Man; the mounted Minacious Legion; the devouring Ingurgitant
Vortex) plus Level One-Five rituals up to Boukephos' Chosen Oubliette
(which once consumed an entire town, Latava). **Assamite Sorcery**
(pp.38-44): a fuller treatment of Alamut, the viziers' hidden mountain
city, its Chamber of Elders and sacred Pool, the generational conflict
between elder viziers and younger sorcerers, and the Keepers of the Pool
path plus Level One-Five rituals. **Koldunic Sorcery** (pp.44-51):
Tzimisce Voivode Count Vladimir Rustovich's call to reunite the fractured
practice against a Tremere/Fiefdoms of the Black Cross alliance, the rival
"Many-Headed Seekers" faction, and a new "Bialowieza Kraina" with rituals
(Truth in Water, Servitor's Perception, Borne by Wind, Autumn's Armor,
Concentrated Vitae, Master of the Domain, and others). **Necromancy**
(pp.52-60): Cappadocian/Giovanni/Laibon material including the Path of
Skulls and Path of Woe. **Setite Sorcery** (pp.60-68): new Merits/Flaws
(Heretical Sympathizer, Hunted by the Hierophants, Postulant of Echidna)
and three new Revelations paths tied to different mythologies — Duat
(Egyptian), Eden, and Midgard (Norse, including Apep's Transfiguration,
which turns a loyal cultist into a giant serpent-ghoul). **Witchcraft**
(pp.68-77): an extensive treatment of mortal magic in the Dark Medieval
world — the class divide between elite "high magic" Fellowships (the
Tremere's mortal cousins in the Order of Hermes, the Messianic Voices,
the Ahl-i-Batin, the Craftmasons) and folk "low magic"/witchcraft
traditions (Spirit-Talkers, Valdaermen, the Old Faith); Folk Wisdom and
Incantations as two tiers of non-Awakened magic; what mortal witches lose
or retain if Embraced or ghouled.
**Key themes:** every tradition here is explicitly framed as expanding,
not superseding, `v20-dark-ages`'s existing treatment — this book adds
depth to established systems rather than introducing new ones from
scratch; body horror and the price of power recur across Abyss Mysticism,
Setite Sorcery, and Koldunic Sorcery alike.
**Key terms/entities:** Oubliette (Background), Blatherskite, Infantile
Bezoar, Umbriferous Man, Minacious Legion, Ingurgitant Vortex, Boukephos;
Alamut, Chamber of Elders, Keepers of the Pool; Count Vladimir Rustovich,
the Many-Headed Seekers, Bialowieza Kraina; Revelations of Duat/Eden/
Midgard, Apep's Transfiguration; Order of Hermes, Ahl-i-Batin, Craftmasons,
Spirit-Talkers, Valdaermen, the Old Faith.
**Notable claims/data points:** the Koldunic Sorcery section explicitly
frames Rustovich's push to reunify the kraina as leverage in the
Tzimisce/Tremere War of Princes over Gargoyle-servant Fiefdoms of the
Black Cross — directly continuous with `v20-dark-ages`'s and this
library's existing Tremere/Tzimisce Gargoyle-war material.

## Chapter Three: The Weight of Nobility (pp. 78-99)
**Summary:** A feudalism and warfare toolkit. **Fiefdoms and Vassalage**
(pp.78-81) provides Background-sharing mechanics for liege/vassal
relationships (a new Vassal Social Merit, rules for lieges granting
Anchor Backgrounds to vassals at favorable exchange rates, and the mutual
"Terms" oath structure), explicitly drawing the parallel between mortal
feudalism, the Camarilla's Traditions, and sire/childe bonds. **Knightly
Orders** (pp.82-90) covers both mortal knighthood's evolution and
"the Cainite Knight" — vampire orders that share Disciplines across clan
lines (explicitly forbidden to Tremere), with new combination
Disciplines like Dark Father's Stigmata (Cainite Heresy-linked,
Obtenebration+Vicissitude) and Eye for the Weakness of Steel (from the
now-collapsed Fellowship of Our Lady). **Warfare** (pp.90-96) offers a
streamlined mass-combat system for the Storyteller System, covering why
medieval wars start (conquest, debt, succession disputes) and how to run
large battles without granular unit-tracking.
**Key themes:** medieval social structures as a direct mirror of Kindred
power structures (sire/childe as liege/vassal); knightly orders as one of
the few sanctioned spaces where Disciplines cross clan lines.
**Key terms/entities:** Vassal Merit, Anchor Background, Terms (oath
structure); Path of Chivalry variants for knightly orders; Dark Father's
Stigmata, Eye for the Weakness of Steel; the collapsed Fellowship of Our
Lady (1218).

## Chapter Four: Faith (pp. 100-115)
**Summary:** Expands True Faith and its dark mirror. **The Holiest of
Things** (pp.100-106) gives storytelling guidance for True Faith as
belief rather than knowledge (a Follower of Set's sincere faith in Set as
a benevolent god is presented as a valid, mechanically-supported example),
an optional "Aspects of Faith" rule tying relic potency to use consistent
with its founding purpose, five example Holy Artifacts with full
narrative backstories (the Finger Bone of Sofia, the Water of Meriba, the
Hand of Nikolas, the Caul of Jos, the Mercy of Ra), a note on "Biblical
Necromancy" practiced by self-styled sanctified necromancers like the
Rosselini lineage, and an extended writeup of **the Faithful of Nikolas**
— a Nosferatu-founded cult of kindness and self-forgiveness martyred by
the Lasombra inquisitor-sponsor Ambrosio Luis Monçada, now split into
three rival factions (the relic-guarding Guardians of the Hand, the
top-down "Bishop" Sanchez's Faithful of the Bishop, and the egalitarian
Friends of Delena) with their own combination Disciplines. **The
Unholiest of Things** (pp.106-115) covers demonic patronage (new
Merits/Flaws: Blasphemous Pact, Demonic Patron, Profane Trappings, Unholy
Stain) and **Dark Thaumaturgy**, tracing its taboo status to the Assamite
sorcerer Ur-Shulgi (said to have fallen torpid after probing it too
deeply) and noting it is most practiced by Baali, unmentored young
Tremere, and Carthage-descended Assamite/Brujah lines — with the Setites
positioned as the clan most contemptuous of it despite their own
occult dealings.
**Key themes:** True Faith and infernalism as structural mirrors of each
other (both require genuine belief/commitment, both reshape the
believer, both are agnostic about which specific religion or entity is
involved); faith-based cults as a recurring source of political splinter
factions (the Faithful of Nikolas' three-way split echoes the library's
broader "unreliable, plural" factionalism theme).
**Key terms/entities:** Aspects of Faith; Finger Bone of Sofia, Water of
Meriba, Hand of Nikolas, Caul of Jos, Mercy of Ra; Rosselini lineage
(Biblical Necromancy); the Faithful of Nikolas and its three factions;
Ur-Shulgi; Dark Thaumaturgy's clan associations (Baali, Tremere novices,
Carthage-descended Assamites/Brujah).

## Appendix: Player Options (pp. 116-119)
**Summary:** A short grab-bag of fan-submitted combination Disciplines
and rituals, framed as "unique expressions of the Cainite curse developed
by legendary monsters of the Dark Medieval World" — including Bulgroth's
Exquisite Torture (Daimonion+Temporis, from a Baali Methuselah), Livia
Yorke's Ouroboros (Serpentis+Vicissitude, a Tzimisce/Setite hybrid power
that grows a symbiotic serpent-creature inside a mortal host), and
several shorter powers (McShaw's Grace, Nikolai Steen's Acuity, The Rod
Raw Rending). This is reference/crunch material; query the chunk database
directly for specific power text.
**Key themes:** named-inventor "legendary monster" framing as a
lightweight way to seed new NPCs alongside new mechanics.
**Key terms/entities:** Bulgroth (Baali Methuselah), Livia Yorke
(Tzimisce), the Ouroboros creature.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **This book is explicitly a companion, not a standalone setting book**:
  every mechanical chapter (Two through Four) expands a system already
  present in `v20-dark-ages` rather than introducing a new one — Abyss
  Mysticism, Assamite Sorcery, Koldunism, Necromancy, and Setite Sorcery
  all exist in the core book already; this book adds new paths, rituals,
  and lore depth to each. Treat it the same way this library treats
  `v20-dark-ages-companion`: a direct expansion, not an independent
  source, though covering different material (blood sorcery and social
  systems rather than domains).
- **The Bloodless Crusade as a deliberately unresolved mystery**: unlike
  most plot hooks in this library's other books, the Crusade's fate at
  Genoa is never revealed even to the in-universe researcher compiling
  the documents (Eudocia recommends sealing the whole dossier away). A
  future session should not assume this thread has a "true" answer
  recoverable from this book alone.
- **Named V20 Dark Ages canon figures as a connective thread**: Etrius,
  Myca Vykos, Lucita de Aragon, and Rosamund of Islington all appear
  in-character in the Derinkuyu Letters, placing the Bloodless Crusade
  and the Dream of Constantinople's collapse in the same rough historical
  window (see `v20-dark-ages-companion`'s Constantinople chapter for the
  fuller "Dream" account this book's letters gesture toward).
