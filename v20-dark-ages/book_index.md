# Vampire: The Dark Ages 20th Anniversary Edition — Index

## How to use this file

## Overview
V20 Dark Ages is a standalone core rulebook for Vampire: The Masquerade
set in 1242 CE ("the Dark Medieval World"), roughly a millennium before
the modern V20 setting. It shares the modern game's core cosmology
(Caine, the thirteen Clans, Generation, the Beast) but reflects an era
before the Camarilla and Sabbat existed as such — vampire society is
instead organized around the informal, escalating "War of Princes"
among elder rulers, and several Clans/factions extinct or diminished
by modern V20 (the Cappadocians, the still-unified Salubri) are here
active political players. Chapters follow a similar structure to the
core V20 book — setting/cosmology, Clans and bloodlines, morality
("Roads" rather than Paths), full game mechanics, Storyteller advice,
world geography, and appendices — narrated throughout by period-
appropriate in-character voices (most notably "Grandmother Penne," a
Nosferatu elder) rather than a single omniscient narrator. As with the
core V20 book, mechanically dense chapters (Four, Five, Six, Seven) are
noted but not summarized power-by-power; lore-dense chapters and
sections (Introduction, One, Two, Three, Nine, and Appendix B) are
covered in full detail below.

## Front Matter (pp. 1-12)
Standard credits, table of contents, dedication. No lore content.

## Introduction (pp. 13-21)
**Summary:** Frames V20 Dark Ages as a standalone product set in 1242 CE ("the Dark Medieval World"), styled for evocative authenticity rather than strict historical accuracy. Repeats the "true/false" vampire mythology primer (immortality, blood-drinking, no literal demonic possession, sunlight vulnerability, mostly-false religious-symbol vulnerability except for True Faith, stake paralysis not death, real but variable supernatural power, and explicitly affirms vampires have and enjoy sex, though sex requires expending blood for bodily functions like real humans). Reiterates the Caine/Second Generation/Third Generation Clan-founding myth as the setting's near-universal in-period belief. The bulk of the Introduction is an extended, second-person literary set-piece walking through the "Hunger → Hunt → Feeding → Denouement" cycle as the single unifying experience of every vampire regardless of Clan, rendered in visceral sensory prose (contrasting remembered mortal eating with the wordless, gnawing vampiric Hunger; the stalking Hunt as heightened-sense predation; the ecstatic-but-violent Feeding itself; the emotional crash and existential loneliness of the Denouement). Provides a full chapter-by-chapter roadmap for the book.
**Key themes:** the Hunger cycle as the setting's true universal structure beneath politics and Clan, feeding as simultaneously intimate and violent, cyclical inescapability of vampiric nature.
**Key terms/entities:** the Hunger/Hunt/Feeding/Denouement cycle (p.15-19), Caine's Brood mythology (p.14).
## Chapter One: A Place In Time (pp. 22-39)
**Summary:** Narrated by "Grandmother Penne," a Nosferatu elder of London, addressing a newly Embraced childe throughout — a device that grounds cosmology in a specific, weary, street-wise voice rather than an omniscient overview. Opens with a fictional excerpt from "A History of Ash and Bone" by Eudocia Melachrina, framing all Cainite history as unreliable and contested even by those who lived it (using Prince Mithras of London's disputed claim to be the historical founder of Mithraism as an example). Grandmother Penne then walks through: the Embrace and its physical transformation (heightened senses, no heartbeat except during bloodlust, bloody tears, superhuman resilience — true stake-paralysis vs. false decapitation-myths, though beheading is in fact lethal); the Beast as simultaneously protective instinct and destroyer of the human soul; vampire social structure via age-tiers (Fledgling, Neonate, Ancilla, Elder, Methuselah, Antediluvian) each given a personal, cynical gloss; the High Clans/Low Clans distinction as a regionally-variable social hierarchy (illustrated via Clan Ventrue's dominance and Clan Nosferatu's low status specifically in London, credited to Roman vs. Celtic historical influence); and "Roads" as the Dark Medieval World's term for Paths of Enlightenment/morality systems (Grandmother Penne follows the "Road of the Prodigal," roughly parallel to Humanity). Presents the Traditions with parallel quoted translations from multiple named in-universe sources (The God-Regent Translations, the Qaanoon, Traditsiya, the Ways of Hibernia, the Lost Traditions of Phoenicia, the Traditions of Mali Empire) to reinforce the "every culture has a different version" theme — the Sixth Tradition here is named "The Silence of the Blood" rather than "Destruction." Covers city governance with period-appropriate titles: Prince (or Prince Regent), Chamberlain (political fixer/gatekeeper, sometimes the true power behind a weak Prince), Sheriff (enforcer, sometimes overlapping with elder-vassal structures), Keeper (maintains Elysium, described as a nearly impossible, high-turnover job), plus regional variants (Athens' Triumvirate/vampire democracy; a Mongolian court with a separate Sheriff for the council of elders; London's "Rat Catcher"; Quanzhou's "Murder of Harpies" — an elected gossip-council in high-population cities; and the "Scourge," a secret Prince's assassin). Notes a Holy Land Regent-Prince so powerful that elders past their first century are forbidden from meeting each other in his domain, on pain of a forced duel to Final Death.

Covers Caine mythology ("Brother Slays Brother" — presented as the dominant Christendom account but explicitly flagged as one of several competing origin stories worldwide) and Generation mechanics in period terms: Second Generation (Caine's direct childer, presumed destroyed in the Flood, though some Clans dispute this), Third Generation/Antediluvians (Clan founders, believed responsible for orchestrating the War of Princes from the shadows), Fourth-Fifth Generation/Methuselahs ("the Knights" of the Antediluvians' game), Sixth-Seventh (typical elder-rank vampires), Eighth-Tenth (ancillae), Eleventh-Twelfth (neonates, outnumbering elders roughly 3:1), and unnamed-further "Thin Blood" (can't sire further childer, treated as a sign of the coming Gehenna and hunted/culled). Explains the Amaranth (diablerie) in the same hushed, taboo-laden terms as the core book. Introduces "Wassail" — the Dark Medieval term for a vampire's final, irreversible Beast-driven frenzy/collapse into pure destruction (with a specific unconfirmed legend of a cunning, still-strategic Wassail-locked monster in northern forests) — and reiterates Golconda as a rumored, dangerous, and possibly Antediluvian-suppressed transcendent state. Closes with a survey of other supernatural dangers (Hunters/Inquisition, werewolves/Lupines, witches — described as resentful, mortal, and just as likely to exploit vampires as ally with them — and the "Shining Ones," an unnamed-taboo reference to fae/nature spirits) plus a full glossary of period-specific vampire terminology (Lextalionis, the Erciyes Fragments, the Long Night, the War of Ages vs. War of Princes, Laibon, consanguineous, domitor, thrall, etc.).
**Key themes:** unreliable/plural history as explicit narrative principle (mirrored from the modern-day V20 books), regional relativism (High/Low Clan status, city governance, Tradition translations all vary by culture), age and Generation as double-edged status, period-appropriate reframing of familiar V20 concepts (Roads for Paths, Wassail for total Beast-loss, the Silence of the Blood for the Masquerade).
**Key terms/entities:** Grandmother Penne (narrator, p.22), the Traditions with multi-source translations (p.27-30), High/Low Clans (p.26-27), Wassail (p.34), the War of Princes vs. War of Ages (p.24, glossary p.37), Erciyes Fragments (p.36), Shining Ones (p.36), full Lexicon (p.36-37).

## Chapter Two: The Clans of Caine (pp. 40-109)
**Summary:** Covers thirteen Clans as they exist in 1242 — notably including the Cappadocians and Salubri as active (if waning) Clans rather than historical footnotes, since this predates their destruction depicted in the modern-day V20 books. Opens explaining the High/Low Clan system as fluid and regional (Ventrue and Lasombra are High almost everywhere; Nosferatu is Low almost everywhere; but the Followers of Set are High in Cairo specifically) and notes the Tremere's controversial status: they only became a recognized Clan in 1133 after committing Amaranth upon the Salubri founder Saulot's torpor-bound corpse, artificially creating a "Third Generation" founder for themselves.

**Assamites** (pp.42-43): Called "Children of Haqim" or the derogatory "Saracens," based at the mountain fortress Alamut; organized into three cooperating branches — sorcerers (keep communication and clan logistics), warriors (judge and punish "unworthy" Cainites, sometimes via diablerie), and viziers (public-facing ambassadors/information gatherers, most commonly encountered by other Clans). Increasingly linked with Islam among younger members, to older elders' disdain (who see mortal religion as a distraction from Haqim's judgment-focused code).

**Brujah** (pp.44-45): Founding myth reframed for this era — the Brujah Antediluvian ("Brujah") was passionate and prone to frenzy; his childe Troile was calm and logical, and in a climactic argument Brujah attacked Troile in a rage, prompting Troile to diablerize his own sire (framed here as an act of self-defense escalating into consumption). Caine cursed Troile's resulting line with tripled inherited passion. Troile later built a Carthage-like utopia that was destroyed by a Ventrue/Malkavian-incited fifty-year war, cementing Brujah/Ventrue enmity; Troile's own fate since is unknown. Modern (1242) Brujah are disorganized zealots/philosopher-kings prone to salons, cliques, and rivalries rather than unified action.

**Cappadocians** (pp.46-47): Still a fully active Clan (pre-destruction) — death-obsessed scholar-mystics practicing Necromancy, led by the reclusive founder Cappadocius, who dispenses cryptic wisdom (ranging from research directives to apocalyptic visions of underground utopias) to Clan elders. Hold a decennial grand meeting at the temple-city Erciyes (Cappadocius's own foundation), home to the Erciyes Fragments — the most complete surviving version of the Book of Nod. All Cappadocians bear an increasingly corpselike, ashen appearance with age that cannot be disguised via vitae expenditure (a visible Clan weakness). Frequently serve as advisors/seneschals to political power (e.g., Lord Camden, Chamberlain of London) rather than seeking rulership themselves.

**Gangrel** (pp.48-49): "Outlaws" or "Wolf's-Heads" — wilderness-dwelling loners driven from society in both mortal and vampire life, thriving on self-determination and territorial independence rather than any Clan-wide hierarchy (explicitly "there is no organization universally accepted or practiced within the clan"). Embrace is described as intentionally abrupt/brutal, akin to a predator's kill, followed by genuine (if seemingly cold) sire investment in the childe's survival skills rather than emotional nurturing.
**Key terms/entities:** High/Low Clans regional variance (p.40-41), Tremere's 1133 Amaranth-founding (p.40), Alamut/Assamite three-sect structure (p.42-43), Troile's diablerie of Brujah (p.44), Cappadocius and Erciyes (p.46-47), Gangrel's anti-hierarchy culture (p.49).

## Chapter Two, continued: Bloodlines (pp. 68-109)
**Summary:** A framing section (p.68-69) explains that bloodlines come from three narrative categories — Evolutions (branched off a parent Clan, e.g. nomadic/non-conformist Clans like Gangrel produce more bloodlines than settled ones like Lasombra), Unknown Origins (ambiguous connection to any Clan, e.g. the Niktuku's unclear relationship to the Nosferatu), and Alternate Origins (bloodlines with entirely separate creation myths that implicitly challenge the Book of Nod's authority). Also distinguishes Cainites from the Laibon (African vampires with their own non-Caine mythologies, of which the Followers of Set's own creation myth is one variant recognized by other Laibon) and briefly flags an unnamed, not-yet-encountered "Asian breed" of vampire as possibly a wholly different species.

**Ahrimanes** (pp.70-71) — "Valkyrjer": a female-only bloodline founded by Ádísa, a Valkyrie daughter of Freyja who was killed by the Persian king Ahriman and returned to take his name and lead a growing sisterhood in vengeance against wrongs to women. Subsumed into Clan Gangrel for a period, then "reawakened" through an unrelated vampire named Muricia's blood-altering ritual, which unknowingly reignited Ádísa's dormant independence trait. Unbondable (cannot be blood-bound or create blood bonds); concentrated along Scandinavian/British coastlines.

**Anda** (pp.72-73) — "Tartars": Mongol-associated bloodline founded by Dobrul the Brave, believed a childe of the Gangrel Antediluvian Ennoia; serve as the Mongol Horde's nocturnal cavalry counterpart, riding at night to protect sleeping troops while mortal soldiers advance by day, framed within Mongol religious cosmology (Tengri/Etügen) rather than Caine mythology. Suffer daylight-adjacent dice penalties near dawn/dusk from separation anxiety tied to the sun-god.

**Baali** (pp.74-75) — "Devils": infernalist bloodline of contested origin (Tzimisce, Saulot, and other theories are all circulated and denied); origin story holds that three survivors of a destroyed sacrificial cult took a demon's name and became the first Baali. Politically weakened by two prior holy wars against the Salubri and one against the Assamites; maintain covert covens (including the Lasombra-linked Angellis Ater, described here as literally created via a Baali "Rite of Apostasy" corruption ritual) and are, ironically, most often opposed by rival mortal infernalists rather than orthodox Cainites.

**Bonsam** (pp.76-77) — "Unseen"/"Stalkers": West African (Akan/Ivory Coast-origin) bloodline created when a primordial pre-cosmic darkness possessed and killed a mortal hunter, who rose transformed; intensely solitary and territorial, expanding from equatorial forest into coastal regions and coming into new conflict with other Laibon and Cainites. Communicate via a bat-like shriek/whistle language; mortals who see a Bonsap's true form must resist supernatural terror.

**Children of Osiris** (pp.78-79) — "Penitents": a cross-Clan redemption-seeking sect (not a bloodline by blood, but by shared ritual) founded on a reworked Osiris/Set/Isis myth in which Osiris, after being killed and dismembered by his brother Set, was restored by Isis's magic but rendered unable to sire further childer — Followers of Set tell a starkly different, unflattering version of the same myth. Any vampire of any Clan may seek initiation as a Golconda-focused "Penitent," but the initiation ritual permanently renders the initiate's blood inert (unable to Embrace ever again). Organized into communal temples led by "Undying Kings" (a title held regardless of the office-holder's actual gender), with poor inter-temple communication.

**Danava** (pp.80-81) — "Children of Danu": ancient India-based bloodline with two rival internal origin claims — literal divine descent from the goddess Danu, or being asuras (demons) bound into flesh — practicing "Sadhana" (grueling yogic/tantric ascetic rites) to unlock a form of Thaumaturgy predating the Tremere's. Rigidly adherent to the Hindu caste system and a religious ritual calendar; can gain no sustenance at all from animal blood (a stricter version of the Ventrue-style feeding restriction) and require ritual offering of victims to their gods before feeding.

**Gargoyles** (pp.82-83): explicit founding date 1121 CE, created by the Tremere from captured Tzimisce/Nosferatu/Gangrel vitae as near-mindless shock troops against Tzimisce war-ghouls (vozhd/szlachta). By 1242, growing numbers have broken free of Tremere control (one chantry destroyed from within by a rebellion), organizing into free "Grotesqueries" — though escaped Gargoyles increasingly can breed true as an independent bloodline rather than requiring further Tremere creation (only about a third of Gargoyles can sire childer, and half of those childer die in transformation). A specific, severe weakness: total social isolation (no master, mate, childe, or friend) halves all dice pools until remedied.

**Giovanni** ("Young Ones"/"Giovanotti," pp.84+): opens with founder Augustus's origin — surviving a horrific millennial famine (implying cannibalism) that hardened him and drew Cappadocius's attention, leading to his Embrace and eventual founding of a tight-knit "brotherhood" of like-minded mortal friends turned into a secretive Necromancer family; deliberately let outside rumor and myth about their power grow unchecked as a strategic advantage, since a mysterious reputation deterred rivals better than the plain truth would have. (Note: this account is notably gentler than modern V20's "Bite" narrative of systematic Cappadocian extermination — consistent with the Cappadocians still being an active Clan at this point in the timeline.)

**Impundulu/Bomkazi** (pp.86-87): a symbiotic African pair-bloodline — Impundulu (an accidentally-empowered necromancer, killed and revived by an overpowered spirit summoning) and Bomkazi (a lineage of living human life-witches descended from his mortal wife's magical bargain to bear children by proxy); Impundulu feed exclusively on Bomkazi blood, treating them as equal allies rather than servants; the line faces an existential crisis from inbreeding/blood dilution as Bomkazi numbers dwindle.

**Kiasyd** (pp.88-89): a strikingly different origin from the modern V20 version — Kiasyd are created when a vampire's attempted Embrace of a fae-touched mortal doesn't kill the subject outright but instead burns away fae memory/power and transforms them into an isolated, physically fae-marked outcast; a vampire named Marconius claims (possibly falsely) to have been a normal Cainite before self-transforming via "eldritch sorcery" into the first Kiasyd. Vulnerable to cold iron (aggravated damage, triggers frenzy/Rötschreck checks).

**Lamia** (pp.90-91): founded when Lazarus (a wandering childe of Cappadocius) Embraced the sorceress-cultist Lamia, a self-proclaimed descendant of Lilith — after the Embrace, Lamia reportedly whispered something to Lazarus that made him flee in permanent terror, and he has avoided the bloodline ever since. Explicitly coded (via period-appropriate framing) as a haven for what a modern reader would recognize as queer, gender-nonconforming, or otherwise patriarchy-marginalized individuals — the Clan Embraces those who venerate the feminine divine as equal or superior to the masculine. Their bite carries the "Seed of Lilith," a wasting plague-curse.

**Lhiannan** (pp.92-93): a Gangrel-splinter bloodline the elder Gangrel despise and fear in equal measure, founded by an unnamed "mother" who abandoned the Clan for the west; suffers a bizarre generational-blood anomaly (all Lhiannan become Tenth Generation upon Embracing a childe, making sire and childe blood-siblings rather than parent/child in potency) that the eldest members can't explain; also weakens progressively the longer a Lhiannan stays away from wilderness.

**Nagaraja** (pp.94-96): "Flesh-Eaters" — origins traced to Himalayan will-workers who merged with Underworld energies roughly 2000 years prior; dominant necromancers east of the Indus, organized into a formal Shravaka ("listener")/Acarya ("exemplar") disciple-mentor structure; must eat fresh raw meat (human or corpse) to sustain strength, an explicit and severe weakness compounding their monstrous, un-retractable teeth.

**"The Hungry"** (pp.96-98): a mysterious, feared Nosferatu-adjacent bloodline avenging an ancient, largely-forgotten "crime" committed by the Nosferatu founder; eerily beautiful rather than ugly (inverting the Nosferatu curse), losing Appearance dots that migrate into Physical Attributes as they age into "twisted, deadly grotesques." Their unclear organization and vengeful, apocalyptic rhetoric ("We will reap... We will feast") strongly parallel the modern V20's Nictuku concept.

**Ramanga** (pp.98-100): Madagascar-originating bloodline founded by Ramanga, a princess who was passed over for succession by her patriarchal culture and made escalating dark-spirit bargains until one answered, granting her power; spread her bloodline through controlled trade networks to Ghana, Kanem, Constantinople, and the Middle East; Ramanga typically pose as humble royal servants while secretly controlling rulers via blood-drawn "curse removal" rituals — an inversion of typical vampire/prey power dynamics.

**Salubri, three castes** (pp.100-107): Still a recognized Clan at this point but riven by the recent (1133) diablerie of their founder Saulot by the Tremere, which shattered clan unity — the three castes now largely operate independently. **Healers** ("Demon-hunters/Arbiters/Soulstealers") protect mortal herds and maintain Cainite/kine boundaries, embedded with monasteries, Hospitallers, and Cathar heretical cults; largest stronghold at Montségur (a real historical Cathar site, foreshadowing its 1244 siege two years after this book's setting). **Warriors** ("Wanderers/Kin-slayers/Souleaters") root out Baali and Setite infernalists and "degenerate" vampires, closely allied with the Knights Templar (rumored founders of the "Order of the Bitter Ashes"), some sliding onto the corrupted "Path of the Devil" out of rage at the Clan's decline. **Watchers** ("Scholars/Thieves/Breathstealers") founded by the trickster Zao-lat, who stole enlightenment from "Ten Thousand Demons" in "the Middle Kingdom"; operate in East Asia as the "Wu Zao" (feared/mocked by the Wan Kuei, i.e. Kindred of the East) and in the West via sire-childe pairs infiltrating other Clans undetected.

**True Brujah** (pp.106-107): "Scholars/Sages" — claim the mainline Brujah stole their Clan identity via the same diablerie event described in the main Brujah entry, but from the diablerized side's perspective: the "thieves" (modern Brujah) simply out-Embraced and outnumbered the dispassionate, logical loyalists. Command Temporis rather than Celerity; increasingly emotionally flattened over time (a mechanical penalty to Conscience/Conviction rolls that worsens with age).



**Toreador** (pp.60-61): "Aesthetes"/"Vanitas" — this era's Toreador are explicitly tied to the medieval flowering of chivalric/religious art and mysticism; some channel their aesthetic passion into extreme religious practice (self-flagellation, stigmata) precisely because sinning against a forgiving God feels like another aesthetic indulgence. Central tragic irony reinforced: Toreador can never actually create or fully feel the beauty they crave, only chase its memory, which drives obsessive collecting of art, retainers, and lovers. Weakness (aesthetic fixation/distraction) explicitly mechanized here as a Self-Control/Instinct roll against being captivated by beauty.

**Tremere** (pp.62-63): Explicit founding date given as 1022 CE — a group of Hermetic mage-scholars led by Tremere, facing the decline of mortal magic, discovered the vitae elixir and became "the first truly new clan," immediately provoking war with the Tzimisce over Transylvanian territory and rumors (from rivals) that their sudden power came from diablerizing the Salubri Antediluvian Saulot. Structurally rigid Council of Seven leadership through Council Regent/Domain Regent/Chantry Regent/Chantry Apprentice ranks; newly Embraced Tremere are required to surrender a blood sample to a hidden Carpathian vault as an enforcement mechanism/threat. Politically dependent on an alliance of convenience with the Ventrue (trading magical services for protection) due to having few allies and many enemies from their aggressive expansion.

**Tzimisce** (pp.64-65): "Fiends" — depicted at the height of their traditional Carpathian dominance but under active, escalating multi-front assault: Tremere/Gargoyle incursion from the west, Ventrue crusades pushing east into Transylvania, and Mongol invasion providing incidental relief by distracting common enemies. A brewing internal generational conflict pits traditionalist elders against reform-minded younger Tzimisce (some embracing radical bodily transformation via Vicissitude as "liberation" rather than warfare necessity). The Clan's land-bound, incestuous, ghoul-family-based nobility structure (drawing childer almost exclusively from their own revenant bloodlines) is reinforced, alongside their famous, near-sacred tradition of hospitality to any guest, mortal or vampire, regardless of the Clan's otherwise vicious internal politics.

**Ventrue** (pp.66-67): "Power Mongers"/"Ambitiones"/derogatory "Patricians" — trace lineage to the Second Generation and Roman aristocratic tradition (the term "Dominate" is explicitly derived in-fiction from the Roman honorific "Dominus"/"Domina"). Depicted as having deliberately withdrawn from visible power during "the Long Night" (the pre-War-of-Princes calmer era) and now actively repositioning to seize overt rulership as the War of Princes escalates, hindered by their own rigid, hierarchy-obsessed infighting. Reinforces the strict, singular feeding restriction (a specific narrow "type" of mortal) as a defining, non-negotiable Clan weakness.


**Lasombra** (pp.50-51): The Antediluvian founder himself has retreated into troubled torpor at the Castle of Shadows in Sicily, guarded by his eldest childe Montano, who now rules the Clan mostly in absentia. A significant internal religious schism drives the "Shadow Reconquista" in Iberia — Christian vs. Muslim Lasombra warring against each other, mirroring the mortal Reconquista, with Christian Lasombra additionally split by the "Cainite Heresy" (a cult believing vampirism itself marks Cainites as literally angelic beings, viewed as blasphemy by orthodox Lasombra). Governing body: the Amici Noctis ("Friends of Night"), an invitation-only elite that administers the Courts of Blood, the only sanctioned venue for Lasombra to petition for the right to diablerize an "unworthy" clanmate — unauthorized Amaranth draws summary destruction. Montano personally bars the Amici Noctis from Sicily.

**Malkavian** (pp.52-53): Called "Cassandras" in this era — 1242 is depicted as a specifically dangerous time for the Clan, as the medieval Church's shift toward viewing mental illness as spiritual corruption (rather than the older organic/humoral view) increases persecution and exorcism deaths. In response, some Malkavians formed "Ordo" cults that deliberately induce visionary insight through self-harm and psychotropic-laced blood, then pool/share their fragmented prophecies to appear more collectively wise and thus valuable to Princes seeking omens about the War of Princes' outcome. Credited (via a Malkavian seer's influence) with inciting the Ventrue-led destruction of Brujah's Carthage — a claim the Brujah bitterly resent. Notably, this account frames the Malkavian/Baali connection (elsewhere hinted as dangerous secret-keeping) as the Clan having deliberately withheld knowledge that could have destroyed "all of Caine's get."

**Nosferatu** (pp.54-55): "Priors" — founder Absimiliard is given an explicit origin myth here: a vain, beautiful Second-City vampire cursed by Caine with horrific ugliness as punishment for his obsession with his own comeliness, who then turned to destroying his own childer out of self-loathing before this apparently changed (current Nosferatu clearly continue to exist and Embrace). Framed as "hunted" collectively — their monstrous appearance is explicitly tied to dangerous forbidden knowledge, as though the Clan's ugliness and their secret-hoarding are two expressions of the same curse. Zero-dot fixed Appearance (cannot be raised even with vitae) is an explicit mechanical Clan weakness.

**Ravnos** (pp.56-57): "Charlatans," "Shapers," or "Vagabonds" — nomadic by necessity (rarely welcome to haven long in others' cities) organized around the jati caste system carried over from Indian origins, though many European-based Ravnos obscure their actual ancestry. Central philosophical claim: the Embrace grants the ability to see past maya (illusion/reality) and thus manipulate it — younger, more cynical "Charlatans" reject this mystical framing and simply exploit the power for personal gain, which drives the Clan's poor reputation. Despite appearing disorganized/mutually distrustful to outsiders, the jati in fact maintain a strict mutual-aid honor code, including collective retaliation against anyone who wrongs a member.

**Followers of Set** (pp.58-59): "Serpents" — explicitly claim Set, not Caine, as the true progenitor of all vampires, framing the Caine myth as Christianizing propaganda; devoted to restoring worldwide worship of Set (whom Ptolemaic and Roman influence recast as a violence-god before Abrahamic religions erased his worship almost entirely) rather than to chaos for its own sake. Actively work to undermine Christian and Islamic religious dominance in Europe as part of this restoration project. Favor red-haired Europeans (Gauls/Franks) as a "blessing of Set" sign when recruiting outside their traditional Egyptian/Nubian/Arabian base. Organized via an old Egyptian temple hierarchy (Prophet/High Priest of Set leading subordinate Priests). Weakness specifically doubled (not just standard) sunlight vulnerability, tied to the mythological enmity between Set and the sun god Horus.



## Chapter Three: The Roads We Walk (pp. 110-143)
**Summary:** Covers Dark Medieval "Roads" — this era's term for Paths of Enlightenment/Humanity, all built on the same underlying game system (Road ratings 0-10, Ethics, Hierarchy of Sins, Initiates → Adherents → Mentors → Paragons progression, "Moments of Truth" for advancement, Apostates/Excommunicates for those who leave or are cast out). Explains that every vampire defaults to the Road of Humanity at Embrace and may later transition to another Road via a risky, extended-roll "Moment of Truth" ritual that can cost a character her Road entirely (dropping to zero dots means total loss to the Beast). Golconda is addressed as in the core book — a rumored, unverifiable transcendent state that each Road interprets completely differently, making public pursuit of it dangerous since one Road's Golconda can be another's heresy.

Details five major Roads, each with Ethics, a Hierarchy of Sins table, Initiation practices, Organization, Rituals, and Aura:
- **Road of the Beast** ("Apostates") — rejects human society's authority entirely, treating civilization as a weakening "cushion" and pure predatory self-interest as the only legitimate ethic; recruits are often "liberated" by having their mortal-adjacent lives deliberately sabotaged by existing Apostates, which the Road frames as a kindness rather than cruelty. Three sub-Paths: **Path of the Hunter** (the hunt itself as singular life focus and near-sacred ritual), **Path of Journeys** (compulsive wandering/exploration, rejection of any settled attachment), and **Path of Liberation** (revolutionary anti-civilization violence — overthrowing Princes, mass-freeing childer, deliberately breaking the Silence of the Blood to destroy "the city built on falsehoods").
- **Road of Heaven** ("the Faithful") — the most fragmented Road, since adherents mostly retain their pre-Embrace mortal religion (Christian, Muslim, Jewish, Druidic, Roman pagan, etc.) and interpret the Embrace through that faith's specific lens (Christians as a Job-like test of character; Muslims as a call to fight Shaitan's servants; Jews as a scholarly opportunity; Druids serving cosmic Balance). A sidebar ("That Old-Time Religion") explicitly acknowledges the logistical contradiction between literal scriptural dietary/prayer law and vampiric existence, noting most Faithful follow the "spirit" rather than letter of their faith, risking mortal-heresy accusations. A full sample Hierarchy of Sins is given for the Islamic-specific "Path of the Prophet."
- **Road of Humanity** ("Prodigals") — closely mirrors the core book's default Humanity system; framed as a lonely, teacher-to-student personal journey rather than an organized creed, since most other Roads view clinging to human morality as weakness.
- **Road of Kings** ("Luminaries") — traces mythic origin to Caine's own boast that it is "better to rule in darkness than to humble [himself] falsely in the light"; built around a rigid master/servant hierarchical worldview, oath-swearing ceremonies, and the belief that self-mastery over one's own Beast is the prerequisite for mastering others. Includes the **Path of Chivalry** ("Paladins") sub-variant — genuinely self-sacrificing, protective leadership, using love rather than fear to command loyalty.
- **Road of Bones** ("Necronomists," a "Minor Road") — the Cappadocian-dominated scholarly-necromantic path (also drawing some Tzimisce, Malkavians, and morbid Toreador), focused on studying death/undeath/the soul without being ruled by passion; branches into the **Path of the Scholar** (historical/archival focus) and **Path of Death and the Soul** (necromantic/Underworld focus).
- **Road of Yasa** ("Minor Road," briefly noted) — the Anda bloodline's Mongol-derived honor/loyalty code, adapted from Genghis Khan's actual historical Yasa legal code.

A sidebar on pagan traditions (p.140) explicitly corrects Christian-historian misconceptions about paganism, noting practices (human sacrifice, ancestor worship, reincarnation belief) varied significantly by specific pagan culture (Norse, Lithuanian/Latvian, Druidic) rather than being monolithic.
**Key themes:** morality as culturally/religiously constructed and non-monolithic even within a single Road, initiation-as-risk (changing Roads can destroy the character), leadership and hierarchy as recurring philosophical throughline (Kings) contrasted with anti-hierarchy (Beast).
**Key terms/entities:** Road ratings/Moments of Truth mechanic (p.111-115), Golconda (p.115), Road of the Beast/Heaven/Humanity/Kings/Bones/Yasa (p.115-141).

## Chapter Nine: The Dark Medieval World (pp. 406-419)
**Summary:** A geopolitical gazetteer blending real 13th-century history with Cainite politics, opening with a cynical, unnamed elder's fiery monologue dismissing Gehenna prophecy-mongering while cataloguing genuinely destabilizing real events (the sack of Constantinople, Kiev's destruction, the Lasombra's Iberian civil war, mortal witches diablerizing an Antediluvian, the Magna Carta) as signs the old order is ending regardless of divine plan.

**Italy**: Covers the city-state system amid the Guelph/Ghibelline (Pope vs. Emperor Frederick II) conflict — Florence's wool-trade boom, Milan's Toreador fashion scene and Ventrue internal Guelph/Ghibelline split, Bologna's university and Nosferatu affinity for wandering unaffiliated clerici vagantes, Venice's neutral-ground status as a Cainite truce venue (Assamite/Lasombra/Ventrue), Rome's near-total depopulation and lawless power vacuum, and the Kingdom of Sicily under Frederick II — an emperor whose real historical reputation for unsettling scientific experiments is given a sidebar suggesting Tremere or Cappadocian influence. Notes the coming (1243-44) Siege of Montségur and mass burning of 200 Cathar Perfects, with several Cainites (Road of Heaven followers, Cappadocians, Ravnos, and rumored Salubri healers) hiding among the Cathars.

**Eastern Europe / "The Other Europe"**: Extensive coverage of the real 1241-42 Mongol invasion of Hungary and its supernatural consequences — the "War of Omens" between Tremere (allied with Ventrue) and Tzimisce, fought both on the ground and via aerial gargoyle/chiropteran battles above the Carpathians; the near-total destruction of Hungarian Cainite power structures; the Anda's role as a "shadow horde" — a nocturnal supernatural mirror force protecting the Mongol army from vampiric threats the daytime mortal chroniclers never recorded. Transylvania is anchored to the historically-attested demon Kupala (tying back to the Tzimisce origin myth from Lore of the Clans) and named Tzimisce figures include the Voivode of voivodes Vladimir Rustovitch and the revenant boyar Basarab, founder of Wallachia and ancestor-figure to a coming voivode named Dracula. Also covers Bulgaria, Serbia, Bosnia, Bohemia (Ventrue-dominated, pivotal to the entire War of Princes' outcome), Poland, Lithuania (the last major pagan holdout in Europe, protected by an ancient supernatural accord granting equal status to werewolves, fae, and witches, enforced by a pagan "Pope" called Krivé; rumored resting place of Baba Yaga), Livonia/Prussia (Teutonic Knights' Northern Crusade against pagans), the Rus principalities (real 1237-40 Mongol conquest of Kiev/Vladimir, the Baba Yaga "mother of Nosferatu" residing in Rus), Georgia (a Brujah-fueled independence movement seeded within Mongol vassalage), the Steppe peoples (Cumans/Kipchaks), and a closing section on the Mongols themselves — crediting General Subatai's strategic vision and framing the Golden Horde's meritocratic pragmatism (absorbing skilled/useful conquered people regardless of origin) as key to their success, alongside the "new Silk Road" connecting Karakorum-Sarai-Trebizond that Marco Polo would later travel (and possibly how the Black Plague later entered Europe).
**Key themes:** real medieval history integrated seamlessly with vampire politics, the Mongol invasion as a genuine world-historical rupture disrupting both mortal and Cainite power structures equally, regional diversity resisting a single "European" narrative.
**Key terms/entities:** War of Omens (Tremere/Ventrue vs. Tzimisce, p.412-413), Baba Yaga "mother of Nosferatu" (p.414, 416), Kupala (p.412), Vladimir Rustovitch (p.413), Basarab/Wallachia/Dracula lineage (p.413), the Anda "shadow horde" (p.417), Montségur (p.407).

## Appendix B: Apocrypha of the Clans (pp. 432-457)
**Summary:** A collection of Clan-specific expanded lore, told through in-character letters, myths, and fiction vignettes, followed by Kickstarter backer credits (pp.458-470) and a game index/glossary (pp.471-489) — not additional lore content.

**Assamites**: Expands the three-sect structure with specific Discipline/weakness detail — Sorcerers (Assamite Sorcery/Thaumaturgy, Auspex, Celerity; magically detectable) research ways to sustain the Clan without mortal blood, having created the "blood of Alamut" (a preserved blend of vitae from every Assamite ever created). Warriors (Celerity, Obfuscate, Quietus; compelled toward diablerie as a confirmed tradition) act as nomadic judges/executioners who set Clan-wide moral priorities.

**Brujah**: A cache of letters between "Zamra" and correspondents (Brother Cornelius, Safirah) presents multiple competing/contradictory accounts of Troile's founding — one romanticized version has the philosopher (later revealed as Troile) devotedly serving a master until finally Embraced as an equal, undermining the standard "diablerie/betrayal" narrative; another set of letters from doomed Carthage shows the "last prince" Himilcar the Pale as a coward whose defiant last words were pure propaganda, and reveals internal Carthaginian politics around Tanitbaal-Sahar's forbidden infernal-adjacent sacrifices made "for the good of Carthage" against Rome's ally Aeneas.

**Cappadocians**: A theological letter exchange between Ahmad al-Nasrani and Eudocia Melachrina debates whether vampires can achieve godlike transcendence by directly "feeding" on divine/spiritual entities (ghosts/daimones) rather than mere blood — distinguishing benevolent "wraiths" from malevolent "spectres." Introduces the **Cult of Lamia** in more depth: as the Church suppresses women's religious authority, Lilith-veneration and the Lamia bloodline are correspondingly written out of Cainite history and recast (falsely, per this text) as mere Cappadocian bodyguards. A sidebar speculates the Lamia may be deliberately abandoning the Cappadocians (foreseeing the coming Giovanni betrayal via Lamia's own prophetic gifts) rather than being destroyed alongside them.

**Gangrel**: "The Last Age of Adventure" — a first-person Gangrel hunting narrative in which the elder frames expanding human civilization as squeezing out "wild" mysteries, hunts down what may be one of the last of a now-extinct monstrous creature (an oversized, twisted "deer-thing"), and reflects with genuine grief on the vanishing of unexplained wonders from the world even as she profits from the kill.

**Giovanni**: Profiles several "Rising stars" (Amina la Giovane da Palermo — a brilliant scholar-physician running a silk/medicine business; El Zovane Yesugei — a Mongol-born Giovanni spy embedded in Venice via the historical Jebe/Subutai-Venice intelligence arrangement; Yannis Lu Grico — a death-diviner secretly serving Emperor Frederick II; Pietro/Petrus Iovialis Pisanus — a Pisan merchant with a fabricated ancient Roman lineage). A explicit sidebar ("Giovani Through the Ages") tells Storytellers outright that 1242 Giovanni are structurally different from the modern, fully independent Clan seen in core V20, framing Augustus's plan to eventually overthrow Cappadocius as an open-ended future the table can decide for itself — directly acknowledging the historical discontinuity between this book and the modern-day metaplot.

**Lasombra**: A found-letter horror story (Ysabel de Palma to Bishop Domingo Caro) depicts a Lasombra pilgrim encountering an "Abyss Mystic" who invokes the name Ahriman and demonstrates the Discipline Obtenebration's darker applications, followed by a chilling reply confirming that Lasombra himself (the Antediluvian) actively protects the Abyss Mystics from Church interference. A separate manifesto from "Ashiqa bint Jifri" of the Angellis Ater ("Black Angels") frames the Clan's power as proof of Shaitan/Lucifer's chosen favor, rejecting all mortal religious morality as weakness. Full **Road of the Abyss** Hierarchy of Sins provided.

**Malkavians**: "The Mysterious Spiral" — a dying sire's letter to childe Agnes maps prophetic significance onto the Fibonacci sequence (explicitly noting the historical Leonardo of Pisa/Fibonacci, "an Italian boy from Bugia," was contemporaneously writing about these numbers), predicting Gehenna-adjacent events at each successive number in the sequence. Introduces two rival Malkavian "Ordos" (mystic orders): the **Ordo Aenigmatis** ("Order of Enigmas," symbol/numerology-focused divination) and the **Ordo Ecstasis** ("Order of Ecstatics," pain/hedonism-focused divination), each with mechanical rituals (Rite of Enigmas, Rite of Ecstasy) trading temporary derangement suppression for divinatory insight; a forbidden, unexplained "Rite of Malfeasance" is mentioned but shunned by both orders.

**Ravnos**: Reinforces the jati caste system (warrior/priest/merchant castes in Indian tradition, blood-relation-based in European diaspora) and the universal hospitality-to-fellow-Ravnos code. Provides the full **Road of Paradox** (called "Asura" here rather than "Shilmulo") with Hierarchy of Sins, largely consistent with the core V20/Lore of Clans version.

**Salubri**: A detailed "future history" speculative sidebar imagining an alternate timeline where the three castes reunify before the historical 1291 Fall of Acre, potentially avoiding the Clan's near-total destruction — explicitly framed as one possible direction, not canon fact, for Storytellers running long chronicles bridging Dark Ages to modern V20. Introduces **Samiel's Code/Path of Vindication** in depth: Samiel (founder of the Warrior caste) dedicated his unlife to fighting the Baali and was ultimately diablerized by the Baali demon-associate Nergal at a place called Chorazin; his teachings later fused with Crusader-era militant Christianity. Also references Adonai as a "Black Hand Seraph" claiming the Warriors are "Uncursed by Caine" — consistent with the modern V20 Salubri antitribu material.

**Setites**: A full retelling of the Set/Osiris/Apep myth (Osiris tricks Set into vulnerability against the chaos-serpent Apep out of sibling rivalry; Set survives but is forever marked/cursed by the encounter) as this book's specific creation myth. Introduces internal Setite divisions beyond Priests: the **Warriors of Glycon** ("Crooks" — violence-focused, power-obsessed, referencing the real Alexander-the-Great-related "sacred serpent" myth) and the **Witches of Echidna** ("Gorgons" — an older, pre-Set matriarchal serpent-goddess cult predating and underlying the Ptolemaic Set mythology entirely, tracing the same mother-serpent archetype across Hydra/Draco/Jörmungandr mythological traditions worldwide). Full **Path of Apep** (heretical chaos-worship, opposed to mainline Set-devotion) provided with Hierarchy of Sins.

**Toreador**: "Courtly Romance" — extensive real-history integration covering chivalric love conventions, the Roman de la Rose (attributed here to a fictional Toreador author "Guillaume de Torris," later hiding under the real historical pen name "Jean de Meun"), the Toreador social institution of the locus amoenus ("pleasant place," private artistic/romantic gatherings) and its "Parliament of Birds" ritual for adjudicating disputed unauthorized Embraces. Also weaves the real medieval poet Marie de France into Toreador social history as a mortal who frequented their gatherings without being Embraced, reframing her poem "Laüstic" as a real Toreador tragedy and her poem "Bisclavret" as being about an actual Gangrel werewolf-adjacent incident.

**Tzimisce**: Introduces the **Dracul** ("Dragons"/"Old Clan") — Tzimisce who predate Vicissitude and view fleshcrafting as a debasement of the Clan's "true" destiny as territorial sovereigns — and the **Koldun** (land-bound blood sorcerers who commune directly with the buried demon Kupala, respected across factional lines). A lengthy, anxious in-character philosophical fragment meditates on whether Kupala's buried influence has fundamentally corrupted the entire Clan's aesthetic and psychological character (explaining the Tzimisce's obsession with monstrous rather than beautiful transformation) — ending on the unresolved, dread-laden suggestion that the Tzimisce are simultaneously "the bait and the rat" trapped by their own founding demonic pact. Details several named revenant families with specific Disciplines/weaknesses: Basarab (Dracula's ancestral line), Bratovich (kennel-masters, Brujah-style frenzy), Danislav (secretly lupine-kin, hiding stolen werewolf Gifts from their masters), Grimaldi (spies against the Giovanni), Obertus (scholarly, fostered by a Methuselah named Dracon, scattered after Constantinople's fall), Szantovich (addictive-personality spy-assassins), Vlaszy (horse-cavalry, blood-bound at maturity), and Khavi (serve an entity called Byelobog, vulnerable to sunlight).

**Ventrue**: "The Round Table" — an extended, tragic Arthurian-legend retelling in which Morgane le Fey is revealed as a Lasombra-Ventrue-adjacent(?) vampire who deliberately Embraces Arthur via a corrupted "Grail," and Guinevere's affair with Lancelot, Arthur's escalating cruelty/frenzy, and her near-execution unfold as vampire political tragedy layered onto the familiar myth — ending on a cliffhanger with Lancelot's rescue of Guinevere from the pyre.
**Key themes:** Clan-specific mythologies given deliberately unreliable, multi-source, or explicitly reinterpreted treatment (consistent with the modern V20 Lore books' approach), integration of real historical/literary figures (Fibonacci, Marie de France, Frederick II, Roman de la Rose) into Cainite secret history, explicit designer transparency about historical discontinuity between 1242 and the modern V20 timeline (the Giovanni sidebar).
**Key terms/entities:** Blood of Alamut (p.432), Cult of Lamia (p.436-437), Ordo Aenigmatis/Ordo Ecstasis (p.443-444), Road of the Abyss/Angellis Ater (p.442), Warriors of Glycon/Witches of Echidna (p.449-450), Dracul/Koldun/Kupala (p.453-455), Morgane le Fey's corruption of Arthur (p.455-457).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **Unreliable, multi-voiced history as structural principle**: Continues the modern V20 Lore books' approach even more intensely — nearly every Clan's origin myth is presented via letters, oral tradition, or dueling accounts that explicitly contradict each other (Brujah's founding, Salubri's castes, Setite theology, the Cappadocian/Giovanni relationship).
- **The War of Princes / War of Omens as background drumbeat**: Nearly every chapter references the era's central political instability — elder infighting escalating toward open war — as the shared backdrop against which individual Clan and character stories unfold.
- **Real history as vampire history's scaffolding**: More thoroughly than the modern V20 books, Dark Ages integrates real documented historical events (the Mongol invasion, the Cathar Crusade, the Guelph/Ghibelline conflict, Frederick II's court, Roman de la Rose) directly into Cainite secret history, often naming real historical figures as unwitting participants or victims of vampire politics.
- **Bloodlines/Clans as historically contingent, not fixed**: The book repeatedly signals (the Giovanni sidebar, the Salubri "what-if" reunification scenario, the still-active Cappadocians and Salubri) that the modern V20 setting's status quo is one possible outcome among several, encouraging Storytellers to treat 1242 as a genuine branch point rather than foreordained backstory.
- **The Beast, the Hunt, and predation as the true universal experience**: Established forcefully in the Introduction and echoed throughout — beneath all the political and philosophical superstructure, every vampire shares the same Hunger-Hunt-Feed-Denouement cycle.
- **Regional/cultural specificity over generic "medieval Europe"**: Extensive, deliberately non-monolithic treatment of Italy's city-states, Eastern Europe's ethnic patchwork, Mongol culture and religion, and explicit Storyteller guidance encouraging non-Western-European settings.
